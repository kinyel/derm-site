## How it works

Haemoglobin, the red pigment in blood, absorbs certain wavelengths of light. The laser heats the blood inside a small vessel until the vessel wall seals. The body then absorbs the closed vessel over the following weeks.

## What it treats

- **Broken capillaries** (telangiectasia) on the nose and cheeks
- **[Spider veins](/conditions/spider-veins/)** that are very fine
- **Redness and visible vessels of [rosacea](/conditions/rosacea/)**
- **Vascular birthmarks,** such as port-wine stains, and some [haemangiomas](/conditions/birthmarks-and-haemangiomas/)
- **Cherry angiomas** and small vascular spots
- **Red, raised scars,** to reduce redness

## On brown and Black skin

Redness is harder to see on darker skin, so vascular conditions are often diagnosed late, and they are treated less often with laser than in light skin. Melanin sits between the laser and the vessel and absorbs some of the light. The 1064-nanometre long-pulsed Nd:YAG laser, strong cooling and conservative energy make treatment possible, but the risk of pale or dark marks is higher than in light skin. For leg veins, sclerotherapy avoids that problem and is usually advised first.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** The dermatologist confirms what the vessels are and checks for causes that need treatment in their own right. Avoid sun for four weeks, and tell the doctor about blood thinners.

**During.** Eye shields are worn. Each pulse feels like a hot snap, with cooling. Small areas take minutes.

**After.** Redness and swelling for a day or two; treated vessels may look darker before fading. Occasionally a bruise forms and lasts up to two weeks. Avoid heat, alcohol and vigorous exercise for 48 hours, and use sunscreen.

## Risks

Bruising, swelling, blistering, crusting, temporary or lasting light or dark marks, and recurrence of vessels. Scarring is uncommon.

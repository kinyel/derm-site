## How resurfacing lasers work

Resurfacing lasers are absorbed by water in the skin. **Ablative** lasers, the CO2 laser and the [Erbium YAG laser](/services/laser-treatments/erbium-yag-laser/), vaporise tissue. **Fractional** delivery splits the beam into hundreds of microscopic columns, leaving untouched skin between them. Those bridges of healthy skin allow fast healing and make the treatment far safer than older full-surface resurfacing.

CO2 laser carries more heat into the skin, which tightens collagen but also inflames more. Erbium laser removes tissue with less heat, so recovery is quicker and pigment problems are less likely.

## What it treats

- [Acne scars](/conditions/acne-scars/): rolling and boxcar scars
- Scars from surgery, injury or burns, once mature
- [Stretch marks (striae)](/conditions/stretch-marks/)
- Rough texture, [enlarged pores](/conditions/enlarged-pores/) and fine lines
- Some raised scars, as part of a combined plan

Active acne, infection and [keloid scars](/conditions/keloids/) in the treatment area are reasons to wait or choose another method.

## Resurfacing on brown and Black skin

Heat is what triggers pigment cells. The more heat a laser deposits, the higher the chance of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, which is common after CO2 laser on dark skin and can last months. The plan for darker skin therefore uses:

- sunscreen and a pigment-calming cream for at least four weeks beforehand
- lower density (fewer columns per pass) and fewer passes
- erbium in preference to CO2 where it will do the job
- six to eight weeks between treatments
- pigment cream restarted as soon as the skin has healed

A test area is treated first. Non-laser options, [microneedling](/services/aesthetic-treatments/microneedling/) and [chemical peels](/services/aesthetic-treatments/chemical-peels/), are discussed alongside.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** Tell the dermatologist about cold sores, keloids, acne tablets in the past six months, and any bleaching cream use. Antiviral tablets are given if you are prone to cold sores.

**During.** Numbing cream for 45 to 60 minutes. Eye shields are worn. Each pass feels hot and prickly. A face takes 20 to 30 minutes.

**After.** Cool compresses and ointment for the first days. Wash gently, do not pick, sleep with your head raised if the face is swollen. Use sunscreen once the skin has peeled, and avoid sun, heat and swimming for at least two weeks.

## Risks and how they are managed

- **Redness, swelling, peeling:** expected.
- **Dark marks:** the main risk on dark skin; treated with sun protection and pigment creams.
- **Pale patches:** uncommon with fractional treatment.
- **Infection, including cold sores:** preventive medicine and clean aftercare.
- **Acne flare or small white bumps (milia):** settle or are treated.
- **Scarring:** rare; more likely with picking or infection.

> [!DERM] Contact the clinic if
> - pain, swelling or weeping increases after day three
> - you see blisters, yellow crusts or cold sores
> - the treated area darkens over the following weeks

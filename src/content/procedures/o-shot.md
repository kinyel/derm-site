## What the O-shot is

"O-shot" is a trade name for a PRP injection given in the genital area. PRP stands for platelet-rich plasma. A small amount of blood is taken from your arm and spun in a centrifuge, which separates out the plasma layer that is rich in platelets. Platelets carry growth factors that take part in healing. The idea behind the O-shot is that injecting them prompts local tissue repair and better blood flow.

PRP is also used for [hair loss](/services/hair-and-scalp/prp-for-hair-loss/) and with [microneedling](/services/aesthetic-treatments/prp-microneedling/) on the face, where the evidence is further along. Our article [Exosomes, PRP or PDRN](/blog/exosomes-prp-or-pdrn/) explains what each of these treatments is.

## What is known and what is not

**What studies have looked at.** Small studies and case series have followed women who had PRP injections for low sexual arousal, difficulty reaching orgasm, vaginal dryness after menopause, mild stress urinary incontinence and the skin condition [lichen sclerosus](/conditions/lichen-sclerosus/).

**What they show.** Many participants reported improvement on questionnaires. Reported side effects were mostly mild and short-lived.

**The limits.** Most studies had few participants, no placebo or comparison group, and short follow-up. Sexual function responds strongly to expectation, so without a comparison group it is impossible to say how much of the reported benefit is due to the injection. Professional bodies in gynaecology do not currently recommend PRP as an established treatment for these problems.

For those reasons the evidence is best described as emerging. We make no claim that the O-shot will improve arousal, orgasm, lubrication or bladder control.

## Start with a medical assessment

Difficulties with arousal, pain during sex, dryness and leaking urine are common and have many causes: hormonal change after childbirth or at menopause, medicines such as some antidepressants and blood pressure tablets, diabetes, thyroid disease, pelvic floor weakness, skin conditions, infection, stress and relationship strain. Several of these have treatments with good evidence, including pelvic floor physiotherapy and vaginal oestrogen where suitable.

A doctor should look for these first. The clinic's [internal medicine service](/services/internal-medicine/) can assess general health, and a referral to a gynaecologist or pelvic floor physiotherapist is made where that is the better route.

## Why a doctor must do it

Blood must be drawn, processed and re-injected under sterile conditions to avoid infection. The injection sites lie close to the urethra and to sensitive nerves. A doctor can also recognise when symptoms need investigation and not an injection.

## Who is not suitable

- Pregnancy or breastfeeding
- Genital, vaginal or urinary infection, or any unexplained sore, lump or bleeding
- Blood disorders, low platelets or anaemia that has not been treated
- Blood-thinning medicine
- Current cancer treatment
- Under 18 years of age

## Before, during and after

**Before.** A private consultation, with a chaperone and the female dermatologist if you wish. Your symptoms, health and medicines are reviewed, and the evidence above is discussed. You are given time to decide.

**During.** Numbing cream is applied. Blood is taken from your arm and prepared, which takes about 15 minutes. After a small local anaesthetic injection, the PRP is injected at two sites with a fine needle. The appointment lasts about 45 minutes.

**After.** You can return to normal activities the same day. Mild soreness, spotting or a sense of fullness can last a day or two. You are usually advised to avoid sex, tampons and swimming for 24 to 48 hours.

## Risks and how they are managed

- **Pain, bruising, spotting:** common and brief.
- **Infection:** uncommon with sterile technique; treated with antibiotics.
- **Temporary change in sensation or urinary discomfort:** reported occasionally.
- **No noticeable effect:** a real possibility that you should weigh before paying for treatment.

> [!URGENT] Urgent care
> Contact a doctor the same day for heavy bleeding, increasing pain, fever, foul-smelling discharge, or difficulty or burning when passing urine.

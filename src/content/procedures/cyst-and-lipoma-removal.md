## Telling them apart

| | Cyst | Lipoma |
|---|---|---|
| Feel | Firm, round, moves with the skin | Soft, doughy, slides under the skin |
| Surface | Often a small dark central pore | Normal skin |
| Common sites | Scalp, face, neck, back, genital skin | Shoulders, back, arms, thighs |
| Can become inflamed | Yes, red, sore and may discharge | Rarely |

Other lumps, including swollen glands, [boils](/conditions/boils-and-abscesses/) and [keloid scars](/conditions/keloids/), can look similar. The doctor examines the lump and, if there is doubt, arranges an ultrasound scan first.

## How they are removed

**Cysts.** After numbing, a small cut is made over the cyst and the sac is freed and removed whole, then the skin is stitched. A minimal-incision technique removes the contents and sac through a tiny opening, leaving a smaller scar. An infected cyst is drained and treated first.

**Lipomas.** The lump is eased out through a cut over it. Larger or deeper ones need a longer incision and sometimes referral to a surgeon.

Removed tissue is sent to the laboratory when the diagnosis is not certain.

## On brown and Black skin

The scar may be darker than the surrounding skin for months. Cysts on the chest, shoulders and upper back sit in the areas most prone to keloids, so the decision to operate there weighs the nuisance of the lump against the risk of a raised scar. Tell the doctor if you form keloids.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** Mention blood thinners, allergies and keloids. Do not squeeze the lump beforehand.

**During.** About 20 to 40 minutes under local anaesthetic.

**After.** Keep the dressing dry for 24 to 48 hours, then clean daily. Bruising and firmness under the scar settle over weeks.

## Risks

Bleeding, bruising, infection, a collection of fluid under the wound, a scar or keloid, numbness around the scar, and recurrence.

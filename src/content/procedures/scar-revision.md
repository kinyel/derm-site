## Types of scar and their treatment

| Scar | What it looks like | Usual treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Flat, wide or stretched | Pale or dark, broader than the cut | Surgical revision; sometimes laser |
| Hypertrophic | Raised, firm, within the wound's edge | Silicone, pressure, steroid injection |
| [Keloid](/conditions/keloids/) | Raised and growing beyond the wound | Steroid injection, pressure, cryotherapy; surgery only with other treatment |
| Depressed ([acne scars](/conditions/acne-scars/), chickenpox) | Dips in the skin | [Microneedling](/services/aesthetic-treatments/microneedling/), [laser resurfacing](/services/laser-treatments/laser-skin-resurfacing/), subcision |
| Contracture | Tight, limits movement, often after a burn | Surgery, sometimes with a graft or flap |
| Dark or pale scars | Colour differs from skin | Sun protection, pigment treatment, time |

## Surgical revision

Under local anaesthetic the old scar is cut out and the wound closed carefully in layers. Techniques such as Z-plasty change the direction of a scar so that it lies along natural skin lines or releases tightness. The new scar is supported with tape, silicone and sun protection.

## On brown and Black skin

Scars on darker skin are more likely to be raised and to change colour. Most dark scars lighten over a year. Because of keloid risk, the clinic favours non-surgical methods first on the chest, shoulders, back, jawline and ears, and adds preventive treatment when surgery is done.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** The dermatologist examines the scar, asks how it formed and how old it is, and sets out what can realistically be achieved.

**During.** Injections take minutes. Surgical revision takes 30 to 60 minutes under local anaesthetic.

**After.** Wound care as for any minor surgery, then silicone and sun protection for several months. Steroid injections are repeated every four to six weeks as needed.

## Risks

A scar that is no better or wider, infection, bleeding, pigment change, thinning of skin or pale patches from steroid injections, and keloid formation.

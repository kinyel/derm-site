## What high blood pressure is

Blood pressure is written as two numbers. The first, systolic, is the pressure when the heart beats. The second, diastolic, is the pressure when it rests between beats. Both are measured in millimetres of mercury (mmHg).

When pressure stays high for years, artery walls thicken and stiffen, and the heart works harder. The damage builds silently in the brain, heart, kidneys and eyes.

## How common it is in Nigeria

High blood pressure is one of the commonest long-term conditions in Nigerian adults. Pooled national studies estimate that about three in ten adults have it, and that most of them do not know. Of those who know, many are not on treatment, and of those on treatment, many are not controlled. People of African ancestry tend to develop hypertension earlier and to have more complications, particularly stroke and kidney disease, which makes early detection worthwhile.

## Why it matters

Uncontrolled hypertension is the leading risk factor for:

- **stroke**
- **heart attack and heart failure**
- **chronic kidney disease**
- **loss of vision**
- **vascular dementia**

Lowering blood pressure reduces each of these risks, whatever your age.

## How it is diagnosed

One high reading is not a diagnosis. Blood pressure varies through the day and rises with pain, stress, caffeine and rushing to an appointment.

1. **Clinic measurement** with a correctly sized cuff, after five minutes of sitting quietly, repeated in both arms.
2. **Confirmation** on a separate day, or with home readings taken morning and evening for several days.
3. **Assessment of overall risk:** blood tests for kidney function, blood sugar and cholesterol; a urine test; and often a heart tracing (ECG).

The doctor also looks for a treatable cause, especially in younger adults or when pressure is very high or hard to control.

## Hypertension treatment: how it is managed

**Lifestyle measures** lower blood pressure in almost everyone and make medicine work better:

- Cut salt. Much of it is hidden in stock cubes, seasoning, processed foods, bread and snacks. Taste food before adding salt.
- Eat more vegetables, fruit, beans and whole grains.
- Be active for at least 150 minutes a week; brisk walking counts.
- Lose weight if you are above a healthy weight.
- Limit alcohol.
- Stop smoking.
- Sleep enough, and seek help for loud snoring with daytime sleepiness.

**Medicine.** When lifestyle measures are not enough, or pressure or overall risk is high, the doctor prescribes blood pressure medicine. Several types exist, and many people need more than one. The choice depends on your age, other conditions and how you respond. Some types are known to work better than others in people of African ancestry, which is one reason treatment should be individual.

**Review.** Blood pressure is rechecked after any change until it is on target, then every few months. Kidney function is monitored with blood tests.

This page does not tell you to start, stop or change any medicine. If you have side effects, or are thinking of stopping, speak to your doctor first.

## Home blood pressure monitoring

A validated upper-arm monitor is a good investment. Sit with your back supported and feet flat, arm resting at heart level, and avoid caffeine, exercise and smoking for 30 minutes beforehand. Take two readings a minute apart and write them down. Bring the record to each review.

## Skin and blood pressure

Some blood pressure medicines can cause swollen ankles, rashes or sensitivity to sunlight, and a few skin and hair treatments can raise blood pressure. If you attend the clinic's dermatology service, tell both doctors what you take. Long-standing high pressure in the leg veins is a different problem and causes [venous leg ulcers](/conditions/venous-leg-ulcers/).

> [!DOCTOR] See a doctor soon if
> - your readings are regularly 140/90 or above, or 135/85 or above at home
> - you have high blood pressure and have not been reviewed in the past year
> - you are pregnant, or planning pregnancy, with raised blood pressure
> - you have diabetes or kidney disease and do not know your blood pressure

> [!URGENT] Urgent care
> A reading of 180/120 or higher with chest pain, breathlessness, severe headache, confusion, blurred vision, weakness or numbness on one side, or difficulty speaking is an emergency. Go to the nearest hospital emergency department immediately. Do not wait to see whether it settles.

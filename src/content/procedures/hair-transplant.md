## The consultation

Not everyone who asks for a transplant should have one. The consultation establishes:

- **The diagnosis,** with a scalp examination and [trichoscopy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/trichoscopy/). Scarring alopecia that is still active will destroy transplanted hairs.
- **Stability.** Hair loss that is still progressing fast, or loss in a very young person, is usually treated medically first.
- **Donor supply.** The number and quality of hairs at the back and sides limit what can be achieved.
- **Your aims.** A transplant redistributes hair; it cannot restore the density of adolescence.
- **Keloid history** and general health.

## How the procedure works

Under local anaesthetic, follicles are harvested in one of two ways:

- **Follicular unit excision (FUE):** individual follicle groups are removed with a tiny punch, leaving small dot scars.
- **Strip method (FUT):** a narrow strip of scalp is removed and divided into grafts, leaving a line scar hidden by hair.

The grafts are then placed into small openings made in the recipient area, following the natural direction of growth. A procedure takes most of a day.

## African-textured hair

Curved follicles are easily cut during harvesting, so technique and experience with this hair type decide graft survival. A trial of a small number of grafts may be advised first. Keloids and dark or pale marks in the donor area are possible, and people prone to keloids need individual advice.

For [traction alopecia](/conditions/traction-alopecia/), a transplant can restore edges that have not regrown after a year or more of loose styling, provided the styles that caused the loss are not resumed.

## Aftercare

- Sleep with the head raised for the first few nights.
- Spray or wash the grafts gently as instructed; do not rub or pick crusts.
- Avoid exercise, sweating, swimming and sun for about two weeks.
- No tight styles, wigs with glue, relaxers or dyes until the surgeon agrees.
- Swelling of the forehead for a few days is common.
- Continue medical hair loss treatment as advised.

## Risks

Bleeding, infection, swelling, numbness, cysts or pimples in the grafted area, poor growth of some grafts, visible donor scars, keloids, shock loss of nearby hair that usually recovers, and an unnatural hairline if poorly planned.

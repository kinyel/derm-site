## What "Q-switched" means

Inside a laser, energy builds up before it is released. A Q-switch is a shutter that holds that energy back and then lets it out all at once, as one very short, very powerful pulse. The pulse lasts a few nanoseconds, which are billionths of a second.

That brevity is the point. Pigment granules and ink particles absorb the pulse and break apart before heat has time to spread into the skin around them. The technique is called selective photothermolysis: the target is damaged and its surroundings are largely spared.

Most Q-switched lasers in dermatology are **Nd:YAG** lasers, which produce two wavelengths:

- **1064 nanometres** (infrared): deeper reach, less absorption by surface melanin, used on dark skin and for black ink.
- **532 nanometres** (green): for surface pigment and red ink, but strongly absorbed by melanin and used with great caution on dark skin.

## What it treats

- **Tattoos,** especially black and dark blue. See [tattoo removal by laser](/services/laser-treatments/laser-tattoo-removal/).
- **Sun spots and freckles.**
- **Certain birthmarks,** such as naevus of Ota, over a series of treatments.
- **Selected [dark marks](/conditions/hyperpigmentation/)** that have not responded to creams and peels.
- **[Melasma](/conditions/melasma/) and uneven tone,** as low-energy toning in carefully chosen cases.

## Q-switched laser on brown and Black skin

This laser has a long record in Asian and African skin types, which is why it remains a standard tool even as newer [pico lasers](/services/laser-treatments/pico-laser/) appear. Its limits on dark skin are well described:

- **Post-inflammatory darkening** after spot treatment, usually temporary.
- **Mottled pale spots** after many toning treatments at close intervals. This is the complication dermatologists most want to avoid, because it can last.
- **Rebound of melasma** when treatment stops.

For those reasons the clinic keeps energy low, spaces treatments, sets a limit on the length of a course and reviews the skin before each visit. Laser toning is not offered as an open-ended "brightening" package.

## Q-switch laser or pico laser?

Both break up pigment with very short pulses. Pico lasers are shorter still and often clear tattoos in fewer visits. For pigment on dark skin, studies have not shown a consistent winner. The choice is made on the diagnosis, your skin's response to a test patch, and practical factors. Read [Pico laser or Q-switch laser for dark spots?](/blog/pico-laser-or-q-switch-laser-for-dark-spots/) for a fuller comparison.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** The dermatologist confirms the diagnosis, prepares the skin with sunscreen and sometimes a pigment cream, and treats a test area. Avoid sun and irritating products on the area.

**During.** Eye shields are worn. Each pulse makes a snap. Treated spots may whiten for a few minutes. Toning takes about 10 to 15 minutes for a face.

**After.** Spots crust lightly and shed in one to two weeks; do not pick. After toning, mild redness lasts an hour or two. Moisturise, avoid heat and sun, and use sunscreen every day.

## Risks

Redness, swelling, crusting, temporary dark or light patches, mottled pale spots after repeated toning, blisters at high energy, and incomplete response. Scarring is uncommon with correct technique.

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> Be wary of unlimited "carbon laser" or "laser toning" packages sold without a doctor's assessment. Repeated treatment on dark skin, month after month, is how the pale-spot complication happens.

## What cholesterol is

Cholesterol is a waxy fat that the body uses to build cells and make hormones. The liver makes most of it; the rest comes from food. It travels in the blood inside particles called lipoproteins.

A blood test called a lipid profile measures:

- **Total cholesterol**
- **LDL cholesterol:** deposits cholesterol in artery walls
- **HDL cholesterol:** removes cholesterol
- **Non-HDL cholesterol:** all the harmful types together
- **Triglycerides:** another blood fat, raised by excess weight, sugar, alcohol and poorly controlled diabetes

## Why it matters

When LDL is high, cholesterol collects in the lining of arteries and forms plaque. Arteries narrow and stiffen. A plaque can rupture and trigger a clot, which causes a heart attack if it blocks an artery to the heart, or a stroke if it blocks one to the brain.

Cholesterol is one risk among several. The same level is more dangerous in someone who also smokes or has [high blood pressure](/services/internal-medicine/hypertension/), [diabetes](/services/internal-medicine/diabetes/), kidney disease or a strong family history. Doctors therefore treat overall risk, not a number on its own.

## What raises cholesterol

- A diet high in saturated and trans fats
- Excess weight and inactivity
- Smoking and heavy drinking
- Diabetes, an underactive thyroid, kidney and liver disease
- Some medicines
- **Inherited high cholesterol** (familial hypercholesterolaemia), which runs in families and causes very high levels from childhood
- Age, and for women, the menopause

## How it is assessed

1. **Lipid profile** from a blood sample.
2. **Other tests:** blood sugar, kidney, liver and thyroid function, to look for causes and to check that treatment will be safe.
3. **Blood pressure, weight and waist.**
4. **Risk estimate.** The doctor combines your results with age, sex, smoking and medical history to estimate your chance of a heart attack or stroke in the coming years.

The decision about treatment is based on that estimate and discussed with you.

## High cholesterol treatment: lowering your risk

**Food.** In practical Nigerian terms:

- Use less oil in soups and stews; skim visible oil from the surface.
- Grill, boil, steam or bake more often than you fry. Do not reuse frying oil repeatedly.
- Choose fish, skinless chicken and beans more often than fatty red meat, offal and processed meat.
- Fill half the plate with vegetables; choose whole grains, oats, beans and unripe plantain for fibre.
- Cut back on pastries, meat pies, doughnuts, biscuits and fried snacks.
- Limit sugary drinks and alcohol, which raise triglycerides.

**Activity and weight.** Regular exercise raises HDL and lowers triglycerides. Losing excess weight improves the whole profile.

**Smoking.** Stopping lowers risk quickly, whatever your cholesterol.

**Medicine.** For people at higher risk, or with inherited high cholesterol, doctors prescribe cholesterol-lowering medicine, most often from the group called statins. They are among the most thoroughly studied medicines in use and reduce heart attacks and strokes. Blood tests check the response and safety. If you are worried about side effects, discuss them with your doctor before making any change; this page does not advise anyone to start, stop or alter a medicine.

**Review.** Cholesterol is retested after about three months of any change, then yearly once stable.

## Cholesterol and the skin

Soft yellowish patches on the inner eyelids, called xanthelasma, are deposits of cholesterol. About half of people who have them have raised cholesterol, so they are a reason for a blood test. They can be removed by the dermatology team for appearance, but they tend to return if cholesterol stays high. Firm yellow lumps over knuckles, elbows, knees or the Achilles tendon suggest inherited high cholesterol and need medical assessment.

> [!DOCTOR] See a doctor soon if
> - a parent, brother or sister had a heart attack or stroke before 60, or has very high cholesterol
> - you have diabetes, high blood pressure or kidney disease and have never had a cholesterol test
> - you have yellowish deposits on your eyelids or over your tendons
> - you were told your cholesterol was high and it has not been rechecked

> [!URGENT] Urgent care
> Chest pain or pressure, pain spreading to the arm or jaw, sudden breathlessness, weakness or numbness on one side, a drooping face or slurred speech are emergencies. Go to the nearest hospital emergency department immediately.

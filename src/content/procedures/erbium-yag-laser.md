## How it works

"Er:YAG" names the crystal inside the laser. It produces infrared light at a wavelength of 2940 nanometres, which matches the peak absorption of water. Skin is mostly water, so each pulse is absorbed within a few thousandths of a millimetre and vaporises that thin layer cleanly.

Because so little heat escapes sideways, the doctor can remove tissue with precision, layer by layer. In **fractional** mode the beam treats a grid of tiny columns and leaves skin between them intact for quick healing.

## What it is used for

- [Acne scars](/conditions/acne-scars/) and other shallow scars
- Rough texture and [enlarged pores](/conditions/enlarged-pores/)
- Fine lines and sun damage
- Precise removal of some benign raised growths
- Preparing skin for better absorption of treatments applied afterwards

For the treatment itself, including preparation and aftercare, see [laser skin resurfacing](/services/laser-treatments/laser-skin-resurfacing/).

## Why it suits brown and Black skin

Pigment cells react to heat. A laser that leaves less heat behind gives them less reason to overproduce pigment. Studies comparing erbium with CO2 resurfacing report less redness, faster healing and fewer dark marks with erbium, at the cost of less tightening. For darker skin, where avoiding pigment change is the priority, that trade is usually worth making.

## Risks

Redness, swelling, peeling, temporary dark or light marks, infection including cold sores, and rarely scarring. These are explained before treatment and reduced by preparation and aftercare.

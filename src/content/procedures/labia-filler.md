## What a labia puff is

The outer labia are folds of skin with a pad of fat beneath. That pad can shrink with age, after significant weight loss or after pregnancy, leaving the skin looser. Labia filler places a soft gel into this fat layer to restore fullness. The inner labia and the vagina are not treated.

It is an elective treatment. Professional bodies in obstetrics and gynaecology advise that women considering any genital cosmetic procedure should be told clearly that normal appearance varies a great deal, and that the evidence for these procedures is limited. This page follows that advice.

## Which product is used

Hyaluronic acid filler is the product type used, because it is temporary and can be dissolved with an enzyme if needed. The specific brand and the amount proposed are explained to you at consultation, and you can see the sealed packaging. Permanent fillers and fat-dissolving or unlicensed products are never used in this area.

Its use here is "off-label": these fillers were approved for the face, and their use in the labia is supported by small case series rather than large trials.

## What is and is not known

- **Known:** hyaluronic acid adds volume, and in published series most women reported satisfaction over the following months.
- **Not well known:** how results hold beyond one to two years, the true rate of late complications, and whether filler improves comfort or sexual function. Studies are small and have no comparison group.

We make no promise about appearance, sensation or sexual function.

## Why a doctor must do it

The area has many blood vessels and nerves, and its skin is prone to infection. A doctor can first check that symptoms are not caused by a skin condition such as [lichen sclerosus](/conditions/lichen-sclerosus/), eczema or thrush, which need treatment and not filler. The doctor works with sterile technique, knows where vessels run, and has the dissolving enzyme and emergency medicines to hand.

## Who is not suitable

- Pregnancy, or within a few months of childbirth; breastfeeding
- Any genital infection, sores, rash or unexplained lump
- A bleeding disorder or treatment with blood thinners
- Allergy to hyaluronic acid filler or local anaesthetic
- An untreated skin condition of the vulva
- Under 18 years of age
- Pressure from a partner, or distress about appearance that a procedure is unlikely to resolve

If the doctor thinks treatment is not in your interest, she will tell you so.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** The consultation is private and unhurried, and you can ask for the female dermatologist and a chaperone. Your medical history is taken and the skin is examined. You are given written information and time to decide. Treatment is avoided during a period.

**During.** The skin is cleaned with antiseptic. Local anaesthetic is given, then filler is placed through one small entry point on each side with a blunt cannula. It takes about 30 to 45 minutes.

**After.** Swelling and tenderness last three to seven days; bruising can last longer. Wear loose cotton underwear, keep the area clean and dry, and avoid sex, tampons, swimming, cycling, hot baths and strenuous exercise for about a week. A review is arranged at two weeks.

## Risks and how they are managed

- **Swelling, bruising, tenderness:** expected; cool packs and simple pain relief.
- **Lumps or unevenness:** often settle; persistent ones can be dissolved.
- **Infection:** reduced by sterile technique and aftercare; treated promptly with antibiotics.
- **Dark marks at entry points:** more likely in skin that pigments easily; they fade.
- **Blocked blood vessel:** rare; treated at once with the dissolving enzyme.

> [!URGENT] Urgent care
> Contact the clinic the same day for severe or increasing pain, skin that turns pale, dusky or blistered, spreading redness, discharge, fever or difficulty passing urine.

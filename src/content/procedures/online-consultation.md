## Who an online consultation is for

Nigeria has far fewer dermatologists than it needs, and most practise in a handful of cities. For many people the nearest skin specialist is hours away. An online consultation closes that gap for a large share of skin problems.

It suits you if:

- you live outside Lagos, or the trip to Victoria Island is difficult
- you need a follow-up to check how treatment is working
- you want a first opinion on acne, a rash, dark patches or hair thinning
- you want a medication review or advice on a skincare routine
- you are unsure whether your problem needs a clinic visit at all

## What can be assessed online

| Works well online | Needs an in-person consultation |
|---|---|
| [Acne](/conditions/acne/) and acne follow-up | A changing, bleeding or new dark mole |
| [Eczema](/conditions/eczema/), psoriasis and other long-term rashes | Any lump under or on the skin |
| [Dark spots and uneven tone](/conditions/hyperpigmentation/) | Rashes with fever, blisters or skin pain |
| [Ringworm](/conditions/ringworm-and-fungal-skin-infections/) and other common infections | Conditions needing a scraping, swab or biopsy |
| Hair thinning and scalp conditions (first assessment) | Hair loss with scarring; nail lesions |
| Medication and skincare reviews | Procedures, peels, laser and injections |

If the doctor finds during the call that an examination is needed, you are told why and what to do next. Nothing is guessed.

## On brown and Black skin

Photographs flatten colour. Redness that is already subtle on dark skin can disappear on a phone camera, and pale patches can look washed out. Daylight, a plain background and no filters make a real difference. The dermatologist will also ask what the skin feels like, because raised, rough, warm or tender skin cannot be seen on screen. Dermatologists who see mostly brown and Black skin are used to reading these images; that experience matters more online than in the room.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** Book a time and tell us briefly what the consultation is about. You will be asked for photographs, a list of your medicines, creams and supplements, and any previous results. Check that your phone or laptop camera, microphone and internet connection work, and have the products you use beside you.

**During.** The dermatologist asks how the problem started, what makes it better or worse, and what you have tried. You may be asked to show the skin on camera, press on it or stretch it. The doctor explains what it is likely to be, how sure that is, and the treatment options. Ask anything you like.

**After.** You receive a written plan: what to use, how and for how long, what to stop, and when to be reviewed. If a prescription, a test or a visit is needed, the next step is arranged. Follow-up can usually be online as well.

## Limits and how they are managed

- **A diagnosis may be less certain** than in person. The doctor says so, and brings you in when it matters.
- **Photographs can mislead.** You may be asked to retake them.
- **Connections fail.** If the call drops, the clinic rings you back or rebooks.
- **Some medicines cannot be prescribed** without an examination or tests.
- **Emergencies are not handled online.**

> [!URGENT] Urgent care
> Do not wait for an online appointment if you have a rash with fever and feel unwell, skin that is blistering or peeling, swelling of the lips, tongue or face, difficulty breathing, or a rapidly spreading, hot, painful area of skin. Go to the nearest hospital emergency department.

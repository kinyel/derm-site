## What a general medical consultation is for

A general medical consultation is the starting point for most health concerns in adults. It is the right appointment when:

- you have a **new symptom** that you want explained: tiredness, headaches, dizziness, chest discomfort, breathlessness, stomach or bowel changes, fever, joint pain, weight change, poor sleep
- you want a **health check**, with or without symptoms
- you have a **long-term condition** that has not been reviewed recently
- you need a **review of your medicines**
- a skin, hair or nail condition suggests something in your **general health** needs checking

## What the doctor can do

- **Assess and diagnose** common medical problems
- **Measure and monitor** blood pressure, blood sugar, weight and waist
- **Arrange tests:** blood tests such as full blood count, glucose, cholesterol, kidney, liver and thyroid function; urine tests; and referral for scans or heart tracings where needed
- **Start and review treatment** for [high blood pressure](/services/internal-medicine/hypertension/), [diabetes](/services/internal-medicine/diabetes/) and [high cholesterol](/services/internal-medicine/high-cholesterol/)
- **Assess and treat infections;** see [infectious diseases](/services/internal-medicine/infectious-diseases/)
- **Advise on prevention:** diet, activity, weight, alcohol, smoking and sleep
- **Refer** to hospital specialists when required

## Why skin patients are sometimes sent to the doctor

The skin often gives the first clue to an internal condition. The dermatology team may suggest a medical consultation if you have:

- dark, velvety skin on the neck or underarms, which is linked to insulin resistance ([dark underarms, inner thighs, knees and neck](/conditions/dark-underarms-inner-thighs-knees-and-neck/))
- repeated [boils](/conditions/boils-and-abscesses/) or fungal infections, or cuts that heal slowly
- hair loss with tiredness or weight change
- generalised itching with no rash
- a skin treatment that needs blood pressure or blood tests monitored

Our article [Diabetes and your skin](/blog/diabetes-and-your-skin/) describes some of these links.

## How to prepare

- Bring every medicine, supplement and herbal product you take, or photographs of the labels.
- Bring previous test results, hospital letters and any home blood pressure or sugar readings.
- Write down your symptoms, when they started and what affects them.
- Know your family history if you can: high blood pressure, diabetes, stroke, heart disease, cancer.
- Wear sleeves that roll up easily.

## What happens afterwards

You are given a clear summary: what the doctor thinks is going on, which tests have been arranged and when the results will be ready, what to do meanwhile and when to come back. Long-term conditions are followed at regular reviews. This site does not advise anyone to start, stop or change a medicine; those decisions are made with your doctor.

> [!URGENT] Urgent care
> Do not book a routine appointment for chest pain or pressure, sudden shortness of breath, weakness or numbness on one side, a drooping face, slurred speech, sudden severe headache, fainting, confusion, vomiting blood or black stools. Go to the nearest hospital emergency department at once.

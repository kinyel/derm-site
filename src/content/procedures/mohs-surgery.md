## How it works

1. The visible tumour is marked and the area numbed.
2. A thin layer of tissue is removed around and beneath it, and the wound is dressed.
3. The layer is mapped, frozen, sliced and stained in the laboratory.
4. The doctor examines the entire edge and base under the microscope.
5. If cancer cells are seen, their exact position is known from the map, and another thin layer is taken only from that spot.
6. The steps repeat until no cancer remains. The wound is then closed with stitches, a skin flap or graft, or left to heal.

Standard excision removes the tumour with a fixed margin of normal skin and checks samples of the edge afterwards. Mohs checks all of the edge at the time of surgery.

## Who it is for

Mohs is chosen when the cancer type and site justify it. The dermatologist considers the biopsy result, the size and position of the tumour and your general health. Many small skin cancers on the trunk and limbs are treated just as well by standard excision; see [skin cancer care](/services/skin-cancer-care/).

## On brown and Black skin

Skin cancer is less common in dark skin, but squamous cell carcinoma can arise in old scars, burns and long-standing ulcers, and people with albinism develop sun-related cancers on the face and neck at a young age. These are the situations in which tissue-sparing surgery is most valuable. See [skin cancer on dark skin](/conditions/skin-cancer-on-dark-skin/).

## Before, during and after

**Before.** A [biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/) confirms the diagnosis. Tell the team about blood thinners, a pacemaker, allergies and any tendency to keloids. Eat normally and bring someone to take you home.

**During.** You are awake. Between stages you wait in comfort with a dressing in place.

**After.** A pressure dressing stays on for 24 to 48 hours. Rest, keep the head raised if the face was treated, and avoid heavy lifting for a week or two. Stitches are removed after about a week on the face. You are given written wound care instructions and a follow-up plan, including regular skin checks.

## Risks

Bleeding, infection, pain, numbness around the scar, a scar that is darker, lighter, raised or stretched, and rarely recurrence of the cancer.

> [!DERM] Contact the clinic after surgery if
> - bleeding does not stop with 20 minutes of firm pressure
> - the wound becomes more painful, swollen, hot or produces pus
> - you develop a fever

## Who needs a patch test

- Eczema on the hands, face, eyelids, neck or feet that keeps returning
- A rash under jewellery, a watch, a belt buckle or clothing fasteners
- Scalp, hairline or ear rash after hair dye, relaxer or braiding products
- Eczema that worsens despite correct treatment
- Rashes linked to work; see [corporate skin screening](/services/dermatology-consultations/corporate-skin-screening/)
- A reaction around a wound, to creams, plasters or antiseptics

## What is tested

A standard series covers the allergens responsible for most contact allergy. Extra series can be added for hairdressing, cosmetics, medicaments or rubber. Your own products, such as a face cream, hair product or black soap, can be tested too. Bring them, in their packaging, to the first visit.

## On brown and Black skin

A positive reaction is a small raised, itchy patch. On light skin it is red; on dark skin it may look darker brown, purple or simply bumpy, so the doctor reads by touch as well as sight. A strong reaction can leave a dark mark for weeks. Hair dyes, relaxers, bonding glue, fragrance and skin-lightening products are frequent causes of contact allergy seen in Lagos.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** Your back must be free of rash. Avoid sun on the back for two weeks. Tell the doctor about steroid tablets or immune-suppressing medicines, which can hide reactions, and do not apply steroid cream to the back for a week. Antihistamines do not need to be stopped.

**During.** Strips of small discs are taped to the upper back and outlined with a marker. They may itch. At the second visit they are removed and the skin is read; at the third it is read again.

**After.** You receive a written list of what you are allergic to, where each substance is found and how to avoid it. Avoidance is the treatment, and it often clears eczema that has lasted years.

## Risks

Itching, a strong local reaction, a lasting dark or pale mark, a flare of existing eczema, irritation from the tape and, rarely, becoming sensitised to a tested substance.

**Other allergy and light tests.** Prick testing, intradermal testing, specific IgE blood tests, phototesting and photopatch testing are arranged when the history points to immediate or light-triggered allergy.

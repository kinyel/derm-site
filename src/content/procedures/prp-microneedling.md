## The two parts of the treatment

**Microneedling** uses a pen-like device with fine sterile needles that pass in and out of the skin at a set depth. Each tiny puncture closes within hours, but the skin responds as it does to any injury, by laying down new collagen and elastin over the following weeks. See [microneedling](/services/aesthetic-treatments/microneedling/) for the treatment on its own.

**PRP** stands for platelet-rich plasma. Blood taken from your arm is spun in a centrifuge to separate the plasma that contains concentrated platelets. Platelets release growth factors that take part in wound healing. In a "vampire facial" the PRP is spread over the skin during needling, so that it passes into the channels, and sometimes small amounts are injected into scars.

The name comes from the pink colour of the plasma on the skin. Nothing is taken from anyone else: the PRP is your own.

## What it is used for

- [Acne scars](/conditions/acne-scars/), especially rolling and shallow boxcar scars
- [Enlarged pores](/conditions/enlarged-pores/) and rough texture
- Fine lines and loss of firmness
- [Stretch marks](/conditions/stretch-marks/), with more modest results
- Dull or uneven skin

It does not treat active acne, deep ice-pick scars on its own, or melasma.

## How strong is the evidence?

Microneedling for acne scars is supported by a reasonable number of trials. PRP has a longer history in orthopaedics and hair loss. For the combination, studies are small and vary in how PRP was prepared, which makes them hard to compare. Taken together they suggest a modest added benefit for scars and a quicker recovery. PRP is not a regulated medicine with a standard recipe, so results differ between clinics and between people. We describe it as a reasonable addition, not a certain upgrade. Our article [Exosomes, PRP or PDRN](/blog/exosomes-prp-or-pdrn/) compares it with newer options.

## PRP microneedling on brown and Black skin

Lasers and deep peels carry a real risk of dark marks on darker skin because they heat or strip it. Needles do neither, which is why microneedling is widely used for acne scars in skin of colour. The risk is not zero: needling too deeply or too often, or sun exposure afterwards, can still cause post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. The clinic prepares the skin, uses conservative depths over bony areas, spaces treatments at least four weeks apart and insists on sunscreen.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** The dermatologist checks that acne is controlled, asks about cold sores, keloids, blood disorders and medicines, and may start a pigment-calming cream. Stop retinoids and exfoliating acids about five days beforehand. Drink water and eat normally on the day; do not take anti-inflammatory painkillers unless prescribed, because they affect platelets.

**During.** Numbing cream is applied for 30 to 45 minutes. Meanwhile blood is taken and prepared. The skin is cleansed with antiseptic, PRP is applied and the device is passed over the skin in sections. Pinpoint bleeding is normal. The whole appointment takes about 90 minutes.

**After.** Expect redness and warmth like sunburn. For 24 hours, do not wash the face with anything but water, and avoid make-up, sweating and touching the skin. For a week, avoid sun, swimming, saunas, scrubs, retinoids and acids. Use a plain moisturiser and sunscreen.

## Risks and how they are managed

- **Redness, swelling, pinpoint scabs:** expected for a few days.
- **Dark marks:** uncommon; treated with pigment creams and sun protection.
- **Infection:** rare; sterile single-use needle cartridges and a clean technique are essential.
- **Cold sore flare:** preventive tablets if you are prone.
- **Bruising** where blood was taken or PRP injected.
- **Tram-track marks or scarring:** linked to excessive depth or pressure; avoided by correct technique.

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> PRP must be prepared and used in a clinical setting with single-use sterile equipment. Blood-borne infections have been reported from unlicensed premises that reused equipment. Home dermarollers with long needles are also best avoided; they blunt quickly and cannot be sterilised.

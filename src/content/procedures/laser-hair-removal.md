## How laser hair removal works

Laser light is absorbed by melanin, the pigment in the hair shaft. The light turns to heat, which travels down the hair and damages the follicle that produces it. A damaged follicle makes a finer hair, a slower-growing hair or none.

The difficulty in dark skin is that the same melanin is present in the skin itself. A laser that cannot tell hair pigment from skin pigment will heat both. Three things solve this:

- **A longer wavelength.** Light at 1064 nanometres penetrates deeper and is absorbed less at the skin surface.
- **A longer pulse.** Delivering the energy more slowly gives the skin time to cool while the thicker hair holds its heat.
- **Cooling** of the skin surface before, during and after each pulse.

The device and settings used for your skin type are confirmed at your consultation, and a test patch is carried out before your first full treatment.

## Areas treated

Upper lip, chin, jawline and neck; underarms; bikini line and Brazilian; arms and legs; chest, abdomen and back; and full body plans. Men commonly ask for the beard line, neck, chest and back. The area around the eyes is not treated.

## Who it is for

- People who want less hair to shave, wax or thread
- Men and women with [razor bumps](/conditions/razor-bumps/) or [ingrown hairs](/conditions/ingrown-hairs/)
- Women with [excess facial or body hair](/conditions/hirsutism/), alongside treatment of any hormonal cause
- People with repeated [folliculitis](/conditions/folliculitis/) from shaving

Laser works on dark hair. It does not work on grey, white or very fine vellus hair. It is postponed in pregnancy, over tattoos, with active skin infection, recent sun exposure, or within six months of acne tablets from the retinoid family.

## Laser hair removal on brown and Black skin

Hair removal lasers were first built for light skin with dark hair. Used on dark skin with the wrong settings, they cause burns, blisters, dark patches and sometimes permanent pale marks. Most complaints dermatologists see after hair removal elsewhere come from intense pulsed light (IPL) machines and from operators who did not adjust for skin tone.

The safeguards are the ones described above, plus sun avoidance and a careful history. If you have used a bleaching cream on the area, say so: it changes how the skin responds to heat. Our article [Is laser hair removal safe on dark skin?](/blog/is-laser-hair-removal-safe-on-dark-skin/) explains what to ask any clinic before you book.

Paradoxical hair growth, where fine hair becomes thicker near a treated area, is uncommon but is reported more often on the face and neck in people with darker skin. You are told about it before facial treatment.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** At the consultation the doctor checks your skin type, hair colour and thickness, medicines and any signs of a hormonal cause. A test patch is done and reviewed. Shave 12 to 24 hours before each treatment.

**During.** You wear protective goggles. The handpiece is pressed to the skin and fired in a pattern across the area, with cooling. Underarms take about 10 minutes; full legs up to an hour.

**After.** The skin is pink and each follicle may look slightly raised for a few hours, like goosebumps. This is the expected response. Apply a cool compress and plain moisturiser. For 48 hours avoid heat, hot showers, exercise that causes sweating, deodorant on treated underarms, and perfumed products. Use sunscreen on exposed areas. Treated hairs shed over one to three weeks; do not pluck them.

## Risks and how they are managed

| Possible effect | What is done |
|---|---|
| Redness, swelling around follicles | Expected; settles in hours |
| Burn or blister | Prevented by test patch and settings; treated with dressings and review |
| Dark or light patches | Usually fade over months; sun protection and pigment treatment |
| Folliculitis or acne-like bumps | Settles; treated if persistent |
| Increased fine hair growth nearby | Uncommon; settings and plan are changed |
| Eye injury | Prevented by goggles |

> [!DERM] Contact the clinic after treatment if
> - the skin blisters, crusts or stays painful after 24 hours
> - a treated area becomes darker or lighter than the skin around it
> - you develop pus-filled bumps

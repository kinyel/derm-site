## What butt filler is

Butt filler is an injectable treatment that adds volume to the buttocks or fills the hollow at the side of the hips. It appeals because there is no general anaesthetic, no liposuction and little time off work. It is also heavily marketed, and the marketing often leaves out how limited the change is and what can go wrong. This page sets out both.

## Which products are used

Two medical product types are used for this purpose:

- **Hyaluronic acid body fillers.** Thicker versions of the gels used in the face. They give volume straight away, are absorbed over one to two years and can be dissolved if there is a problem.
- **Collagen stimulators,** such as poly-L-lactic acid or calcium hydroxylapatite. These are injected in a diluted form and prompt the body to lay down its own collagen over several months. The change is gradual and cannot be reversed with an enzyme.

The product type proposed for you, its brand and the amount are explained at consultation, and you can ask to see the sealed packaging. At the time of writing, most regulators have approved these products for the face or hands; their use in the buttocks is "off-label", which means the doctor is responsible for explaining the evidence and the uncertainty to you.

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> Liquid silicone, "biopolymers", hydrogel, PMMA, oils and any injection described only as "butt shots" or "enhancement injection" must never be injected into the body. They cause hard lumps, chronic infection, skin death, disfigurement and deaths, sometimes years later. No doctor at this clinic will inject them, and no licensed clinic should.

## Why a doctor must do it

The buttock has large blood vessels and a deep nerve. Product placed too deep, or into a vessel, can block blood flow. A doctor knows the anatomy, works in the fat layer above the muscle, uses sterile technique for what is a large-volume injection, and can recognise and treat infection or a blocked vessel. Those are medical skills, and they are the reason this is a medical procedure.

## Who it may suit, and who it does not

It may suit an adult at a stable weight who wants a modest change in fullness or a smoother hip contour and accepts that the result is subtle and temporary.

It is not suitable if you:

- are pregnant or breastfeeding
- have a skin infection, a bleeding disorder or uncontrolled diabetes
- have had silicone, biopolymer or unknown injections in the area
- have an active autoimmune or connective tissue condition
- are hoping for a large change in size or shape

We do not promise a size, a shape or a measurement. Bodies respond differently, and a consultation that promises a specific outcome is a warning sign.

## On brown and Black skin

Injection points can leave small dark marks that take weeks or months to fade. People who form [keloids](/conditions/keloids/) should tell the doctor. [Stretch marks](/conditions/stretch-marks/) and uneven skin tone on the buttocks are not improved by filler and have their own treatments.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** The consultation covers your health, medicines, previous procedures and what you hope to change. The doctor examines the area and tells you plainly what is achievable. You are given time to think; treatment is not carried out at a first conversation under pressure.

**During.** The skin is cleaned thoroughly with antiseptic and numbed with local anaesthetic. The product is placed through a few entry points with a blunt cannula, in the fat layer. Treatment takes 45 to 90 minutes.

**After.** Soreness, swelling and bruising are expected for one to two weeks. You are asked to avoid sitting for long periods and lying on your back for the first days, to avoid strenuous exercise for about two weeks, and to keep the entry points clean. Collagen stimulators need massage as instructed.

## Risks and how they are managed

- **Infection:** reduced by sterile technique; treated early with antibiotics. Deep infection can need drainage.
- **Lumps, nodules and unevenness:** reduced by correct depth and dilution; some settle, some need treatment.
- **Product movement or loss of result** as the body absorbs it.
- **Blocked blood vessel:** rare; needs immediate treatment.
- **Dark marks** at entry points.

> [!URGENT] Urgent care
> Seek medical help at once for increasing pain, spreading redness, heat or swelling, fever, skin that turns pale, dusky or blistered, shortness of breath or chest pain after any body injection.

## Alternatives to consider

Strength training changes the muscle under the fat and is the only method that alters shape without risk. Surgery gives a larger change with greater risk and should be discussed with a plastic surgeon. Doing nothing is also a reasonable choice, and the doctor will say so if the risks outweigh the benefit for you.

## What PDRN is

PDRN stands for polydeoxyribonucleotide. Together with related products called polynucleotides (PN), it consists of short fragments of DNA extracted from the reproductive cells of salmon or trout and purified. Fish DNA is used because it is very similar in structure to human DNA and can be produced with a high degree of purity.

In laboratory studies, these fragments bind to a receptor on skin cells that is involved in tissue repair. They appear to encourage the cells that make collagen, reduce inflammation and attract water into the skin. PDRN was first developed in medicine for slow-healing wounds.

## How it is given

- **Injection.** A series of very small injections placed just under the surface across the treated area, sometimes described as skin boosters.
- **With microneedling.** The solution is applied to the skin during [microneedling](/services/aesthetic-treatments/microneedling/) so that it enters the channels.

Creams and serums containing polynucleotides are also sold. Whether enough passes through intact skin to have an effect is uncertain.

The product used, its registration status and the method proposed are explained at your consultation.

## What it is used for

People ask for this treatment to improve dullness, fine lines, crepey skin under the eyes, enlarged pores, mild scarring and skin that looks tired or dehydrated. It is not a treatment for deep wrinkles, sagging, volume loss or pigmentation.

## What is known and what is not

**Known.** PDRN has an established role in wound healing research. Small clinical studies in aesthetic use report gains in hydration, elasticity and texture, and in some, improvement in acne scars and under-eye skin. Side effects in these studies were mild.

**Not known.** Whether results hold up in large, independent, controlled trials; how long any benefit lasts; the best dose and schedule; and how it compares with established treatments. Many studies are short and involve few people. Our article [Exosomes, PRP or PDRN](/blog/exosomes-prp-or-pdrn/) compares the three.

The evidence is best described as emerging. We make no promise about results.

## On brown and Black skin

The product does not target pigment and does not heat the skin, so it does not carry the pigment risks of lasers and peels. The risk on darker skin comes from the needles: each injection point or needling channel is a small injury that can leave a pinpoint dark mark in skin that pigments easily. Fine needles, sun protection and not touching the skin afterwards keep this to a minimum.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** The dermatologist asks about allergies, including fish, your medicines, pregnancy, cold sores and any tendency to keloids, then examines the skin and agrees the aim with you.

**During.** Numbing cream is applied for about 30 minutes. The solution is then injected in a grid of tiny points, leaving small raised bumps, or applied with microneedling. Treatment takes 20 to 40 minutes.

**After.** The bumps flatten within a day or two. Redness, pinpoint bruising and mild swelling can last two to three days, longer under the eyes. Avoid make-up for 12 to 24 hours, and heat, heavy exercise, sun and active skincare for a few days.

## Risks and how they are managed

- **Bumps, redness, swelling, bruising:** expected and short-lived.
- **Dark marks at injection points:** uncommon; fade over weeks.
- **Infection:** rare with sterile technique.
- **Allergic reaction:** rare; tell the doctor about fish or seafood allergy.
- **Little or no visible change:** a real possibility with a treatment whose evidence is still developing.

## Who should avoid it

Pregnancy and breastfeeding, active skin infection or inflammation in the area, known fish allergy unless the doctor advises otherwise, bleeding disorders, and anyone who does not wish to use an animal-derived product.

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> Do not buy polynucleotide ampoules online to inject at home, and do not have them injected outside a clinic. The sterility and contents of such products cannot be checked.

## What "pico" means

A picosecond is one trillionth of a second. Earlier pigment lasers, called Q-switched lasers, work in nanoseconds, which are a thousand times longer. The shorter the pulse, the less time heat has to spread from the target into the skin around it.

At picosecond speeds the laser's effect on pigment is mostly mechanical. The particle absorbs energy faster than it can expand, and it fractures. This is called a photoacoustic effect. Nanosecond lasers rely more on heat. The practical consequences are finer fragments of pigment and less heating of nearby skin.

## What a pico laser treats

**Tattoos.** The strongest evidence for picosecond lasers is in [tattoo removal](/services/laser-treatments/laser-tattoo-removal/), where they often need fewer treatments than nanosecond lasers and do better with blue and green inks.

**Pigment.** Sun spots, freckles, some birthmarks and selected stubborn [dark marks](/conditions/hyperpigmentation/). [Melasma](/conditions/melasma/) is approached cautiously with any laser because it tends to relapse. See [laser pigmentation removal](/services/laser-treatments/laser-pigmentation-removal/).

**Scars and texture.** A fractional lens divides the beam into a grid of tiny, intense points. Each point creates a microscopic bubble inside the skin without breaking the surface, and the repair response builds collagen. This is used for [acne scars](/conditions/acne-scars/), enlarged pores and fine lines, with less downtime than ablative [resurfacing](/services/laser-treatments/laser-skin-resurfacing/).

## Pico laser on brown and Black skin

Less heat is an advantage on skin that responds to heat by making pigment. Published studies in Asian and darker skin types report good clearance of several pigment problems with pico lasers and a lower rate of burns than with older devices. They also report temporary darkening and lightening in a share of patients, so the technology reduces risk; it does not remove it.

What decides safety is the same on any machine:

1. The right diagnosis, because laser is the wrong tool for some pigment.
2. The 1064-nanometre wavelength for dark skin.
3. Low energy, built up over visits.
4. A test patch read after four weeks.
5. Sun avoidance and sunscreen before and after.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** A consultation establishes what is being treated and whether laser is the right choice. A test area is done first. Avoid sun, waxing and irritating skincare on the area for two to four weeks.

**During.** You wear eye shields. Numbing cream is used for tattoos and fractional treatment. The pulses feel like quick hot pinpricks. A face takes about 15 to 20 minutes.

**After.** Cool the skin, moisturise and wear sunscreen daily. Do not pick crusts. Avoid heat, exercise that causes heavy sweating and active skincare for a few days.

## Risks

Temporary darkening or lightening of the treated skin, redness, swelling, pinpoint bleeding or crusting, blisters, a cold sore flare, and incomplete clearance. Scarring is uncommon. These are discussed with you before treatment, and the plan is adjusted to your skin's response.

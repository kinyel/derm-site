## What dermal fillers are

Dermal fillers are gels injected under the skin to restore volume or change contour. The most widely used are made of hyaluronic acid, a substance that occurs naturally in skin and holds water. These fillers are temporary and can be dissolved if needed. Other types stimulate the skin to make collagen over several months; they last longer but cannot be reversed.

The product type and brand chosen for you are explained at your consultation, with the reason for the choice. You can ask to see the sealed box before treatment.

Fillers add or replace volume. They do not relax muscles; that is the job of [Botox](/services/aesthetic-treatments/botox/). The two are often used together, and our article on [Botox and fillers](/blog/botox-or-fillers/) explains which suits which concern.

## Areas treated

- **Lips:** definition of the border, balance between upper and lower lip, or more fullness.
- **Cheeks:** volume lost with age or weight loss, which also softens the folds beside the nose.
- **Chin and jawline:** a stronger chin or a cleaner jaw contour.
- **Under-eye hollows (tear troughs):** in carefully selected people.
- **Facial contouring** and some depressed scars.

## Who they are for

Adults in good health who want a specific, realistic change. A good consultation often narrows the plan: many people who ask for lip filler need only a small amount to restore shape, and many "dark circles" are pigment that filler cannot help.

## Fillers on brown and Black skin

Faces with darker skin tend to age through loss of volume in the mid-face rather than fine wrinkles, so cheek support is a common request. Lips are often naturally full, and treatment aims at shape and symmetry more than size. Proportions matter: the standards in filler training materials were drawn largely from European faces, and a plan should follow your own features.

Two risks need attention on darker skin. Needle entry points can leave small dark marks, so the fewest possible entry points are used. People who form [keloids](/conditions/keloids/) should say so, although keloids from fine filler needles are uncommon.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** The doctor examines your face at rest and in movement, takes photographs for your record with your consent, and explains the plan, the amount and the likely result. Avoid alcohol, and ask your own doctor before stopping any prescribed blood thinner. If you get cold sores, tell the doctor: lip injections can trigger one, and preventive treatment can be given.

**During.** Numbing cream is applied for 20 to 30 minutes. The filler is placed with a fine needle or a blunt cannula, a little at a time, with checks from different angles. Treatment takes 20 to 45 minutes depending on the area.

**After.** Expect swelling, tenderness and sometimes bruising for a few days, most of all in the lips. Sleep with your head raised on the first night. Avoid heavy exercise, alcohol, saunas and pressure on the area for 24 to 48 hours, and dental treatment or facials for two weeks. The result is judged at two weeks, once swelling has gone.

## Risks and how they are managed

**Common and short-lived:** swelling, redness, bruising, tenderness and small lumps that settle or can be massaged out.

**Less common:** uneven results, filler that can be seen or felt, prolonged puffiness under the eyes, infection, and late lumps that appear weeks or months afterwards. Hyaluronic acid can be dissolved; infection is treated with antibiotics.

**Rare but serious:** blockage of a blood vessel. The doctor works with knowledge of where the vessels run, injects slowly in small amounts and keeps the dissolving enzyme ready in the room.

> [!URGENT] Urgent care
> Contact the clinic immediately, or go to a hospital emergency department, if after filler you have severe or increasing pain, skin that turns white, grey, mottled or dusky, blisters, or any change in your vision. These are signs that a blood vessel may be blocked, and treatment is most effective in the first hours.

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> Never have permanent fillers, liquid silicone or products bought online, and never have filler injected by someone who is not a doctor with access to emergency treatment. Needle-free "hyaluron pens" are unsafe and are not used here.

## How long results last

Six to twelve months in the lips, twelve to eighteen months in the cheeks, chin and jawline, and often longer under the eyes. You can let filler fade or have a smaller top-up before it has gone completely.

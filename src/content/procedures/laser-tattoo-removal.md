## How laser tattoo removal works

Tattoo ink sits in the deeper layer of skin as particles too large for the body's cleaning cells to carry away. A tattoo laser fires a pulse lasting a billionth of a second ([Q-switched laser](/services/laser-treatments/q-switch-laser/)) or a trillionth ([picosecond, or pico, laser](/services/laser-treatments/pico-laser/)). The ink absorbs the energy and breaks apart before the heat can spread to the surrounding skin. Over the following weeks, immune cells remove the fragments, and the tattoo fades.

Each ink colour absorbs a particular wavelength. Black absorbs all of them and is the easiest to treat. Coloured inks need specific wavelengths, some of which are also absorbed strongly by melanin.

## What affects the result

- **Colour:** black and dark blue respond best; red is variable; green, light blue, yellow and white are stubborn.
- **Amateur or professional:** amateur tattoos use less ink, placed unevenly, and often clear faster.
- **Age:** older tattoos have already faded and need fewer treatments.
- **Position:** tattoos on the trunk and upper limbs clear faster than those on hands, ankles and feet, where circulation is slower.
- **Layering and cover-ups:** more ink, more treatments.
- **Skin tone and scarring** from the original tattoo.

## Tattoo removal on brown and Black skin

Black ink on dark skin is treated with the 1064-nanometre wavelength, which passes the pigment at the surface with relatively little absorption. Wavelengths used for green and red inks are absorbed by melanin and can strip pigment, leaving pale patches, so coloured tattoos on dark skin can sometimes be faded only partly.

Other points that matter on darker skin:

- Settings start low and rise slowly, with longer gaps between treatments.
- Temporary dark or light patches are common and usually recover over months.
- [Keloid scar](/conditions/keloids/) risk is assessed before any treatment.
- Cosmetic tattoos (eyebrows, lips) and some white or skin-coloured inks can turn dark when lasered. These always have a small test first.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** At the consultation the dermatologist examines the tattoo, asks how and when it was done, and checks for keloids, cold sores, allergies to the ink and medicines. A test area is treated and reviewed after six to eight weeks. Keep the area out of the sun for four weeks before each visit.

**During.** Numbing cream is applied for 30 to 45 minutes. You wear eye protection. The laser makes a snapping sound; treated ink turns white for a few minutes ("frosting") and the skin swells slightly. A dressing is applied.

**After.** Expect redness, swelling, pinpoint bleeding, and sometimes blisters and crusts over the next few days. This is part of healing. Keep the dressing on for 24 hours, then wash gently, apply the ointment you are given and cover the area. Do not pick scabs or burst blisters. Avoid soaking, swimming and sun until healed, usually within two weeks.

## Risks and how they are managed

| Possible effect | What is done |
|---|---|
| Blisters, crusts, swelling | Expected; dressings and ointment |
| Light patches (loss of pigment) | Often recover over months; settings reduced, intervals lengthened |
| Dark patches | Sun protection and pigment creams |
| Scarring or change in texture | Uncommon; reduced by conservative settings and good aftercare |
| Keloid | Screened for beforehand; treated early if it occurs |
| Darkening of cosmetic or white ink | Test spot first |
| Allergic reaction as ink is released | Rare; treated medically |
| Infection | Clean dressings; antibiotics if needed |

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> Do not use tattoo removal creams, acids, salt scrubbing or injections sold online. They burn the skin and leave scars without removing the ink. Avoid laser removal offered by tattoo studios or salons without medical supervision.

## Alternatives

A small tattoo can sometimes be cut out and stitched, leaving a line scar; see [scar revision](/services/skin-surgery/scar-revision/). Fading a tattoo so that it can be covered by a new design needs fewer treatments than full removal.

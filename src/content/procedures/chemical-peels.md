## How a peel works

A peel solution loosens the bonds between skin cells so that the outer layers shed in a controlled way. New skin from below replaces them, with more even pigment and a smoother surface. How deep the peel goes depends on the acid, its strength, how long it stays on and how the skin was prepared.

| Depth | Reaches | Used for | Downtime |
|---|---|---|---|
| Superficial | Outer epidermis | Dark marks, acne, dullness, rough texture | 0–5 days of light flaking |
| Medium | Upper dermis | Deeper pigment, fine lines, some scars | 7–10 days of peeling |
| Deep | Mid dermis | Deep wrinkles, on light skin only | Weeks; not advised on dark skin |

Common superficial peel acids include glycolic, lactic, mandelic and salicylic acid. The doctor chooses by what is being treated: salicylic acid suits oily, acne-prone skin, while mandelic and lactic acid are gentler choices for pigment in dark skin.

## What peels treat

- [Dark marks and uneven tone](/conditions/hyperpigmentation/), including marks left by acne
- Active [acne](/conditions/acne/) and blocked pores
- [Melasma](/conditions/melasma/), as part of a wider plan
- Rough, dull or sun-damaged skin
- Shallow [acne scars](/conditions/acne-scars/)
- Body areas: back, chest, and [dark underarms, knees and elbows](/conditions/dark-underarms-inner-thighs-knees-and-neck/), where friction is addressed as well

## Chemical peels on brown and Black skin

Dark skin responds well to peels, but the margin for error is narrower. Any injury that goes too deep can trigger the pigment cells and leave a new dark patch, called post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, or occasionally a pale one. This is why the common fear that peels darken Black skin has some truth in it: it happens when a peel is too strong, the skin was not prepared or the sun was not avoided. Our article [Do chemical peels darken Black skin?](/blog/do-chemical-peels-darken-black-skin/) goes through this in detail.

The approach on darker skin is therefore:

1. **Prepare the skin** for two to four weeks with sunscreen and, where needed, a prescription cream that calms pigment cells.
2. **Start low.** A mild peel for a short time, increasing only if the skin tolerates it.
3. **Repeat instead of going deep.** A series of gentle peels gives a better result with less risk than one strong peel.
4. **Protect afterwards.** Sunscreen every day, and no picking.

## Before, during and after

**Before.** The dermatologist examines your skin and asks about cold sores, keloids, eczema, recent waxing, pregnancy and medicines, including acne tablets taken in the last six months. You may be asked to stop retinoids, scrubs and hair removal a week beforehand. Arrive without make-up.

**During.** The skin is cleansed and degreased. The solution is applied with gauze or a brush and left for a set time, usually a few minutes, while the doctor watches the skin's reaction. It is then neutralised or removed, and moisturiser and sunscreen are applied. The appointment takes about 30 minutes.

**After.** The skin may look slightly darker and feel tight before it flakes. Let it shed on its own. Wash gently, moisturise often and use sunscreen. Stay out of direct sun and avoid heat and sweating for 48 hours. Restart your usual products only when advised.

## Risks and how they are managed

- **Redness, stinging, flaking:** expected and short-lived.
- **Dark marks (post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation):** the main risk on dark skin; reduced by preparation, mild peels and sun protection, and treated with pigment creams if it occurs.
- **Pale patches:** uncommon with superficial peels.
- **Cold sore flare:** preventive tablets are given if you are prone.
- **Infection or scarring:** rare with superficial peels; more likely if skin is picked.
- **Irritation or allergy** to the solution.

> [!DERM] Contact the clinic after a peel if
> - the skin blisters, weeps or forms thick crusts
> - pain or swelling increases after the first day
> - a patch becomes much darker or lighter over the following weeks
> - you develop a cold sore

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> Do not use peel solutions bought online or in markets. Many are far stronger than their label says, and home peels are a frequent cause of burns and lasting dark patches in Lagos.

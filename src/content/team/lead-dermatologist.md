## A consultant-led clinic

At {{clinic}}, consultations for medical skin conditions are led by a consultant dermatologist. A consultant is a doctor who has finished specialist training in dermatology and takes responsibility for diagnosis and treatment. {{experience}}

That length of practice matters most with the conditions that are hardest to tell apart. A pale patch can be vitiligo, pityriasis versicolor, a mark left by eczema or, rarely, something more serious. A dark mark on the cheek can be melasma, a mark from acne or the effect of a bleaching cream. On brown and Black skin these look different from the textbook photographs, and telling them apart quickly comes from having seen them many times.

## Why some patients ask for a female dermatologist in Lagos

A full skin examination can mean undressing, and some conditions affect the breasts, buttocks, groin or genital skin. Many women, and some men, find that easier with a female doctor. Others ask for religious or cultural reasons. You do not have to explain your reasons. You can also ask for a chaperone to be in the room.

Parents often tell us that children and teenage girls talk more freely about acne, hair loss and body changes with a woman doctor. Whatever your reason, say so when you book and the clinic will arrange it.

## What a consultation with her is like

She starts by listening: what you have noticed, how long it has been there, what you have already used, and what worries you most. She then examines the skin, often with a dermatoscope, a handheld magnifier with a light. If the diagnosis is clear, she explains it and agrees a treatment plan with you. If it is not, she will suggest a test such as a skin scraping, a swab, a biopsy or a blood test.

You can expect direct answers about what treatment can and cannot do, how long it usually takes and what it involves. Where a cosmetic treatment is unlikely to help, or carries more risk on darker skin, she will say so.

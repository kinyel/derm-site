## What shingles looks like on brown and Black skin

Pain usually comes first, and is often mistaken for a pulled muscle, a heart or kidney problem, or toothache. Then a band of skin on one side becomes swollen and darker or dusky, and clusters of small blisters appear along it. The band follows one nerve, so it wraps from the spine round to the front of the chest or abdomen, or runs down one side of the forehead, and does not cross the midline.

Blisters turn cloudy, sometimes blood-stained, then dry to dark crusts. On dark skin, healing commonly leaves lighter or darker patches, and sometimes scars or [keloid scars](/conditions/keloids/), in the same band.

## How to spot it

- The rash is on one side only.
- It is painful or burning more than itchy.
- Blisters are grouped in patches along a stripe.
- You felt pain or tingling there before anything showed.

## Causes and triggers

After chickenpox, the varicella-zoster virus lies dormant in nerve roots. When immune surveillance dips with age, illness or stress, it multiplies and travels down the nerve to the skin.

> [!URGENT] Seek medical care the same day if
> - the rash is on the forehead, eyelid or tip of the nose, or the eye is red or painful
> - you have weak immunity, are pregnant, or the rash is widespread
> - there is weakness of the face, hearing change or a rash in the ear
> - you have a severe headache, confusion or a high fever

> [!DERM] See a doctor within 72 hours of the rash appearing
> Antivirals work best when started early. Later than this they are still considered in older adults, severe cases and reduced immunity.

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Herbal pastes, chalk, engine oil or battery fluid on the blisters
> - Steroid creams
> - Bursting blisters or picking crusts
> - Close contact with pregnant women, babies and unwell people until crusted

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The pattern is usually unmistakable. A swab confirms the virus when the rash is atypical. Tests for diabetes and HIV may be offered through the [internal medicine service](/services/internal-medicine/general-medical-consultation/).

## Shingles treatment at the clinic

- **Antiviral tablets** for seven days.
- **Pain control** stepped up as needed, including medicines that calm nerve pain.
- **Skin care:** loose cotton clothing, non-stick dressings, and antibiotics only if bacteria infect the blisters.
- **Eye involvement** is referred urgently to an ophthalmologist.
- **Afterwards:** treatment for persistent nerve pain and for marks or scars.

## Prevention and daily care

- Keep the rash clean, dry and covered.
- Wash hands after touching it.
- Rest, eat well and keep long-term conditions under control.
- Ask about shingles vaccination if you are over 50.

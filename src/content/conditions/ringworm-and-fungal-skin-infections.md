## What fungal infections look like on brown and Black skin

The classic ring is a round patch with a slightly raised, scaly border and a calmer centre. On dark skin the border looks dark brown, grey or violet, not red, and the patch often leaves a darker stain behind it as it spreads outwards.

The name changes with the site:

- **Body (tinea corporis):** one or several rings on the trunk, limbs or face.
- **Groin (tinea cruris, "jock itch"):** a dark, itchy rash on the inner thighs with a defined edge, usually sparing the scrotum.
- **Scalp (tinea capitis):** mainly in children. Scaly patches, black dots where hairs have broken at the surface, patchy hair loss, and sometimes a swollen, boggy, pus-filled lump (kerion) with tender glands in the neck.
- **Feet:** see [athlete's foot](/conditions/athletes-foot/).
- **Nails:** see [fungal nail infection](/conditions/fungal-nail-infection/).
- **Beard area:** inflamed bumps and broken hairs in men.

After steroid or mixed creams, ringworm loses its shape. It becomes a large, ill-defined, dark, itchy area with scattered bumps and little scale. Doctors call this tinea incognito, and it is now common in Lagos.

A related but different condition, [pityriasis versicolor](/conditions/pityriasis-versicolor/), is caused by a yeast and produces many small pale or dark patches on the chest and back.

## How to spot it

- An itchy patch that grows outwards over days or weeks
- A scaly, more active edge and a quieter centre
- Someone else at home, a pet, or a recent barber visit with a similar rash
- In a child: scaling on the scalp, patches of broken hair, or swollen glands behind the ears
- A rash that improved with a mixed cream and then spread

Eczema can look similar. See [Ringworm or eczema: how to tell the difference](/blog/ringworm-or-eczema/).

## Causes and triggers

Ringworm is caused by fungi called dermatophytes that live on keratin in skin, hair and nails. They thrive in warmth and moisture, which Lagos supplies year-round. You catch them from:

- **People:** skin contact, shared towels, clothes, bedding, combs and hats
- **Barbers' clippers** that are not disinfected between customers
- **Animals:** cats, dogs, goats and cattle
- **Your own feet or nails,** which can reinfect the groin or body
- **Gym mats and changing rooms**

Sweat, tight synthetic clothing, obesity, diabetes, weakened immunity and steroid creams all make infection easier.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - a child has a scaly or bald patch on the scalp
> - the rash has not improved after two weeks of antifungal cream
> - it is spreading, widespread or keeps returning
> - you have used a mixed or steroid cream on it
> - the area is painful, swollen or draining pus
> - you have diabetes or a weakened immune system

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Mixed "triple-action" creams and any cream containing a steroid
> - Battery acid, bleach, brake fluid, hot metal and other caustic home treatments
> - Stopping treatment as soon as the itch goes
> - Sharing towels, clippers, combs, caps and bedding
> - Shaving a child's head as the only treatment for scalp ringworm

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Typical ringworm can be diagnosed by looking. When it is not typical, has been treated with creams already, or involves the scalp or nails, a [fungal test](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/fungal-tests/) is done: a scraping, hair sample or nail clipping examined under the microscope and sometimes cultured. A Wood's lamp helps with some scalp infections.

## Ringworm treatment at the clinic

- **Limited body or groin infection:** an antifungal cream for two to four weeks, applied beyond the visible edge and continued after the skin clears.
- **Scalp, beard, nail or extensive infection, and tinea incognito:** antifungal tablets for weeks, with an antifungal shampoo for scalp infection. Liver blood tests are checked when treatment is prolonged.
- **Resistant infection:** some strains now respond poorly to standard treatment; culture guides the choice of medicine.
- **Household:** family members and pets with signs are treated at the same time; clippers, combs and bedding are cleaned.
- **Dark marks** afterwards fade with time and sun protection; see [hyperpigmentation](/conditions/hyperpigmentation/).

Medicines are dispensed by the clinic's [pharmacy](/services/dermatology-pharmacy/). Children are seen through [children's skin care](/services/childrens-skin-care/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Dry skin folds and between the toes after bathing.
- Wear loose cotton underwear and change it daily; change out of sweaty clothes promptly.
- Do not share towels, clothing, combs or hats.
- Take your own clippers to the barber, or ask for them to be disinfected.
- Treat athlete's foot so that it does not spread to the groin.
- Wash bedding and towels in hot water during treatment.
- Have pets with bald, scaly patches checked by a vet.

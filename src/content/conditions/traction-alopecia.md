## What traction alopecia looks like on African hair

The hairline moves back at the temples and in front of the ears, usually on both sides, and the remaining hair is sparse and short. A thin row of fine hairs often survives at the very front, marking where the hairline used to be; dermatologists call this the fringe sign. With cornrows, thinning may follow the partings; with a tight bun, it sits where the pull is strongest.

Early on there may be tenderness, tiny bumps at the roots and flaking. Later there is no discomfort at all, only loss, which is why many people do not seek help until the skin is smooth.

## How to spot it

- Compare your hairline with a photograph from five years ago.
- Run a finger along the temples: are hairs shorter, finer and fewer?
- Does your scalp hurt on the first night after braiding?
- Are there bumps or crusts at the roots within a week of a new style?

Thinning over the crown is more often [female pattern hair loss](/conditions/female-pattern-hair-loss/) or a [scarring alopecia](/conditions/scarring-alopecia/), and the two can coexist with traction.

## Causes and triggers

Constant pulling inflames the follicle. If it continues, the follicle shrinks and is replaced by scar tissue. Risk rises with tightness, weight and duration: extensions added to braids, long heavy locs, styles kept for many weeks, and re-braiding the same pattern each time. Chemically relaxed hair combined with tension carries the highest risk. Children whose hair is braided tightly from an early age can show loss by their teens.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - your hairline has moved back or your edges are thinner
> - a hairstyle causes pain, bumps or sores
> - hair has not started to return three months after you loosened your styles
> - there is itching, burning or loss on the crown as well

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Tight styles on a hairline that is already thin
> - Edge-control gels and brushing the edges flat every day
> - Glue, tape and tight bands on the hairline
> - Painkillers to tolerate a hairstyle; have it loosened or taken out
> - Unproven "growth" oils and serums containing unlabelled steroids

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The dermatologist examines the pattern of loss and looks at the scalp with a magnifier ([trichoscopy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/trichoscopy/)) to see whether follicle openings are still present. A small scalp biopsy is taken if another scarring condition is suspected.

## Traction alopecia treatment at the clinic

1. **Remove the tension.** Loose, larger braids without added hair at the hairline; styles changed every few weeks; rest periods between installs; a satin scarf at night. This step matters more than any medicine.
2. **Settle inflammation** with a prescribed scalp lotion or small injections where the scalp is tender or bumpy.
3. **Stimulate regrowth** with topical minoxidil used daily for at least six months.
4. **[PRP for hair loss](/services/hair-and-scalp/prp-for-hair-loss/)** may be discussed for early, non-scarred thinning; evidence is still developing.
5. **[Hair transplant](/services/hair-and-scalp/hair-transplant/)** can restore a scarred hairline once the loss has been stable and traction has stopped.

## Prevention and daily care

- Ask your stylist to braid loosely at the front and leave the finest hairline hairs out.
- Keep braids and weaves in for no longer than six to eight weeks.
- Alternate styles so the pull moves.
- Teach children that braids should never hurt.

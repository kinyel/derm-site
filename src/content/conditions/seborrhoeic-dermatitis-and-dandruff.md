## What it looks like on brown and Black skin

On the scalp, flakes may be loose and white or stuck down in greasy, yellowish plates, most often along the front hairline, the crown and behind the ears. The scalp underneath can look darker or greyish, not red.

On the face and body, the picture is distinctive in dark skin:

- **Pale patches with fine scale** in the folds beside the nose, between and through the eyebrows, on the forehead near the hairline and in the beard area.
- **Petal-shaped or ring-like patches** on the central face or chest, sometimes mistaken for ringworm.
- **Darker, scaly patches** in the centre of the chest and upper back.
- **Scale and soreness in and behind the ears.**

The pale patches worry people because they resemble vitiligo. Unlike vitiligo they are not chalk-white, they have scale, and the pigment returns once the inflammation is treated. See [What is this white patch on my skin?](/blog/what-is-this-white-patch-on-my-skin/)

## How to spot it

- Flakes on the shoulders and itching that improve after washing and return within days
- Scale concentrated at the hairline, eyebrows, nose folds, ears or mid-chest
- Worse with stress, illness, heat or long gaps between washes
- Better after a medicated shampoo

Thick, silvery scale extending beyond the hairline may be [psoriasis](/conditions/psoriasis/). In a child, scale with broken hairs or bald patches should be tested for [scalp ringworm](/conditions/ringworm-and-fungal-skin-infections/).

## Causes and triggers

*Malassezia* yeast feeds on skin oil and releases substances that irritate the skin of susceptible people. The skin responds with faster cell turnover, which shows as scale.

Things that make it worse:

- **Infrequent washing** under braids, weaves, wigs and locs
- **Oils, greases and pomades** applied to the scalp, including shea butter and coconut oil; the yeast feeds on them
- **Heat, sweat and humidity**
- **Stress, poor sleep and illness**
- **Weakened immunity.** Sudden, severe or widespread seborrhoeic dermatitis in an adult is a reason for a general health check, including an HIV test with consent.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - four weeks of medicated shampoo has not helped
> - the scalp is sore, weeping or crusted
> - hair is thinning or breaking in the affected area
> - scale is thick and stuck down, or extends onto the forehead and neck
> - patches on the face are spreading or losing colour
> - the condition began suddenly and severely

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Greasing the scalp to "treat" flakes
> - Scratching with nails, combs or rat-tail combs
> - Leaving braids or weaves in for more than six to eight weeks without washing the scalp
> - Strong steroid lotions or mixed creams used for weeks on the face
> - Undiluted vinegar, lime, alcohol or antiseptic on the scalp

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The diagnosis is usually made by examining the scalp and skin, with [trichoscopy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/trichoscopy/) where hair loss is a concern. A [skin scraping or hair sample](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/fungal-tests/) rules out ringworm when the picture is unclear, and that test is routine for children with a scaly scalp.

## Dandruff treatment at the clinic

**Medicated shampoo** is the main treatment. The dermatologist recommends one, or a rotation of two with different active ingredients, and a schedule that fits your hair type and style. For tightly curled hair, the shampoo is applied to the scalp along partings and the lengths are conditioned afterwards to prevent dryness and breakage.

**For thick scale,** a softening preparation is applied before washing so that plaques lift without pulling out hair.

**For itch and inflammation,** a steroid scalp lotion or foam is used for a short, defined period.

**For the face and chest,** an antifungal cream, sometimes with a mild anti-inflammatory cream for a week or two. Non-steroid creams are used where repeated treatment is needed.

**Maintenance.** Seborrhoeic dermatitis returns when treatment stops. A medicated wash every one to two weeks keeps most people clear.

Treatments are dispensed by the clinic's [pharmacy](/services/dermatology-pharmacy/). Scalp care is part of [hair and scalp](/services/hair-and-scalp/) at the clinic.

## Prevention and daily care

- Wash the scalp every one to two weeks, more often in hot weather or after exercise.
- With braids or locs, dilute shampoo in an applicator bottle to reach the scalp, and rinse thoroughly.
- Dry the scalp fully before covering it with a wig, scarf or bonnet.
- Put oils on the hair lengths, not the scalp.
- Wash wig caps, scarves and pillowcases weekly.
- Read [Why does my scalp itch after braiding?](/blog/why-does-my-scalp-itch-after-braiding/) for other causes of itch.

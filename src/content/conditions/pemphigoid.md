## What pemphigoid looks like on brown and Black skin

Early on, there may be nothing but scratch marks and raised, darker, itchy patches that resemble [hives](/conditions/hives/) or [eczema](/conditions/eczema/). Then dome-shaped blisters, one to several centimetres across and firm to the touch, rise on darker, swollen skin. They last for days before breaking, leaving raw areas that crust and heal with dark or light marks but no scars.

## How to spot it

- The person is elderly.
- Itch has been severe for weeks.
- Blisters are tense, not flimsy.
- A new medicine was started in the months before.

Itch in an older person living with others who itch should also raise the question of [scabies](/conditions/scabies/).

## Causes and triggers

Antibodies bind to proteins at the junction of the epidermis and dermis, and the resulting inflammation lifts the surface as a blister.

> [!URGENT] Seek same-day care if
> - blisters are widespread, or the person is unwell, feverish or not eating and drinking
> - broken blisters look infected

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - an older person has persistent severe itching
> - blisters appear without a burn or injury

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Herbal remedies and powders on blisters
> - Removing blister roofs
> - Adhesive tape directly on fragile skin
> - Stopping steroid treatment abruptly

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

[Skin biopsies](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/) for routine examination and immunofluorescence confirm the diagnosis. Current medicines are reviewed, and baseline blood tests arranged.

## Pemphigoid treatment at the clinic

For limited or moderate disease, a very potent steroid cream applied to the whole affected area is effective and safer than tablets in frail people. Extensive disease is treated with steroid tablets and additional medicines. Blisters are drained and dressed. Blood pressure, sugar and bone protection are managed with the [internal medicine](/services/internal-medicine/) team, and carers are shown how to apply treatment.

## Prevention and daily care

- Moisturise and protect the skin from knocks.
- Keep nails short.
- Bring an up-to-date list of medicines to each visit.

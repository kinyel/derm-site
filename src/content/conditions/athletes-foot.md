## What athlete's foot looks like

There are three patterns:

- **Between the toes:** the skin turns white, soft and soggy, then peels and splits. It itches and may smell. The space between the fourth and little toes is usually first.
- **Dry "moccasin" type:** fine, powdery scaling over the sole, heel and sides of one or both feet, with little itch. It is often mistaken for dry skin and goes untreated for years.
- **Blistering type:** crops of small, itchy blisters on the instep.

On dark skin, the scale looks white or grey against the brown of the foot, and long-standing infection darkens and thickens the skin. Check the toenails and the palms: fungal infection of "two feet and one hand" is a classic pattern.

## How to spot it

- Peeling is worst between the outer toes.
- It itches most when you take off your shoes.
- Moisturiser does not clear the scaling.
- One or more toenails are thick or discoloured.

Deep painful cracks at the heel edge are [cracked heels](/conditions/cracked-heels/).

## Causes and triggers

Dermatophyte fungi live on keratin in the outer skin and spread on shed skin flakes left on floors, mats and in shoes. Heat, sweat and occlusion let them grow. Rainy-season wading and long days in closed work shoes are typical triggers in Lagos.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - a pharmacy antifungal has not cleared it in four weeks
> - the foot is red, hot, swollen or painful
> - you have diabetes or poor circulation
> - the rash is spreading to the hands, groin or nails
> - there are blisters or open sores

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Steroid or "triple action" creams, which spread the fungus under a calmer-looking surface
> - Bleach, kerosene or battery fluid soaks
> - Sharing slippers, shoes and towels
> - Wearing the same closed shoes two days running

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

A skin scraping is examined under the microscope and cultured ([fungal tests](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/fungal-tests/)). A lamp examination helps identify bacterial infection between the toes, which needs antibiotics, not antifungals.

## Athlete's foot treatment at the clinic

- **Antifungal cream** applied to all toe webs and the soles of both feet for the full course.
- **Tablets** for the dry moccasin type, blistering infection, or when nails are involved.
- **Drying agents** for soggy toe webs.
- **[Fungal nail infection](/conditions/fungal-nail-infection/)** treated at the same time.

Foot conditions are managed within [foot skin care](/services/foot-skin-care/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Dry between each toe after bathing and after ablution.
- Wear sandals when you can; choose cotton socks and change them daily.
- Alternate shoes and air them in the sun.
- Wear slippers in shared bathrooms and changing rooms.

## What it looks like on African hair

The change is diffuse: no bald patch, but a crown where the scalp shows through, and a parting that widens like the branches of a tree towards the front. Individual hairs become finer, so braids look thinner and styles hold less volume. Tightly coiled hair can hide early thinning, so many women first notice it in photographs taken from above or under salon lights.

In women of African descent, crown thinning has another important cause, central centrifugal cicatricial alopecia, a [scarring alopecia](/conditions/scarring-alopecia/) that starts at the vertex and spreads outwards, often with tenderness or itching. Telling the two apart early matters, because scarring loss is permanent.

## How to spot it

- Compare the width of your parting at the crown with the back of the head.
- Thinning has crept on over years, without clumps falling.
- Mother, aunts or grandmothers had similar thinning.
- There is no pain, scale or broken hair.

Sudden heavy shedding all over points to [telogen effluvium](/conditions/telogen-effluvium/); loss at the temples to [traction alopecia](/conditions/traction-alopecia/).

## Causes and triggers

Genes set the sensitivity of crown follicles. Each cycle, affected follicles spend less time growing and produce a thinner hair. Lower oestrogen after menopause, raised androgens, low iron, thyroid disease and rapid weight change can each make existing thinning more obvious.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - your parting is widening or your crown is thinning
> - thinning comes with irregular periods, acne or new facial hair
> - the crown itches, burns or is tender
> - you have used hair growth products for six months without benefit

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Tight styles and heavy extensions on thinning hair
> - Supplements in high doses without a proven deficiency
> - Stopping and starting treatment; gains are lost within months
> - Scalp "treatments" of unknown content

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Examination with [trichoscopy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/trichoscopy/) shows hairs of varying thickness on the crown compared with the back of the scalp. Blood tests are arranged where relevant, and a scalp biopsy is done if scarring is suspected.

## Female pattern hair loss treatment at the clinic

- **Topical minoxidil,** applied to the scalp daily, is the first step.
- **Tablets** that act on hormones or on hair growth may be prescribed after a discussion of benefits, side effects and pregnancy plans.
- **Correction of contributing factors,** such as low iron.
- **[PRP for hair loss](/services/hair-and-scalp/prp-for-hair-loss/)** as an addition for some women; studies show benefit in some patients and the evidence continues to develop.
- **[Hair transplant](/services/hair-and-scalp/hair-transplant/)** where the donor hair at the back is strong.
- **Styling advice** and fibres or toppers for coverage that does not pull.

Progress is tracked with photographs every few months within the [hair and scalp](/services/hair-and-scalp/) service.

## Prevention and daily care

- Start treatment early; keeping hair is easier than regrowing it.
- Handle hair gently and keep styles loose.
- Eat enough protein and iron-rich foods.
- Review treatment yearly with your dermatologist.

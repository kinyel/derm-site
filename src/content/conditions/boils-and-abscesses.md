## What boils look like on brown and Black skin

A boil begins as a sore, firm bump around a hair. Over two to four days it swells, becomes hot and very tender, and softens in the centre as pus collects. On dark skin the surrounding redness shows as a deeper brown, purple or dusky area, and warmth and tenderness are more reliable signs than colour. After healing, a dark mark or a small pitted scar often remains.

Common sites are the buttocks, thighs, armpits, groin, neck, face and beard area. A **carbuncle** is a group of connected boils, usually on the back of the neck or back, with several openings; it is more serious and more frequent in people with diabetes.

Lumps that look like boils but are not:

- an inflamed cyst, which has been present as a painless lump for months
- **hidradenitis suppurativa:** repeated deep lumps in the armpits, groin, buttocks or under the breasts that leave tunnels and rope-like scars
- acne cysts on the face, chest and back
- an infected [ingrown hair](/conditions/ingrown-hairs/)

## How to spot it

- It came up over days, not months.
- It is painful to touch and warm.
- A soft, pale centre is forming.
- You may feel feverish.

## Causes and triggers

Staphylococcus aureus enters through a hair follicle or a small break in the skin. Heat, sweat and friction from tight clothes in Lagos help it along, as do scratching and shaving. High blood sugar impairs the white cells that clear bacteria.

> [!URGENT] Seek medical care the same day if
> - redness or swelling is spreading quickly, or red streaks appear
> - you have a fever, chills or feel very unwell
> - the boil is on the face, nose, spine or near the eye
> - you have diabetes or weak immunity

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - a boil is larger than a groundnut, or has not improved in a week
> - you have had three or more boils in a year
> - lumps recur in the armpits or groin and leave scars

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Squeezing, or piercing with a pin or blade
> - Herbal poultices, toothpaste or hot objects on the lump
> - Leftover or shared antibiotics
> - Steroid or mixed creams

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The doctor examines the lump and takes a swab of pus to identify the bacteria and the antibiotics that work. For recurrent boils, swabs from the nose and folds are taken and blood sugar is checked, with referral to the [diabetes service](/services/internal-medicine/diabetes/) if raised.

## Boil treatment at the clinic

- **Drainage.** Under local anaesthetic, a small cut releases the pus; the cavity is cleaned and dressed. Relief is quick.
- **Antibiotics** where indicated.
- **Stopping recurrence.** An antiseptic body wash and a nasal ointment for a set number of days for you and, sometimes, the household; attention to shaving and clothing; treatment of itchy skin conditions.
- **Cysts** that have been infected are removed once settled ([cyst and lipoma removal](/services/skin-surgery/cyst-and-lipoma-removal/)).
- **Hidradenitis suppurativa** is managed long term with medical treatment.

## Prevention and daily care

- Wash daily with soap and dry folds well.
- Do not share towels, razors or sponges.
- Wear loose, breathable clothing.
- Keep diabetes under control.

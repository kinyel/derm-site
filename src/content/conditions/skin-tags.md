## What skin tags look like on brown and Black skin

A skin tag is a soft, smooth or slightly wrinkled bit of skin that hangs from a thin stalk. On dark skin, tags are usually the same colour as the skin or a shade darker. Most are 2 to 5 millimetres across; a few grow to the size of a grape.

They cluster where skin rubs on skin, clothing or jewellery: the sides of the neck, underarms, eyelids, under the breasts, the groin and the folds of the buttocks.

They are easily confused with other growths that are common on dark skin:

- **[Dermatosis papulosa nigra](/conditions/dermatosis-papulosa-nigra/):** small, dark, slightly raised spots on the cheeks and temples, with a rough surface and no stalk.
- **[Moles](/conditions/moles/):** usually firmer, with a broader base.
- **[Warts](/conditions/warts/):** rough, firm, and can spread; in the genital area they need a different approach.
- **Neurofibromas:** soft lumps that push back into the skin when pressed.

## How to spot it

- It is soft and you can wiggle it on its stalk
- It is painless unless a necklace, collar, bra strap or razor catches it
- It has been there for months or years without changing
- You have several, in the folds described above

A tag that suddenly turns dark, swollen and painful has usually twisted on its stalk and lost its blood supply. It will often drop off, but it can be removed if sore.

## Causes and triggers

Tags form where skin is repeatedly rubbed. They are more common with:

- **Excess weight,** which creates more skin folds
- **Insulin resistance and [type 2 diabetes](/services/internal-medicine/diabetes/).** High insulin levels stimulate skin growth. Many tags together with [dark, velvety skin on the neck](/conditions/dark-underarms-inner-thighs-knees-and-neck/) are a recognised sign.
- **Pregnancy**
- **Age,** from the forties onwards
- **Family tendency**
- **Chains, tight collars and rough fabrics**

They are not contagious and are not caused by poor hygiene.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - a growth is bleeding, painful, firm or changing
> - you are not sure it is a skin tag
> - tags are on the eyelids or genital area
> - you have many tags and have never had your blood sugar checked
> - tags catch on clothing or jewellery, or you want them removed

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Tying tags with thread, hair or dental floss
> - Cutting with blades, scissors or nail clippers
> - Removal creams, acids and "wart pens" bought online
> - Removal at salons and roadside stalls, where instruments are not sterile
> - Removing tags near the eye yourself

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

A dermatologist recognises skin tags on sight and uses a dermatoscope if a growth looks unusual. Anything doubtful is sent for laboratory examination after removal. If you have many tags, your weight, blood pressure and blood sugar may be checked, with the clinic's [internal medicine service](/services/internal-medicine/) where needed. Read [Diabetes and your skin](/blog/diabetes-and-your-skin/).

## Skin tag removal at the clinic

Removal is quick and done in the consulting room:

- **Snip excision.** The stalk is cut with fine sterile scissors. Small tags need no anaesthetic; larger ones are numbed first. Bleeding is stopped with light pressure or cautery.
- **Electrocautery.** A fine heated tip removes the tag and seals the base. Useful for many small tags.
- **Cryotherapy.** Freezing with liquid nitrogen. Used less often on dark skin because it can leave a pale mark.

**On brown and Black skin** the aim is the least injury to surrounding skin, since inflammation leaves dark marks. Snipping and fine cautery give the cleanest result. A small dark or pale dot at each site usually fades over weeks to months. Keloids from tag removal are rare, but tell the doctor if you form them.

Removal is part of [skin surgery and minor procedures](/services/skin-surgery/) at the clinic.

**Aftercare.** Keep the sites clean and dry for 24 hours, then wash normally. Apply petroleum jelly for a few days. Avoid necklaces and tight collars until healed, and protect the area from sun.

## Prevention and daily care

- Reduce friction: looser collars, no heavy chains, well-fitting bras.
- Work towards a healthy weight.
- Have blood sugar checked if you have many tags or a family history of diabetes.
- Do not pull or twist tags.

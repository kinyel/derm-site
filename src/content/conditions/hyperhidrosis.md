## What hyperhidrosis looks like

The skin itself looks normal; the sweat is the sign. Palms may glisten or drip, leaving damp patches on paper and screens. Underarms soak shirts within minutes of dressing. Feet slide in sandals, and the skin between the toes turns white, soft and smelly. On the face and scalp, sweat runs during meals or mild exertion.

Constantly wet skin invites other conditions: [athlete's foot](/conditions/athletes-foot/), pitted soles with strong odour, fungal rashes in folds, and friction darkening of the underarms.

## How to spot it

- Sweating has troubled you for more than six months.
- It affects both sides equally.
- It began before the age of 25.
- You do not sweat in your sleep.
- A relative has the same.
- It disrupts work, school or social life at least once a week.

## Causes and triggers

In primary hyperhidrosis the sweat glands are normal but the nerves that control them are overactive. Heat, emotion and exercise trigger it, and worry about sweating feeds the cycle. Secondary hyperhidrosis is caused by another condition or a medicine, and usually affects the whole body.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - sweating affects your work, studies or confidence
> - ordinary antiperspirants do not help
> - sweating started suddenly in adult life or occurs at night
> - you also have weight loss, fever, palpitations or tremor

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Lime, alum, bleach or methylated spirit on the skin
> - Layering perfume over sweat; it irritates and does not reduce it
> - Tight synthetic clothing and closed plastic shoes
> - Buying tablets for sweating without a medical review

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The pattern of sweating in your history is usually enough. Where sweating is generalised or new, blood tests and a medical review are arranged with the [internal medicine service](/services/internal-medicine/general-medical-consultation/).

## Hyperhidrosis treatment at the clinic

- **Prescription antiperspirant** applied at night to dry skin, then washed off in the morning; once controlled, used once or twice a week.
- **Iontophoresis** for hands and feet: sessions in which a mild electrical current is passed through water.
- **[Botulinum toxin](/services/aesthetic-treatments/botox/)** for underarms, and for palms or soles in selected patients.
- **Tablets** that reduce sweating all over, balanced against a dry mouth and other effects.
- **Skin care** for the infections and odour that follow wetness.

Treatment is part of [clinical dermatology](/services/clinical-dermatology/) at the clinic.

## Prevention and daily care

- Wear loose cotton or linen and carry a spare shirt.
- Alternate shoes so each pair dries for a day; choose leather or canvas with cotton socks.
- Apply antiperspirant at bedtime, when sweat glands are least active.

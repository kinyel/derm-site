## What vitiligo looks like on brown and Black skin

Vitiligo patches are completely white, like milk or chalk, with sharp borders and a smooth, normal surface. There is no scale and no itch in most people. On dark skin the contrast is striking, which is why vitiligo is noticed early and why it can cause such distress.

Typical sites:

- around the eyes, nostrils and mouth
- fingers, backs of the hands, wrists and elbows
- knees, shins, ankles and feet
- underarms, groin and genitals
- the waistline and other places where clothing rubs

Hair growing within a patch may turn white. Patches often appear in a mirror image on both sides of the body. A less common form, segmental vitiligo, affects one band on one side, starts young, spreads for a year or so and then stops.

Not every pale patch is vitiligo. Pale, slightly scaly patches are more often [pityriasis versicolor](/conditions/pityriasis-versicolor/), [seborrhoeic dermatitis](/conditions/seborrhoeic-dermatitis-and-dandruff/), pityriasis alba in children, or [marks left by a healed rash](/conditions/post-inflammatory-hypopigmentation/). Our article [What is this white patch on my skin?](/blog/what-is-this-white-patch-on-my-skin/) explains the differences.

## How to spot it

- The patch is pure white, not merely lighter than your skin
- Its edge is clearly defined
- The surface is smooth with normal feeling when touched
- Patches are in the typical places listed above
- New white patches appear where skin was cut, burned or rubbed

## Causes and triggers

Vitiligo is an autoimmune condition. It is associated with other autoimmune conditions in the person or the family, most often thyroid disease, and also [alopecia areata](/conditions/alopecia-areata/), type 1 diabetes and pernicious anaemia.

Triggers for new patches include skin injury and friction (the Koebner response), severe sunburn, contact with certain chemicals including some hair dyes and rubber, and emotional or physical stress.

White patches after long use of lightening creams are a separate problem; see [skin damage from bleaching and steroid creams](/conditions/skin-damage-from-bleaching-and-steroid-creams/).

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - you have a new white patch and are not sure what it is
> - patches are spreading or new ones are appearing
> - a child has white patches
> - a white patch is numb, raised or scaly
> - vitiligo is affecting your mood, work or relationships

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Herbal pastes, battery fluid and caustic "repigmenting" mixtures, which burn the skin and trigger more patches
> - Tattooing over patches
> - Tight straps, belts and jewellery that rub
> - Sunburn on white patches, which have no natural protection
> - Stopping treatment after a few weeks; repigmentation is slow

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The dermatologist usually makes the diagnosis by examination. A Wood's lamp makes vitiligo glow bright white and shows patches that are hard to see, and it helps separate vitiligo from other pale patches. A [skin scraping](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/fungal-tests/) rules out fungal causes, and a [biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/) is taken only when the diagnosis is uncertain. Blood tests check thyroid function. Photographs record the patches so that change can be measured.

## Vitiligo treatment at the clinic

The plan depends on how much skin is affected, whether the vitiligo is active, where the patches are and what you want.

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| A few patches | Prescribed steroid cream in cycles, or a calcineurin inhibitor cream on the face and folds |
| Many patches, or spreading | [Narrowband UVB phototherapy](/services/clinical-dermatology/phototherapy/) two to three times a week, with creams |
| Small stubborn patches | Targeted handheld or excimer light |
| Rapid spread | A short course of steroid tablets in pulses, to halt activity |
| Stable for a year or more, not responding | Surgical grafting of pigment cells, by referral where suitable |
| Any stage | Sunscreen on white skin; cosmetic camouflage if wanted |

**What to expect.** Colour returns as small brown dots around hair follicles that spread and merge, or from the edges inward. The face and neck respond in most people; fingers, toes, lips and patches with white hair respond least. A fair trial of phototherapy is six months or more.

**Emotional support.** Stigma and misunderstanding about vitiligo remain common in Nigeria. The clinic takes the psychological impact seriously, and treatment is offered for that reason as much as any other.

## Prevention and daily care

- Protect white patches with sunscreen and clothing; they burn easily.
- Avoid cuts, scrapes and friction where you can.
- Use treatment exactly as planned and attend phototherapy regularly.
- Have thyroid function checked as advised.
- Cosmetic camouflage and self-tanning products can cover patches safely.
- Tell friends and family the facts: it is not contagious and not caused by food.

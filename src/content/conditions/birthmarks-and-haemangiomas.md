## What birthmarks look like on brown and Black skin

**Pigmented birthmarks**

- *Congenital dermal melanocytosis:* flat, blue-grey or slate patches on the lower back, buttocks and sometimes shoulders.
- *Café-au-lait patches:* flat, darker brown, oval patches. One or two are common; six or more needs assessment.
- *Congenital moles:* brown-black, sometimes hairy. Large ones are monitored; see [moles](/conditions/moles/).
- *Naevus of Ota:* blue-grey pigmentation around one eye and temple.

**Vascular birthmarks**

- *Infantile haemangioma ("strawberry mark"):* appears in the first weeks, grows for three to nine months, then slowly flattens. On dark skin it is deep red, purple or dusky; deeper ones look like a bluish swelling.
- *Port-wine stain:* a flat patch, present at birth, pink-purple and harder to see on dark skin, that grows with the child and does not fade.
- *Salmon patches* at the nape or eyelids are faint and usually fade.

## How to spot what needs attention

- Is it growing faster than the baby?
- Is it near the eye, mouth, nose or nappy area?
- Is the surface breaking down?
- Are there many marks?

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist soon if
> - a haemangioma is near the eye, lips, nose or nappy area, or is growing quickly
> - a birthmark is bleeding or ulcerated
> - there are six or more light-brown patches, or five or more haemangiomas
> - a flat purple patch covers part of the face
> - a large or very dark mole is present from birth
> - a mark lies over the lower spine with a dimple or tuft of hair

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Herbal applications, hot compresses or scarification
> - Lightening creams
> - Waiting beyond the first months for a problem haemangioma

## Causes and triggers

Birthmarks arise during development and are not caused by anything the mother did, ate or saw during pregnancy.

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Most are identified by examination and dermoscopy. Photographs track change. An ultrasound or other scan is arranged for deep or multiple haemangiomas and marks over the spine.

## Treatment at the clinic

Many need only reassurance. Haemangiomas that threaten function or are likely to scar are treated with a beta-blocker medicine, by mouth or as a gel, with monitoring. Port-wine stains and residual vessels are treated with [vascular laser](/services/laser-treatments/laser-vascular-treatment/). Babies are seen within [children's skin care](/services/childrens-skin-care/), with referral to paediatric colleagues when needed.

## Prevention and daily care

- Photograph the mark monthly beside a ruler.
- Protect ulcer-prone haemangiomas from friction and keep the nappy area clean and dry.
- Use sun protection on pigmented marks.

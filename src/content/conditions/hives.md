## What hives look like on brown and Black skin

On dark skin, weals are raised, smooth swellings the same colour as the skin or slightly darker or paler; the redness seen on light skin is often absent. They are easier to feel than to see and may be outlined by a faint halo. They vary from pinhead size to large, map-like patches, itch fiercely, and move: a weal on the arm in the morning is gone by evening and another has appeared on the thigh.

Deeper swelling, called angioedema, puffs up the lips, eyelids, hands, feet or genitals and feels tight or sore more than itchy. It takes up to three days to settle.

Hives do not leave marks. Weals that stay in one place for more than a day, hurt, or leave bruises or dark stains suggest a different condition and need a [skin biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/).

## How to spot it

- Draw around a weal with a pen. If it has gone or moved by the next day, it is hives.
- Stroke the forearm firmly with a blunt point; a raised line within minutes indicates dermographism, a common physical type.
- Note whether weals follow heat, exercise, cold water, pressure from straps, or sunlight.

Small prickly bumps in sweaty areas that last for days are more likely [heat rash](/conditions/heat-rash/).

## Causes and triggers

Hives occur when mast cells in the skin release histamine. Acute attacks follow infections (the commonest cause in children), medicines, foods and stings. Physical types are set off by scratching, pressure, heat and sweating (frequent in Lagos), cold, or sun. Chronic spontaneous urticaria is often autoimmune and can be linked with thyroid disease.

> [!URGENT] Call for emergency help or go to the nearest emergency department if
> - the tongue or throat swells, or the voice changes
> - breathing or swallowing is difficult
> - there is dizziness, fainting or collapse

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - hives continue for more than six weeks
> - antihistamines from the pharmacy do not control them
> - weals are painful, last longer than a day or leave marks
> - you have repeated swelling of the lips or face

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Anti-inflammatory painkillers, unless your doctor says otherwise
> - Steroid tablets or injections taken repeatedly for flares
> - Hot baths, tight clothing and alcohol during an attack
> - Cutting out many foods without evidence

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The diagnosis rests on your description, photographs of the weals and examination. Acute hives need no tests. In chronic hives, a small set of blood tests looks for inflammation and thyroid disease, arranged with the [internal medicine service](/services/internal-medicine/general-medical-consultation/) where appropriate. Physical triggers are tested in the clinic.

## Hives treatment at the clinic

- **A modern, non-drowsy antihistamine taken every day,** not only when weals appear. If the standard dose is not enough, the dermatologist may increase it.
- **Add-on medicines** for flares or for hives that do not respond.
- **A biologic injection** for chronic hives that resist antihistamines, where suitable.
- **Trigger advice** and a written plan for what to do if swelling affects the mouth or throat.

Follow-up can be by [online consultation](/services/dermatology-consultations/online-consultation/). Children are seen within [children's skin care](/services/childrens-skin-care/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Take treatment regularly until the dermatologist advises reducing it.
- Keep cool; wear loose cotton.
- Record attacks with dates and photographs.
- Tell every doctor and pharmacist about medicines that have caused hives.

## What pityriasis rosea looks like on brown and Black skin

On dark skin the patches are not rose-coloured. They are grey-brown, violet or darker than the surrounding skin, and often more raised and bumpy, sometimes centred on hair follicles. The herald patch is frequently mistaken for [ringworm](/conditions/ringworm-and-fungal-skin-infections/). The later patches line up along the ribs in a "fir tree" pattern on the back. In people of African descent the rash more often involves the neck, face and limbs, itches more, and leaves more noticeable marks.

## How to spot it

- One patch came first.
- Smaller oval patches followed one to two weeks later.
- Scale sits just inside the edge of each patch.
- You may have had a cold or sore throat beforehand.

## Causes and triggers

The cause is believed to be reactivation of human herpesvirus 6 or 7, viruses most people carry from childhood. It is not an allergy and is unrelated to genital herpes. Some medicines can cause a similar rash.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - the rash lasts longer than three months
> - it involves the palms, soles or mouth
> - itching is severe
> - you are pregnant
> - you have had unprotected sex with a new partner in recent months

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Antifungal or "triple action" creams for weeks
> - Hot baths and harsh soaps
> - Lightening creams on the marks

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The pattern is usually clear. A scraping excludes fungus ([fungal tests](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/fungal-tests/)), a blood test excludes syphilis where appropriate, and a [skin biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/) is taken only if the rash is unusual.

## Treatment at the clinic

No treatment shortens most cases. Itch is managed with moisturiser, a mild steroid cream and antihistamines. Severe, widespread rash may be offered [phototherapy](/services/clinical-dermatology/phototherapy/) or a short course of tablets. Pregnant women are reviewed with their obstetric team.

## Prevention and daily care

- Use lukewarm water and a gentle cleanser.
- Wear loose cotton.
- Use sunscreen on exposed patches so that marks fade evenly.

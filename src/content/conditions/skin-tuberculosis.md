## What skin tuberculosis looks like on brown and Black skin

- **Lupus vulgaris:** a dark brown to violet plaque, commonly on the face, buttock or a limb, that creeps outward over months to years. One edge heals with a thin, pale scar while another stays raised and active.
- **Tuberculosis verrucosa cutis:** a firm, rough, wart-like plaque on a hand, foot, knee or buttock.
- **Scrofuloderma:** a painless lump over a lymph node in the neck, armpit or groin, or over a bone, that softens, breaks through the skin and leaves puckered, bridged scars and draining openings.
- **Tuberculids:** crops of dark bumps or tender nodules on the limbs as an immune reaction to TB elsewhere.

## How to spot it

- A lesion has grown slowly for months.
- It is painless.
- Antifungal, steroid and antibiotic creams have not cleared it.
- You or a household member has had TB or a long-standing cough.

## Causes and triggers

Mycobacterium tuberculosis reaches the skin through the bloodstream, by spread from an infected lymph node or bone beneath, or by direct entry through broken skin.

> [!DERM] See a doctor if
> - a skin patch, ulcer or lump has lasted more than six weeks despite treatment
> - a neck lump is discharging
> - you have a cough for more than two weeks, weight loss, fever or night sweats

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Steroid creams and herbal pastes on a persistent lesion
> - Incomplete courses of antibiotics
> - Stopping TB medicines when the skin looks better

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

A [skin biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/) is examined under the microscope and tested for the TB bacterium by culture or molecular methods. A chest X-ray, sputum tests where there is cough, and HIV and blood sugar tests are arranged through the [infectious diseases](/services/internal-medicine/infectious-diseases/) and internal medicine services.

## Treatment at the clinic

The clinic confirms the diagnosis and coordinates treatment with the national TB programme, which supplies the medicines and follows patients to completion. Skin lesions are reviewed during treatment and wounds dressed. Scars can be assessed after cure.

## Prevention and daily care

- Complete BCG vaccination for babies as scheduled.
- Seek testing for any cough lasting more than two weeks.
- Take every dose of TB treatment for the full course.

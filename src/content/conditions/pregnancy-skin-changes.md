## What pregnancy does to brown and Black skin

**Pigment.** Nearly every pregnant woman with dark skin notices darkening. The neck and armpits may look almost black, and old scars and moles deepen in colour. [Melasma](/conditions/melasma/), brown patches on the cheeks, forehead, nose and upper lip, appears in the second half of pregnancy and is made worse by Lagos sun.

**Stretching.** [Stretch marks](/conditions/stretch-marks/) begin as dark or purplish lines and fade to lighter streaks. Genetics decide most of the risk.

**Oil and hair.** Acne can flare, mainly in the first half of pregnancy. Scalp hair feels thicker because fewer hairs are shed; they fall together a few months after birth ([telogen effluvium](/conditions/telogen-effluvium/)).

**Growths and vessels.** [Skin tags](/conditions/skin-tags/) appear on the neck and under the breasts. Gums bleed more easily, and palms may feel warm.

**Existing conditions.** Eczema commonly worsens; psoriasis often improves.

## Rashes specific to pregnancy

- **Atopic eruption of pregnancy:** itchy, dry, eczema-like patches or small bumps, usually starting before the third trimester. The commonest pregnancy rash, harmless to the baby.
- **Polymorphic eruption of pregnancy:** very itchy bumps and weals that begin in abdominal stretch marks late in a first pregnancy, sparing the navel. It clears after delivery and does not affect the baby.
- **Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy:** intense itch with no rash other than scratch marks. Needs blood tests and obstetric monitoring.
- **Pemphigoid gestationis:** rare. Itchy weals around the navel that become blisters. Needs specialist treatment.

## How to spot what needs attention

- Is the itch out of proportion, on the palms and soles, or keeping you awake?
- Are there blisters?
- Is the rash spreading quickly or accompanied by fever?
- Has a mole changed shape or bled?

> [!URGENT] Contact your obstetrician or doctor promptly if
> - you have severe itching without a rash, dark urine, pale stools or yellow eyes
> - blisters appear
> - a rash comes with fever, or you have been in contact with chickenpox or measles

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - itching or a rash is affecting your sleep
> - acne, eczema or psoriasis is flaring and you are unsure what is safe to use
> - dark patches are spreading on your face
> - a mole is changing

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Lightening, toning and "triple action" creams
> - Retinoids, including many anti-ageing and acne products
> - Herbal mixtures taken or applied without your doctor's knowledge
> - Hot baths for itching; they make it worse
> - Elective peels, lasers and injectables until after pregnancy

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The dermatologist examines the skin, reviews your pregnancy record and medicines, and coordinates with your obstetrician. Liver and bile acid blood tests are requested for unexplained itch, and a small skin sample is taken if a blistering condition is suspected.

## Treatment at the clinic

- **Itch and eczema:** fragrance-free moisturisers, cool compresses, and steroid creams of suitable strength, which are safe in pregnancy when used as prescribed.
- **Acne:** topical options with a good safety record in pregnancy.
- **Melasma:** shade, a tinted broad-spectrum sunscreen and patience; active treatment after delivery.
- **Specific pregnancy rashes:** treatment planned jointly with your obstetric team.

## Prevention and daily care

- Use sunscreen and a hat every day.
- Moisturise the abdomen, breasts and thighs to ease tightness and itch.
- Wear loose cotton clothing.
- Tell every clinician who treats you that you are pregnant.

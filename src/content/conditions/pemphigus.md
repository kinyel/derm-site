## What pemphigus looks like on brown and Black skin

Because blisters rupture almost at once, what is seen are shallow, raw, pink or pale areas where the dark surface layer has been lost, with a collar of loose skin at the edge, and dark crusts. Healed areas are left much darker for months, without scarring. In pemphigus foliaceus, the mouth is spared and the scalp, face and upper trunk are covered in crusted, scaly patches that resemble severe [seborrhoeic dermatitis](/conditions/seborrhoeic-dermatitis-and-dandruff/).

## How to spot it

- Mouth sores have lasted more than three weeks.
- New raw areas keep appearing on the scalp or trunk.
- Skin near a sore slides off when rubbed.

## Causes and triggers

Antibodies target proteins called desmogleins that hold skin cells together. Why this begins is unknown; some medicines can trigger it.

> [!URGENT] Seek urgent care if
> - large areas of skin are raw or blistered
> - you cannot eat or drink because of mouth pain
> - you have fever or the sores are infected

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - mouth ulcers persist beyond three weeks
> - you have recurrent blisters or erosions

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Herbal applications and antiseptic powders on raw skin
> - Bursting blisters
> - Spicy, acidic or rough foods while the mouth is sore
> - Stopping steroid tablets suddenly

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Two small [skin biopsies](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/) are taken: one for routine examination and one for immunofluorescence, which shows the antibodies. Blood tests are done before starting treatment.

## Pemphigus treatment at the clinic

Treatment begins with corticosteroid tablets to gain control, with a second medicine or rituximab to maintain it and allow the steroid dose to fall. Erosions are dressed with non-stick dressings, and infection treated. Blood pressure, sugar, bone health and infection risk are monitored with the [internal medicine](/services/internal-medicine/) team. Severe cases are referred for hospital care.

## Prevention and daily care

- Handle skin gently; wear soft, loose clothing.
- Use a soft toothbrush and mild mouthwash.
- Attend all monitoring visits.

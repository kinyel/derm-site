## What skin cancer looks like on brown and Black skin

**Squamous cell carcinoma** appears as a firm lump, a thick scaly patch, or an ulcer with a raised, rolled edge. On dark skin it most often arises on the legs, in scars, in chronic wounds, around the anus and genitals, and on sun-exposed skin in people with albinism.

**Melanoma** on dark skin favours the feet and hands. Look for a dark, irregular patch on the sole or heel, a patch between the toes, or a brown-black stripe along a nail. Later it thickens, ulcerates or bleeds.

**Basal cell carcinoma** is a slow-growing bump on the face or scalp. On dark skin it is usually pigmented: a shiny, dark brown or black bump with a smooth surface that may bleed or crust.

**Other skin cancers,** such as Kaposi sarcoma (purple-brown patches and lumps on the legs or in the mouth) and lymphoma of the skin (persistent pale or dark scaly patches that do not respond to treatment), are diagnosed by biopsy.

## How to spot it

Check once every few months, with a mirror or a partner:

- soles, heels and between the toes
- palms and all twenty nails
- old burn scars, vaccination scars and any long-standing wound
- scalp, lips, inside the mouth and the genital area

Anything growing, bleeding, changing colour or failing to heal in four to six weeks should be shown to a dermatologist. See [moles and when to worry](/conditions/moles/) for the ABCDE signs.

## Causes and triggers

Ultraviolet light is the main cause in light skin and in albinism. On dark skin, chronic scarring and inflammation, weakened immunity and certain viruses play a larger part, and the cause of acral melanoma is not known; it is not linked to sun or to injury.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist promptly if
> - a dark patch on your sole, palm or nail is new or changing
> - a sore has not healed after a month
> - a scar or old wound has changed
> - you have albinism and a rough, scaly or bleeding spot
> - a "wart" or "fungal nail" has not responded to treatment

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Herbal pastes, acids and burning applied to a growth
> - Repeated dressings for a non-healing ulcer without a biopsy
> - Waiting for pain; early skin cancer is painless

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

A full skin examination with a dermatoscope comes first ([mole mapping and dermoscopy](/services/skin-cancer-care/mole-mapping-and-dermoscopy/)). Any suspicious area has a [skin biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/) under local anaesthetic, and a pathologist's report confirms the type. Further scans or referrals are arranged if needed.

## Treatment at the clinic

Early cancers are removed with a margin of healthy skin, usually in one visit. Cancers on the face where tissue must be preserved may be suitable for [Mohs surgery](/services/skin-cancer-care/mohs-surgery/). The clinic coordinates with oncology and surgical teams when a cancer needs treatment beyond the skin. After treatment, regular checks continue within [skin cancer care](/services/skin-cancer-care/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Have non-healing wounds and changing scars assessed early.
- Use broad-spectrum sunscreen, hats and shade; essential in albinism. See [Do Black people need sunscreen?](/blog/do-black-people-need-sunscreen/)
- Look at your feet and nails when you cut your nails.
- Keep follow-up appointments after any skin cancer.

## What a diabetic foot ulcer looks like on brown and Black skin

The typical ulcer is a round, punched-out sore surrounded by a ring of thick, pale callus, under the ball of the foot, the big toe or the heel, or over a toe joint that rubs in the shoe. The base may be pink, yellow with slough, or black.

Signs of infection on dark skin are swelling, warmth compared with the other foot, a darker or dusky zone around the wound, increasing discharge, smell and a shiny, tight surface. Redness may not be obvious. Pain may be absent even when infection is serious, and a fever or unexplained rise in blood sugar can be the only signal.

Ulcers from poor circulation are different: on the tips of toes or the foot edge, painful, with cool, shiny, hairless skin.

## How to spot it

- You have diabetes and a break in the skin of your foot.
- The foot is numb, tingling or feels like cotton wool.
- A callus has a dark or wet patch within it.
- One foot is warmer or more swollen than the other.

## Causes and triggers

High blood sugar over years damages the nerves, so pressure, a tight shoe, a hot surface or a stone goes unfelt. It also narrows blood vessels and weakens the immune response. Dry, cracked skin and fungal infection between the toes give bacteria a way in. In Lagos, walking barefoot at home, hot-water soaks and ill-fitting shoes are common starting points.

> [!URGENT] Go to an emergency department or call your doctor the same day if
> - redness, darkening or swelling is spreading up the foot or leg
> - the foot is hot, very swollen or newly painful
> - there is pus, a foul smell, or black tissue
> - you have fever, chills, vomiting or feel confused
> - a toe or part of the foot has turned blue, grey or black

> [!DERM] See a doctor within days if
> - any sore, blister or crack on your foot has not healed in a week
> - you have a thick callus or a nail problem and cannot feel your feet

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Walking on the ulcer, or barefoot at any time
> - Hot water soaks, hot water bottles and heaters near the feet
> - Herbal dressings, engine oil, antiseptic powders and methylated spirit
> - Cutting calluses or corns yourself
> - Tight shoes and socks with tight tops

## How it is assessed at the clinic

The clinician measures and photographs the ulcer, probes its depth, checks sensation with a fine filament and feels the foot pulses. A swab or tissue sample guides antibiotics. Blood tests review sugar control and kidney function through the [diabetes service](/services/internal-medicine/diabetes/). An X-ray or scan is arranged if bone infection is suspected, and reduced circulation is referred to a vascular team.

## Diabetic foot ulcer treatment at the clinic

- **Offloading.** Pressure must come off the wound: a special shoe, insole or cast, crutches, and rest.
- **Debridement.** Callus and dead tissue are removed regularly so healthy tissue can grow.
- **Dressings** that keep the wound moist but not wet, changed on a schedule.
- **Infection control** with antibiotics chosen from culture results.
- **Whole-body care:** blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol, with the [internal medicine](/services/internal-medicine/) team.
- **Referral** without delay when surgery or vascular treatment is needed.

Ongoing dressing and review are provided within [wound care](/services/wound-care/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Check your feet daily and your shoes before every wear.
- Wash, dry between the toes, and moisturise the tops and soles, not between the toes.
- Wear well-fitting closed shoes with socks, indoors and out.
- Have nails and calluses managed by a trained professional.
- Attend a foot check at least once a year, more often if you have lost feeling.

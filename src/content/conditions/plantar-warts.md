## What plantar warts look like on brown and Black skin

A plantar wart is a well-defined disc of rough, grey-yellow skin set into the sole, often at the heel or ball of the foot. Callus builds up over it. After soaking and filing, dark specks show in the centre. Several may join into a "mosaic" plaque. The surrounding skin of a dark foot makes the paler, roughened wart stand out.

## How to spot it

- Squeezing the sides hurts more than pressing down.
- Fingerprint-like skin lines stop at its edge.
- Black dots appear on paring.

A hard area over a bony point with lines running through it is a [corn or callus](/conditions/corns-and-calluses/).

## Causes and triggers

HPV enters softened or broken skin of the sole, typically from wet floors in bathrooms, pools and changing rooms. Sweaty feet and walking barefoot raise the risk. See [warts](/conditions/warts/) for other sites.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - the wart is painful or spreading
> - home treatment has failed after twelve weeks
> - you have diabetes, numb feet or weak immunity
> - the lesion is dark, bleeding or changing

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Cutting or burning it yourself
> - Sharing slippers, files and pumice stones
> - Picking, which seeds new warts

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Paring and dermoscopy confirm a wart. Atypical lesions have a [skin biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/).

## Plantar wart treatment at the clinic

The dermatologist pares the wart and applies or prescribes treatment: high-strength salicylic acid under a plaster, cryotherapy at two to three week intervals, or other applied agents for resistant warts. Treatment is planned to limit pain and marks. See [foot skin care](/services/foot-skin-care/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Wear slippers in shared wet areas.
- Dry feet well; change socks daily.
- Cover a wart with a waterproof plaster when swimming.

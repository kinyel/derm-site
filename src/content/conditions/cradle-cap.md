## What cradle cap looks like on brown and Black skin

Scales are yellowish, greasy and waxy, lying in plates on the crown and front of the scalp. Underneath, the skin of a dark-skinned baby is often lighter than the surrounding scalp, or slightly pink-brown, and this pale colour remains for some weeks after the scale has gone before returning to normal. Parents sometimes mistake these pale areas for a fungal infection or [vitiligo patches](/conditions/vitiligo/); they are neither.

The same condition can spread to the eyebrows, forehead, creases beside the nose, behind the ears, neck folds, armpits and nappy area as lighter, slightly scaly patches. The baby remains content.

## How to spot it

- It began in the first weeks of life.
- Scales are oily, not dry and powdery.
- The baby does not scratch.
- Sleep and feeding are normal.

An itchy baby with dry cheeks and body more likely has [baby eczema](/conditions/baby-eczema/), and the two can occur together.

## Causes and triggers

Hormones passed from mother to baby before birth keep the baby's oil glands active for some months. A yeast that lives on oily skin, Malassezia, flourishes and provokes scaling. As the hormones fade, so does the condition. In older children and adults, the same process is called [seborrhoeic dermatitis and dandruff](/conditions/seborrhoeic-dermatitis-and-dandruff/).

> [!DERM] See a doctor if
> - the scalp is swollen, weeping, bleeding or smells
> - the rash is spreading over the face and body
> - the baby is itchy, unsettled or not gaining weight
> - it has not improved after several weeks of home care
> - there are patches of hair loss

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Picking or scraping dry scales
> - Olive oil and other cooking oils, which can irritate and may feed the yeast
> - Adult dandruff shampoos unless a doctor advises one
> - Medicated soaps, antiseptic liquids and "triple action" creams
> - Thick layers of shea butter or petroleum jelly left on the scalp for days

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The dermatologist diagnoses cradle cap by looking at the scalp and the rest of the skin. Tests are not needed unless infection or another condition is suspected.

## Cradle cap treatment

- **Soften, wash, brush** as described in the FAQ, daily at first, then two or three times a week.
- **Antifungal cream or shampoo** for stubborn or widespread scaling, used as directed.
- **A mild steroid cream** briefly if the skin is inflamed.
- **Reassurance** about the pale patches left behind.

The clinic sees babies with a parent within [children's skin care](/services/childrens-skin-care/); mild cases can be reviewed by [online consultation](/services/dermatology-consultations/online-consultation/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Wash the baby's hair two or three times a week with a mild shampoo.
- Brush the scalp gently with a soft baby brush after washing.
- Use only light products on the scalp.

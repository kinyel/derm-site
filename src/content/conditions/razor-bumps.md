## What razor bumps look like on brown and Black skin

Razor bumps are firm, skin-coloured to dark brown bumps, 2 to 5 millimetres across, centred on hair follicles. A curled hair is often visible looping into the bump. They appear on the neck, under the jaw, on the cheeks and along the beard line a day or two after shaving, and itch or sting.

On dark skin, what remains is as noticeable as the bumps themselves: a field of dark dots and patches across the shaved area. Where bumps recur for years, the neck and jawline can develop thick, raised scars and [keloid scars](/conditions/keloids/).

The same problem affects:

- the back of the scalp and neck after a close haircut; see [scalp folliculitis](/conditions/scalp-folliculitis/)
- the bikini line, underarms and legs
- the chin in women who pluck or shave

## How to spot it

- Bumps appear after shaving, waxing or plucking, not at random
- They are confined to the area where hair is removed
- You can see or feel a hair trapped in some bumps
- They improve when you stop shaving and return when you start
- Dark marks follow each crop

Bumps with pus that spread beyond the shaved area, or that do not improve when shaving stops, may be bacterial [folliculitis](/conditions/folliculitis/), [acne](/conditions/acne/) or a fungal infection, and need different treatment.

## Causes and triggers

Razor bumps are a reaction to the person's own hair, not an infection. Two mechanisms are involved:

1. **The hair re-enters the skin from outside.** A short, sharp, curved hair grows out, curls and pierces the skin a millimetre or two away.
2. **The hair pierces the follicle from inside.** When skin is stretched during a close shave, the cut hair retracts below the surface and its tip pushes through the follicle wall.

Things that make them more likely:

- multi-blade razors, which lift and cut the hair below skin level
- shaving against the direction of growth
- pulling the skin taut
- dry shaving and blunt blades
- plucking and waxing, which leave a sharp new hair inside the follicle
- shared or dirty clippers, which add infection

Jobs and uniforms that require a clean shave are a common reason men cannot simply grow a beard. A dermatologist can provide a medical letter where that is appropriate.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - bumps persist despite changing how you shave
> - they are painful, pus-filled or spreading
> - dark marks or raised scars are forming
> - bumps at the back of the scalp are becoming firm and joined together
> - you need a medical opinion for your workplace

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Digging out hairs with needles, pins or tweezers
> - Close shaving with a multi-blade razor
> - Alcohol-based aftershaves, methylated spirit and bleach
> - Steroid or mixed creams used for weeks; they thin the skin and cause more bumps
> - Lightening creams on the dark marks
> - Sharing clippers, or using a barber who does not disinfect them

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The diagnosis is made by examining the skin and seeing ingrown hairs, often with a dermatoscope. A swab is taken if there is pus, and a fungal test if the bumps do not fit the usual pattern. See [men's and women's skin health](/services/mens-and-womens-skin-health/).

## Razor bumps treatment at the clinic

**Step 1: change hair removal.** This is the treatment; everything else supports it.

- Best: grow a beard, or keep stubble with clippers at about 1 millimetre.
- If a shave is required: wash with warm water, use shaving gel, shave in the direction of growth with a single-blade razor, do not stretch the skin, do not go over the same area repeatedly, rinse and moisturise. Shave every two to three days at most.
- Depilatory creams dissolve hair bluntly and suit some people, but they irritate; test a small area first.

**Step 2: calm the skin.** The dermatologist may prescribe a retinoid to keep follicles open, benzoyl peroxide or an antibiotic lotion for inflamed or infected bumps, and a mild anti-inflammatory cream for a few days after shaving.

**Step 3: treat the marks.** Pigment-fading creams, sunscreen and a series of [chemical peels](/services/aesthetic-treatments/chemical-peels/) lighten dark spots once new bumps have stopped.

**Step 4: reduce the hair.** [Laser hair removal](/services/laser-treatments/laser-hair-removal/) with a long-wavelength laser suited to dark skin thins and reduces beard, neck or bikini hair. Clinical studies in men with dark skin show a marked fall in bumps after a course. Men who want to keep a beard can have only the neck and cheek line treated. Our article [Is laser hair removal safe on dark skin?](/blog/is-laser-hair-removal-safe-on-dark-skin/) explains the precautions.

**Raised scars and keloids** are treated with steroid injections; see [keloids](/conditions/keloids/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Never shave dry. Soften the hair with warm water for a few minutes first.
- Change blades often; clean clippers with disinfectant after every use.
- Each night, wash the beard area with a soft cloth in a circular motion to free hairs that are starting to turn in.
- Moisturise after shaving with a fragrance-free product.
- Use your own clippers at the barber.
- Wear sunscreen on areas with dark marks.

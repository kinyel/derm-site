## What stretch marks look like on brown and Black skin

Stretch marks pass through two stages.

**Early (striae rubrae).** Slightly raised lines that are dark brown, purple or violet on dark skin, sometimes itchy. At this stage the marks still have a blood supply and active repair, and they respond best to treatment.

**Late (striae albae).** After months, the lines flatten, narrow and turn paler than the surrounding skin, with a fine, crinkled surface. On dark skin these pale lines stand out, particularly after sun exposure tans the skin around them.

Common sites are the abdomen and breasts in pregnancy; hips, thighs, buttocks and lower back in teenagers; shoulders and upper arms in people who build muscle; and the inner arms, inner thighs and skin folds after steroid creams.

## How to spot it

- Several lines running in the same direction
- A slight dip when you run a finger across older marks
- They appeared during pregnancy, a growth spurt, or a change in weight or muscle
- No pain, and no change over weeks once formed

## Causes and triggers

The middle layer of skin, the dermis, contains collagen and elastin fibres that let it stretch and recoil. When skin is stretched faster than these fibres can adapt, they tear, and the skin heals with a scar-like band. Hormones matter as well: cortisol and related steroids weaken the fibres, which is why stretch marks are common in pregnancy and puberty and after steroid use.

Causes to know about:

- **Pregnancy,** mostly in the last three months
- **Puberty**
- **Weight change,** in either direction
- **Steroid creams and tablets,** including steroids hidden in [bleaching and mixed creams](/conditions/skin-damage-from-bleaching-and-steroid-creams/)
- **Medical conditions** such as Cushing's syndrome and some inherited connective tissue disorders

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - stretch marks appeared without pregnancy, growth or weight change
> - they are wide, purple and widespread, especially with easy bruising, a rounder face or weakness
> - they followed use of a lightening, "toning" or mixed cream
> - you want treatment while the marks are still new and coloured

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Lightening creams to "blend" stretch marks; they cause more
> - Retinoid creams in pregnancy or while breastfeeding
> - Dermarollers with long needles at home
> - Paying for creams that promise complete removal
> - Tanning to hide pale marks; the contrast increases

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Stretch marks are diagnosed by looking. The dermatologist asks about weight, pregnancy, medicines and creams. If the pattern suggests a hormonal cause, blood pressure is checked and blood tests are arranged with the clinic's [internal medicine service](/services/internal-medicine/).

## How to get rid of stretch marks: treatment options at the clinic

Treatment aims to improve colour and texture. Expect improvement, not disappearance, and expect a course of treatment over several months.

| Stage | Options |
|---|---|
| Early, coloured marks | Prescription retinoid cream (not in pregnancy or breastfeeding); [microneedling](/services/aesthetic-treatments/microneedling/); sun protection |
| Older, pale marks | Microneedling or radiofrequency microneedling; [fractional laser resurfacing](/services/laser-treatments/laser-skin-resurfacing/) after a test patch; [PRP](/services/aesthetic-treatments/prp-microneedling/) as an addition |
| Loose skin around the marks | [Skin tightening](/services/aesthetic-treatments/skin-tightening/) |

**On brown and Black skin,** needling-based treatments are preferred because they do not heat the surface and so carry a lower risk of dark marks. Laser is used cautiously, with preparation and a test area.

A typical course is three to six treatments, four to six weeks apart, with results judged three months after the last one. After pregnancy, treatment usually starts once breastfeeding has finished, although needling can be discussed earlier.

Choosing not to treat is reasonable. Stretch marks are a normal feature of skin and fade considerably with time.

## Prevention and daily care

- Aim for gradual weight change where possible.
- Moisturise during pregnancy for comfort and itch.
- Never use a steroid or lightening cream on the body without medical advice.
- Treat marks early if they bother you.
- Protect treated skin from the sun.

## What spider veins look like on brown and Black skin

Fine vessels are less obvious on dark skin and tend to look purple, blue-black or dark brown, most visible on the inner knee, behind the knee, at the ankle and on lighter areas such as the inner thigh. On the face, especially in people who have used steroid-containing creams, a net of thin vessels appears across the cheeks with shiny, thinned skin.

## How to spot it

- Lines blanch when pressed and refill.
- They are flat and painless.
- They are not raised cords (those are varicose veins).

## Causes and triggers

Inherited vessel wall weakness, pressure from standing, hormones in pregnancy, and damage to supporting skin from sun or steroids. Facial vessels are a feature of [rosacea](/conditions/rosacea/) and of [steroid cream damage](/conditions/skin-damage-from-bleaching-and-steroid-creams/).

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - you also have swollen, aching legs or darkening near the ankles
> - vessels appeared after using a cream on your face
> - a vessel bleeds, or a single red spot is growing
> - you would like them treated

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Lightening or steroid creams to hide them
> - Hot waxing over fragile areas
> - Treatment by operators without experience of dark skin

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Examination with dermoscopy; a vein scan through a vascular service if larger veins or symptoms are present.

## Spider vein treatment at the clinic

Facial and fine leg vessels can be treated with [vascular laser](/services/laser-treatments/laser-vascular-treatment/) after a test area. Larger leg vessels respond better to sclerotherapy, arranged with vascular colleagues. The cause is treated at the same time, and sun protection maintains the result.

## Prevention and daily care

- Move regularly; avoid long periods of standing still.
- Raise your legs when resting; consider support stockings in pregnancy.
- Use sunscreen on the face.

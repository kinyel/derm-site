## What it looks like on brown and Black skin

Ordinary scalp folliculitis is a scatter of small, itchy, dark bumps or yellow-headed spots, often along the hairline. Acne keloidalis nuchae begins as a few firm, dome-shaped bumps at the nape. Over months they multiply, harden and fuse into a band or plaque of smooth, dark, raised scar. Hairs within it are lost or emerge in tufts, and the area may ooze or smell when infected.

## How to spot it

- Bumps follow haircuts by a few days.
- They are at the back of the scalp and neck.
- Some feel hard, like small beads under the skin.
- The area itches or is tender when the collar rubs.

## Causes and triggers

Mechanical injury to curved follicles, ingrowing hairs, friction and bacteria combine. It is related to [razor bumps](/conditions/razor-bumps/) of the beard area and is not true acne.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - bumps at the nape persist for more than a month
> - they are becoming firm or joining together
> - there is pus, pain or hair loss
> - pus spots recur over the scalp

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Razor or zero-guard cuts on the nape
> - Picking or shaving over bumps
> - Methylated spirit and aftershave on broken skin
> - Tight collars, helmets and caps that rub
> - Unprescribed steroid creams for months

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Examination with [trichoscopy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/trichoscopy/); a swab if there is pus; a biopsy if another scarring condition is possible.

## Treatment at the clinic

Early disease: haircut changes, antiseptic shampoo, a combined steroid and retinoid or antibiotic lotion, and a course of tablets if inflamed. Firm bumps: steroid injections. Recurrent disease: [laser removal of hair](/services/laser-treatments/laser-hair-removal/) at the nape reduces the hairs that drive it. Large plaques: surgical removal, as described under [scar revision](/services/skin-surgery/scar-revision/). The service sits within [hair and scalp](/services/hair-and-scalp/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Keep hair at the nape slightly longer.
- Insist on disinfected clippers.
- Wash the scalp regularly, especially after sweating.

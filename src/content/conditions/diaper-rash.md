## What diaper rash looks like on brown and Black skin

On a baby with dark skin, the affected area is a deeper brown, purplish or shiny rather than bright red, and feels warm and slightly swollen. It covers the rounded surfaces that press against the nappy: buttocks, lower abdomen, genitals and upper thighs. The creases, which the nappy does not touch, are usually clear in a simple irritant rash.

As it heals, the skin may peel and look lighter or darker for a few weeks.

Patterns that suggest something else:

- **In the folds, with satellite spots:** yeast (Candida).
- **Greasy, salmon or dark patches in the folds with scalp scale:** infantile seborrhoeic dermatitis ([cradle cap](/conditions/cradle-cap/)).
- **Honey-coloured crusts or blisters:** bacterial infection ([impetigo](/conditions/impetigo/)).
- **A rash that never clears, with a rash elsewhere or poor weight gain:** needs specialist assessment.

## How to spot it

- The rash matches the area covered by the nappy.
- It is worse after diarrhoea or a long night.
- It improves when the nappy is left off.

## Causes and triggers

Urine raises the skin's pH and softens it; enzymes in stool then break down the surface. Friction and trapped heat finish the job, and Lagos humidity keeps the area damp. Fragranced wipes and antiseptic bath additives add irritation.

> [!DERM] See a doctor if
> - the rash has not improved after three to four days of good care
> - there are blisters, pus, open sores or bleeding
> - the baby has a fever or seems unwell
> - the rash is spreading beyond the nappy area
> - it keeps coming back

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Antiseptic liquids, medicated soaps and methylated spirit
> - "Triple action" and adult steroid creams
> - Talcum powder
> - Wipes containing alcohol or fragrance
> - Scrubbing off barrier cream at each change

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The doctor examines the whole baby, not only the nappy area, and may take a swab if infection is suspected. An [online consultation](/services/dermatology-consultations/online-consultation/) with clear photographs suits mild, recurring cases.

## Diaper rash treatment

1. **Air.** Nappy-free time on a towel or washable mat.
2. **Barrier.** A thick ointment at every change.
3. **Change** at least every three to four hours by day, and promptly after stool.
4. **Antifungal cream** if yeast is present, for the full course.
5. **A mild steroid** for a few days only when prescribed.

Babies are seen with a parent within [skin care for babies and children](/services/childrens-skin-care/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Fit nappies loosely enough for air to circulate.
- Dry the folds gently after each wash.
- Apply barrier cream routinely at night and during diarrhoea.
- Bathe once a day with plain water or a soap substitute.

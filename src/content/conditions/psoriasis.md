## What psoriasis looks like on brown and Black skin

The pink-red plaques shown in most photographs are how psoriasis looks on light skin. On brown and Black skin the plaques are violet, dark brown or grey, with scale that looks silvery-white or grey. They are sharply outlined and thicker than eczema.

Common patterns:

- **Plaque psoriasis:** raised patches on the elbows, knees, lower back and scalp. The most common form.
- **Scalp psoriasis:** thick scale that crosses the hairline onto the forehead, neck and around the ears.
- **Guttate psoriasis:** many small drop-like spots on the trunk, often two to three weeks after a sore throat, mainly in children and young adults.
- **Flexural psoriasis:** smooth, shiny, dark patches in the armpits, groin, under the breasts and between the buttocks, without much scale.
- **Palms and soles:** thick, cracked skin, sometimes with small pustules.
- **Nails:** pits, thickening, discolouration and lifting; see [nail psoriasis](/conditions/nail-psoriasis/).

When plaques clear they leave dark or light patches that can take months to even out. Many patients find these marks as troublesome as the psoriasis.

## How to spot it

- Patches with a clear edge and thick scale that returns after you pick it off
- The same areas on both sides of the body
- A scalp with stuck-down scale, not just loose flakes
- Tiny pits in the fingernails
- New patches along scratches or scars
- A relative with psoriasis

Psoriasis is often mistaken for ringworm or eczema. Ringworm has an active edge and clearer centre; eczema is less defined and itchier. A [skin scraping](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/fungal-tests/) or [biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/) settles doubt.

## Causes and triggers

In psoriasis, immune cells send signals that make skin cells multiply about ten times faster than normal. The cells pile up as plaques. Genes set the tendency; triggers switch it on.

Psoriasis is more than a skin condition. People with moderate or severe psoriasis have higher rates of [high blood pressure](/services/internal-medicine/hypertension/), [diabetes](/services/internal-medicine/diabetes/), [raised cholesterol](/services/internal-medicine/high-cholesterol/) and heart disease, so these are checked as part of care.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - you have scaly patches that have lasted more than a few weeks
> - patches are spreading or covering large areas
> - your joints are stiff, swollen or painful
> - your nails are changing
> - creams from a pharmacy have not helped, or helped only while you used them
> - psoriasis is affecting sleep, work or mood

> [!URGENT] Urgent care
> Seek same-day medical care if most of your skin becomes inflamed and hot, or if widespread small pustules appear with fever and shivering. These rare forms of psoriasis can make you seriously unwell.

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Strong steroid creams used continuously for months, and steroid tablets or injections for psoriasis without specialist advice: stopping them can cause severe rebound
> - Mixed creams and lightening creams on plaques
> - Picking and forceful removal of scale
> - Smoking and heavy drinking
> - Unverified herbal "cures"

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Psoriasis is usually diagnosed by its appearance and distribution, including a check of the scalp, nails, skin folds and joints. Tests are used when the picture is not typical. The dermatologist measures how much skin is involved and asks how it affects your life, because both guide the choice of treatment. Blood pressure, weight and blood tests are checked before and during systemic treatment.

## Psoriasis treatment at the clinic

**Topical treatment** for limited disease: a steroid ointment or cream of suitable strength, often combined with a vitamin D analogue; milder preparations for the face and folds; scalp-specific lotions, foams and shampoos.

**[Phototherapy](/services/clinical-dermatology/phototherapy/).** Narrowband UVB two or three times a week clears or greatly improves most plaque and guttate psoriasis. Brown and Black skin tolerates it well. A handheld unit treats the scalp, palms and soles.

**Systemic treatment** for severe or disabling psoriasis: tablets that slow skin cell growth or modify the immune response, and biologic injections that block specific immune signals. These need blood tests and regular review.

**Whole-person care:** treatment of joint disease, and attention to weight, blood pressure, blood sugar and mood.

Follow-up for stable psoriasis can be by [online consultation](/services/dermatology-consultations/online-consultation/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Moisturise thickly every day; it reduces scale, itch and cracking.
- Take short, lukewarm baths and pat dry.
- Protect skin from cuts and scratches.
- Stop smoking and limit alcohol.
- Keep to a healthy weight; it improves response to treatment.
- Use treatments as planned and reduce them as advised, not abruptly.
- Report joint pain early.

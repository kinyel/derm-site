## What baby eczema looks like on brown and Black skin

On a baby with dark skin, eczema is seldom red. Look for patches that are darker brown, greyish or purplish, dry and rough, and for tiny raised bumps centred on hair follicles on the chest, back and limbs, which give the skin a goose-flesh feel. The cheeks are often the first site; the nappy area is usually spared because it stays moist.

Babies cannot scratch well, so they rub their face against the mattress or a parent's shoulder. After a flare settles, lighter or darker marks remain for weeks to months and then recover. These are not scars and are not caused by treatment.

## How to spot it

- The rash started after the first month of life.
- The skin is dry all over, not just where the rash is.
- It comes and goes, with clear triggers such as heat, soap or a cold.
- The baby is otherwise well and growing normally.
- A parent or sibling has eczema, asthma or allergies.

Greasy yellow scale on the scalp in the first weeks is [cradle cap](/conditions/cradle-cap/). Bumps on the palms, soles and between the fingers with an itchy household suggest [scabies](/conditions/scabies/).

## Causes and triggers

Eczema skin has a weaker barrier: it loses water and lets irritants in. The tendency is inherited. Flares follow dry air, soaps, heat, saliva, infections and vaccinations (which should still be given on time). Medicated and antiseptic soaps, often recommended for babies in Nigeria, are a frequent trigger.

> [!URGENT] Get medical help the same day if
> - the eczema suddenly becomes painful with clusters of small blisters or punched-out sores
> - the baby has a fever, is drowsy or will not feed

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - the rash is not controlled by moisturiser alone
> - there is weeping, crusting or pus
> - the baby is not sleeping because of itch
> - you are worried about using a steroid cream

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Antiseptic liquids in bath water, medicated soaps and bubble bath
> - "Triple action" creams and adult-strength steroid creams
> - Lightening creams or toning lotions to "clear" the marks
> - Herbal baths and unlabelled balms
> - Cutting out foods without advice

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The dermatologist diagnoses eczema from the history and examination, with the baby undressed in a warm room. Swabs are taken from crusted areas. Allergy testing is considered only if the history suggests a true allergy.

## Baby eczema treatment at the clinic

- **A written plan** covering bath, moisturiser, flare cream and when to return.
- **Moisturiser** in quantity: smooth it on in the direction of hair growth at least twice a day.
- **A mild steroid cream** once a day to rough, itchy areas for the number of days stated, then stop. A non-steroid cream may be used for the face.
- **Infection** is treated promptly.
- **Follow-up** to adjust the plan as your baby grows; reviews can be by [online consultation](/services/dermatology-consultations/online-consultation/).

For older children and adults, see [eczema](/conditions/eczema/). The service is described under [children's skin care](/services/childrens-skin-care/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Dress the baby in soft cotton; avoid overheating.
- Keep nails short and use cotton mittens at night if needed.
- Wash clothes with a mild, fragrance-free detergent and rinse well.
- Apply moisturiser before going out in harmattan wind.

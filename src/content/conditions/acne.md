## What acne looks like on brown and Black skin

On dark skin an inflamed spot is often brown, violet or the same colour as the skin, and tender, instead of bright red. What stands out more is what follows: a flat dark mark where each spot was. Many people come to the clinic for these marks, called post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, without realising that mild acne underneath is still producing them.

Other patterns seen often in Lagos:

- **Small, uniform bumps on the forehead and temples** from hair oils and pomades ("pomade acne").
- **Sudden crops of identical bumps** on the face, chest or back after steroid creams or bleaching products.
- **Deep, painful lumps along the jaw and chin** in adult women, flaring before periods.
- **Spots on the back, chest and buttocks,** worse with sweat, tight clothing and heat.
- **Raised scars or [keloid scars](/conditions/keloids/)** on the chest, shoulders and jawline after severe acne.

## How to spot it

You probably have acne if you have several of these:

- blackheads or whiteheads, particularly on the nose, forehead and chin
- pimples that come in cycles, with new ones as old ones heal
- spots on the face together with the chest, shoulders or back
- oily skin
- dark marks left behind in the same areas

Bumps that are all the same size and itch may be fungal [folliculitis](/conditions/folliculitis/). Redness and bumps on the cheeks and nose without blackheads, after the age of 30, may be [rosacea](/conditions/rosacea/).

## Causes and triggers

Four things happen together: oil glands make more oil, dead cells block the follicle, a skin bacterium multiplies in the blocked pore, and the area becomes inflamed. Hormones called androgens drive the oil glands, which is why acne starts at puberty and why it flares with the menstrual cycle.

Things that make it worse:

- **Heat, humidity and sweat,** which are constant in Lagos
- **Thick creams, shea butter, cocoa butter and hair oils** on acne-prone skin
- **Steroid creams and lightening products**
- **Picking and squeezing**
- **Some medicines,** including steroid tablets, some contraceptive injections and body-building supplements
- **Friction** from helmets, masks, tight collars and bra straps
- **Stress and poor sleep**

Diet plays a smaller part than most people think. Our article [Does food cause acne?](/blog/does-food-cause-acne/) sets out what the evidence shows.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - you have deep, painful lumps or spots that leave scars
> - dark marks are building up
> - acne covers the chest or back
> - two months of pharmacy treatment has not helped
> - acne appeared suddenly in adult life, or comes with irregular periods or new facial hair
> - acne is affecting your mood or confidence

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - "Triple-action" and other mixed creams that contain a steroid
> - Bleaching creams and soaps used to fade marks
> - Toothpaste, lime, alcohol, methylated spirit and antiseptic liquid on spots
> - Scrubbing, harsh soaps and gritty exfoliants
> - Squeezing, or extractions done by anyone who is not trained
> - Heavy oils and butters on the face, hairline and back

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Acne is diagnosed by examining the skin. The dermatologist notes the types of spot, how deep they are, whether there is scarring and how your skin pigments. You are asked about your periods, medicines, hair and skin products. Tests are needed only in particular situations: hormone blood tests if a hormonal condition is suspected, a swab if an unusual infection is possible, and blood tests before and during some tablet treatments. See [diagnostic tests](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/).

## Acne treatment in Lagos: what the clinic offers

Treatment is matched to severity and adjusted at review.

| Severity | What it looks like | Usual treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Mild | Blackheads, whiteheads, a few pimples | Retinoid cream or gel, often with benzoyl peroxide |
| Moderate | Many inflamed pimples; chest or back involved | Topical treatment plus a limited course of antibiotic tablets, or hormonal treatment for women |
| Severe | Deep nodules, cysts, scarring | Isotretinoin tablets with monitoring |

Supporting treatments:

- **Pigment control** from the start: sunscreen and prescription creams that calm pigment cells, so marks fade as the acne clears.
- **[Chemical peels](/services/aesthetic-treatments/chemical-peels/)** with salicylic acid for blocked pores and dark marks.
- **[Medical facials](/services/aesthetic-treatments/medical-facials/)** for careful extraction of blackheads.
- **[LED light therapy](/services/laser-treatments/led-and-ipl/)** as an addition for inflamed acne.
- **Steroid injection** into a single large, painful cyst to settle it quickly.
- **Scar treatment** once acne is controlled; see [acne scars](/conditions/acne-scars/).

Isotretinoin is highly effective but must never be taken in pregnancy and needs blood tests and regular review. Antibiotic tablets are used for a limited time and always with a cream, to limit resistance. Medicines are dispensed by the clinic's [dermatology pharmacy](/services/dermatology-pharmacy/), and follow-up can be by [online consultation](/services/dermatology-consultations/online-consultation/). ## Prevention and daily care

- Wash the face twice a day with a gentle cleanser; more often strips the skin and increases oil.
- Use a light, non-comedogenic moisturiser, even on oily skin.
- Apply a gel or fluid sunscreen every morning. It protects against dark marks.
- Keep hair oils and pomades off the forehead; wash pillowcases and headscarves weekly.
- Shower soon after sweating and wear loose cotton on the back and chest.
- Apply treatment to the whole affected area, not single spots.
- Give each treatment at least eight weeks.

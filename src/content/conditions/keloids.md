## What keloids look like on brown and Black skin

A keloid is smooth, firm, rubbery and raised, with a shiny surface. On dark skin it is usually darker than the surrounding skin, sometimes with a pink or purple tone while it is growing. It extends past the boundary of the wound that started it, sometimes with claw-like edges. That spread is what separates a keloid from a hypertrophic scar, which is raised but stays within the wound and often flattens with time.

Typical sites:

- **Earlobes and ear cartilage,** after piercing: round or dumbbell-shaped lumps
- **Centre of the chest,** often from an acne spot or a scratch too small to remember
- **Shoulders and upper back**
- **Jawline and beard area,** after acne or [razor bumps (pseudofolliculitis barbae)](/conditions/razor-bumps/)
- **Back of the scalp and neck** in men; see [scalp folliculitis](/conditions/scalp-folliculitis/)
- **Surgical scars,** including caesarean scars
- **Vaccination sites** on the upper arm

Keloids rarely form on the palms, soles, eyelids or the centre of the face.

## How to spot it

- A scar that is still getting thicker three months or more after the injury
- It has grown wider than the original cut, piercing or spot
- It itches, burns or is tender, especially when growing
- It feels hard and does not flatten when pressed
- Relatives have similar scars

## Causes and triggers

After an injury, cells called fibroblasts lay down collagen to repair the skin. In a keloid they do not switch off. Collagen keeps accumulating for months or years.

Triggers include ear and body piercing, surgery, [acne](/conditions/acne/), chickenpox, burns, insect bites, injections, tattoos, shaving bumps, and traditional scarification. Tension across a wound and infection or prolonged healing increase the risk. Keloids are commonest between the ages of 10 and 30 and may grow in pregnancy.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - a scar is thickening, itching or spreading
> - a bump is forming around a piercing
> - a keloid is painful, limits movement or catches on clothing
> - you form keloids and need surgery, a caesarean section or a mole removed
> - a "keloid" appeared without any injury, or is bleeding or ulcerated

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Having a keloid cut, tied off or burned without follow-up treatment
> - Home removal with thread, blades, acids or caustic herbal pastes
> - New piercings, tattoos and unnecessary cosmetic procedures
> - Picking or scratching
> - Tight jewellery and friction over a keloid

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The diagnosis is made by examination and the history of an injury. The dermatologist measures and photographs the keloid, with your consent, to track response. A [biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/) is taken only if the lump is unusual, because cutting into a keloid can stimulate it.

## Keloid removal and treatment at the clinic

Treatment is chosen by size, site, age of the keloid and your goals. Most plans combine two or more of the following.

| Treatment | What it does | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Steroid injection | Softens and flattens; eases itch and pain | Every 4–6 weeks; several sessions |
| Silicone gel or sheet | Hydrates and flattens new scars | Daily for months; best for prevention |
| Pressure | Limits regrowth | Pressure earrings after ear surgery; garments for the trunk |
| Cryotherapy | Freezes and shrinks | Can leave a pale mark on dark skin |
| Other injected medicines | For keloids that resist steroid | Chosen by the dermatologist |
| Surgical excision | Removes bulk | Only with injections, pressure or other treatment afterwards |
| Laser | Reduces redness and thickness | An addition, not a stand-alone treatment |

**Earlobe keloids** do well with surgical removal followed by steroid injections and a pressure earring worn for months. **Chest and shoulder keloids** are the hardest; injections and silicone are preferred to surgery. See [scar revision](/services/skin-surgery/scar-revision/) and [skin surgery](/services/skin-surgery/).

Side effects of steroid injection include thinning or a dent in the skin, visible small vessels and a pale halo around the injected area, which is more noticeable on dark skin and usually recovers over months. The dose and spacing are adjusted to limit this.

Be wary of anyone offering to remove a keloid in a single visit with a promise that it will not return.

## Prevention and daily care

- Treat acne and razor bumps early.
- Avoid piercings and tattoos if you or close relatives form keloids.
- Tell every surgeon and dentist about your tendency before any procedure.
- Keep new wounds clean, moist with petroleum jelly and covered; do not let them dry and crack.
- Once a wound has closed, use silicone gel or sheets daily for at least two months.
- Report any thickening early: small, young keloids respond best.

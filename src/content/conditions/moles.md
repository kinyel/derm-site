## What moles look like on brown and Black skin

Normal moles on dark skin are dark brown to black, evenly coloured, round or oval, and usually smaller than the end of a pencil. They may be flat, raised and smooth, or raised with coarse hairs. People with dark skin tend to have fewer moles than people with light skin, and more of them sit on the palms, soles and nail beds.

Spots often mistaken for moles:

- **[Dermatosis papulosa nigra](/conditions/dermatosis-papulosa-nigra/):** many small stuck-on bumps on the cheeks.
- **[Skin tags](/conditions/skin-tags/):** soft and hanging, in skin folds.
- **Seborrhoeic keratoses:** waxy, warty, "stuck-on" patches in older adults.
- **Dermatofibromas:** firm, dark, button-like bumps on the legs.
- **Blood under a nail or callus** after an injury, which grows out.

## How to spot a mole that needs checking

- It looks different from all your other moles (the "ugly duckling").
- It has changed over weeks to months.
- It has two or more colours, or blue, grey, white or red areas.
- Its edge is ragged or blurred.
- It bleeds, itches or forms a crust without injury.
- It is on a palm, sole, between the toes, under a nail, on the lips or genitals.

Photograph the mole beside a ruler and repeat in four weeks; bring the photographs to the consultation.

## Causes and triggers

Moles are formed by pigment cells growing in a group. Some are present at birth; most appear in childhood and the teenage years, and they may darken in pregnancy. New moles after the age of about 40 are less usual and worth showing to a doctor. Sunlight drives new moles in light skin; on dark skin, the melanomas that do occur are mostly on areas the sun does not reach.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - a mole is new, changing, bleeding or sore
> - you have a dark patch on a palm or sole, or a dark stripe in one nail
> - you have albinism, many moles or a family history of melanoma
> - a mole catches on clothing, a comb or a razor

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Cutting, burning, tying or applying acid or "removal cream" to a mole
> - Removal anywhere that does not send the tissue to a laboratory
> - Assuming dark skin cannot get skin cancer

## How moles are checked at the clinic

The dermatologist examines your whole skin, including the scalp, palms, soles and nails, and looks at any spot of concern with a dermatoscope, a lit magnifier that shows the pigment pattern beneath the surface. People with many moles can have them photographed and compared over time. See [mole mapping and dermoscopy](/services/skin-cancer-care/mole-mapping-and-dermoscopy/).

## Treatment at the clinic

- **Reassurance** for moles with a normal pattern.
- **Monitoring** with photographs for moles that are slightly unusual.
- **Removal** under local anaesthetic for any mole with worrying features, sent for laboratory examination; results are explained at follow-up. See [mole removal](/services/skin-surgery/mole-removal/).
- **Removal by choice** for moles that rub or that you dislike, with a discussion of the scar and, on dark skin, the risk of [keloids](/conditions/keloids/).

Mole checks are part of [skin cancer care](/services/skin-cancer-care/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Check your skin every few months, including soles, palms and nails.
- Use sunscreen and shade; this matters most for people with albinism or lighter skin.
- Keep photographs of moles you are watching.

## What paronychia looks like on brown and Black skin

In the acute form, one side of a nail fold swells, feels hot and becomes tense and shiny; the colour deepens to dark brown or purple, and a pale yellow area appears where pus has gathered. In the chronic form, the fold is rounded and puffy, the cuticle has disappeared, and the nail grows out with ridges and green-brown discolouration at the edges.

## How to spot it

- Acute: pain started within the past few days, after a hangnail, bite or manicure.
- Chronic: swelling has lasted six weeks or more and your hands are often in water.

## Causes and triggers

Acute paronychia is usually caused by Staphylococcus entering a small break in the skin. Chronic paronychia is mainly an irritant dermatitis, with yeast as a passenger. On the toes, the usual cause is an [ingrown toenail](/conditions/ingrown-toenails/).

> [!DERM] See a doctor if
> - pus is visible or pain is throbbing
> - redness or swelling is spreading up the finger
> - you have diabetes or weak immunity
> - there are small grouped blisters on the fingertip
> - swelling has lasted more than six weeks

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Cutting or pushing back cuticles
> - Piercing with pins or blades
> - Biting nails
> - Soaking in antiseptic liquid or bleach

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

By examination. A swab is taken from pus, and scrapings from chronic cases where fungus is suspected ([fungal tests](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/fungal-tests/)). Blood sugar is checked in recurrent infections.

## Paronychia treatment at the clinic

Acute: drainage under local anaesthetic when pus is present, and antibiotics if infection is spreading. Chronic: strict hand protection, a steroid cream to the folds for several weeks, and antifungal treatment if indicated. See [nail care](/services/nail-care/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Wear cotton-lined rubber gloves for wet work.
- Dry hands well and apply hand cream.
- Leave cuticles intact; take your own tools to the salon.

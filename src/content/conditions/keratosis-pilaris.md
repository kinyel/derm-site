## What keratosis pilaris looks like on brown and Black skin

The bumps are skin-coloured or darker brown, each the size of a grain of sand, evenly spread over the outer upper arms and thighs. On dark skin, every follicle may carry a small dark halo, giving the area a dotted look that is more noticeable than the roughness. Children can have it on the cheeks.

## How to spot it

- Rough to the touch, like goose flesh that does not go away.
- Symmetrical on both arms or thighs.
- No pus, and little or no itch.
- Other family members have it.

Itchy pus spots suggest [folliculitis](/conditions/folliculitis/).

## Causes and triggers

An inherited variation in how skin forms keratin lets it build up in the follicle opening and trap the hair. Dryness makes it worse: harmattan, air-conditioning, hot baths and harsh soap.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - bumps are inflamed, sore or spreading
> - dark marks are bothering you
> - moisturisers have not helped after two months

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Rough sponges, scrubs and picking
> - Lightening creams on the dark dots
> - Very hot baths and medicated soaps

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

It is recognised on sight and with a dermatoscope, which shows the coiled hair in each plug. No tests are needed.

## Keratosis pilaris treatment at the clinic

A keratolytic moisturiser applied once or twice daily is the foundation. A prescribed retinoid cream can be added. A series of superficial [chemical peels](/services/aesthetic-treatments/chemical-peels/) helps texture and marks, and [laser hair reduction](/services/laser-treatments/laser-hair-removal/) can reduce bumps on the limbs in suitable patients.

## Prevention and daily care

- Use a soap-free wash and lukewarm water.
- Moisturise within minutes of bathing, every day.

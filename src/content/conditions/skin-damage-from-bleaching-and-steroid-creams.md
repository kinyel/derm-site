## What the damage looks like on brown and Black skin

The first sign is often uneven colour: the face and body become lighter while the knuckles, elbows, knees, ankles and the skin around the eyes stay dark or look darker by contrast. With time other changes appear:

- **Thinning.** The skin turns shiny and fragile; small knocks cause bruises and tears, and wounds heal slowly.
- **Stretch marks.** Wide, dark or purple bands on the inner thighs, upper arms, breasts, abdomen and armpits, far broader than ordinary [stretch marks](/conditions/stretch-marks/).
- **Redness and visible veins,** especially on the cheeks and chest.
- **Steroid acne and extra hair** on the face.
- **Infections.** Widespread [ringworm](/conditions/ringworm-and-fungal-skin-infections/), [pityriasis versicolor](/conditions/pityriasis-versicolor/) and scabies that keep returning.
- **Ochronosis.** Sooty, blue-black patches with fine bumps over the cheekbones and temples.
- **A strong body odour** and delayed healing after surgery or childbirth.

## How to spot it

- Have you used a lightening cream, soap, lotion or "toning" product for more than three months?
- Is your face a different shade from your hands and feet?
- Does your skin burn or redden when you skip the cream?
- Have you developed stretch marks without weight change or pregnancy?
- Do rashes return every time treatment ends?

## Causes and triggers

Three groups of ingredients do most of the harm. **Potent steroids** thin the skin and suppress its defences. **Hydroquinone** at high strength or for long periods causes ochronosis. **Mercury** damages the kidneys and nerves and passes to babies in pregnancy. Many products combine them and are sold without a true label. Lagos sun worsens the pigment changes, and applying products over most of the body lets enough steroid into the blood to raise blood pressure and blood sugar.

Steroid creams are valuable medicines when prescribed for the right condition, strength and duration. The damage comes from unsupervised, prolonged use.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - you want to stop a lightening or steroid cream and are afraid of the rebound
> - your skin burns, peels or darkens when you miss the cream
> - you have blue-black patches on the cheeks
> - you are pregnant or planning pregnancy and using lightening products
> - you have wide stretch marks, easy bruising or slow-healing wounds

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Switching to another "stronger" or "organic" lightening product
> - Stopping a potent steroid suddenly after years of use over large areas, without advice
> - Scrubs, acids and peels at home on thin skin
> - Sun exposure without sunscreen
> - Injections, drips and pills sold for lightening

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Bring every product, including soaps and anything mixed for you. The dermatologist examines the skin with a dermatoscope, which shows the pattern of ochronosis and thinning, and takes scrapings where infection is suspected ([fungal tests](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/fungal-tests/)). After heavy use, blood pressure, blood sugar and other blood tests may be arranged with the [internal medicine service](/services/internal-medicine/general-medical-consultation/).

## Treatment at the clinic

1. **A withdrawal plan.** Depending on what you have used and for how long, the product is stopped at once or stepped down over weeks.
2. **Repair.** A gentle cleanser, a plain moisturiser several times a day and a broad-spectrum sunscreen. Nothing else for the first weeks.
3. **Treat what appears.** Acne, fungal infection, eczema or rosacea-like redness are treated properly once they declare themselves.
4. **Pigment, later.** When the skin barrier has recovered, uneven colour is treated with supervised options described under [hyperpigmentation](/conditions/hyperpigmentation/). Ochronosis may be offered careful peels or [Q-switch laser](/services/laser-treatments/q-switch-laser/), with modest, gradual results.

Recovery takes months. No one at the clinic will judge you for what you have used; the aim is healthy skin. If you live outside Lagos, an [online consultation](/services/dermatology-consultations/online-consultation/) is a practical first step.

## Prevention and daily care

- Use prescription creams only for the time and area prescribed.
- Buy skincare with a full ingredient list and a NAFDAC number.
- Wear sunscreen daily; it does more for even tone than any lightening product.
- Read [Is skin lightening safe?](/blog/is-skin-lightening-safe/) before starting any product that promises a lighter shade.

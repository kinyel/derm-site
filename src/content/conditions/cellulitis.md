## What cellulitis looks like on brown and Black skin

The textbook "red leg" is misleading for dark skin. Look for a limb that is larger than the other, with skin that is stretched and shiny, a deeper brown, violet or grey tone, and warmth you can feel. The edge is blurred. Pressing is painful. After recovery the area may stay darker and peel.

Cellulitis nearly always affects one side. Both legs equally swollen and discoloured is more often varicose eczema or fluid retention than infection.

## How to spot it

- One limb only.
- Warm, tender and swollen.
- Came on over a day or two.
- You feel feverish or unwell.

## Causes and triggers

Streptococcus or Staphylococcus bacteria enter through a break in the skin. The commonest entry point is split skin between the toes from [athlete's foot](/conditions/athletes-foot/); others are bites, grazes, ulcers and injection sites.

> [!URGENT] Go to an emergency department if
> - the area is spreading fast or the pain is severe
> - skin is turning black, blistering or losing feeling
> - you have a high fever, are shivering, vomiting or confused
> - the infection is around the eye
> - you have diabetes or weak immunity and feel unwell

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Massaging the limb or applying hot compresses and balms
> - Herbal poultices
> - Stopping antibiotics when you feel better
> - Steroid creams

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Diagnosis is clinical. Blood tests measure infection and blood sugar; a swab is taken from any open wound. A scan may be needed to exclude a blood clot or abscess.

## Cellulitis treatment at the clinic

Antibiotics for the prescribed course, pain relief, rest with the leg raised above hip level, and marking the edge to track response. Patients needing a drip or surgery are referred to hospital. Afterwards, the entry point is treated and skin care, compression and weight advice are given. Wounds are managed within [wound care](/services/wound-care/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Treat athlete's foot and dry, cracked skin.
- Clean and cover cuts and bites.
- Moisturise legs daily; wear compression if advised.
- Keep diabetes controlled.

## What photodermatoses look like on brown and Black skin

The pattern is the main clue: the forehead, cheeks, nose, rims of the ears, back and V of the neck, outer forearms and backs of the hands are affected, with a sharp cut-off at the sleeve or collar. Shaded areas, such as beneath the chin and nose and behind the earlobes, are spared.

- **Polymorphic light eruption:** crops of tiny, itchy, skin-coloured or dark bumps a few hours after strong sun; settles in days.
- **Chronic actinic dermatitis and actinic prurigo:** thick, dark, lined, very itchy skin on exposed areas for months or years.
- **Drug-induced photosensitivity:** an exaggerated sunburn-like darkening or an eczema-like rash on exposed skin.
- **Phytophotodermatitis:** streaks and splashes.
- **Pellagra:** dark, scaly, sharply bordered skin on the neck, forearms and hands with poor diet or heavy alcohol use.

## How to spot it

- The rash maps to where light falls.
- It is worse after outdoor days, travel or the dry season.
- A new medicine or product preceded it.

## Causes and triggers

An abnormal immune response to ultraviolet or visible light, a chemical in or on the skin that reacts with light, or an underlying disease such as [lupus](/conditions/cutaneous-lupus/).

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - a rash keeps appearing on sun-exposed skin
> - it began after a new medicine
> - you also have joint pain, mouth ulcers or fatigue
> - blisters or fragile skin appear on the backs of the hands

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Midday sun between 10 am and 4 pm
> - Perfume, citrus and essential oils on exposed skin
> - Lightening creams, which thin the skin and worsen sensitivity
> - Stopping prescribed medicines without advice

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

History, distribution and a medicine review come first. Blood tests look for lupus; a [skin biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/) and [patch testing](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/patch-testing/), including for sunscreen and fragrance allergy, are used where needed.

## Sun allergy treatment at the clinic

Protection is the mainstay: tightly woven long sleeves, a wide-brimmed hat, and a broad-spectrum SPF 50 sunscreen with high UVA protection, tinted where visible light is a trigger. Flares are treated with steroid creams. Causative medicines are changed with the prescriber. Persistent cases may be offered tablets or carefully graded [phototherapy](/services/clinical-dermatology/phototherapy/) to build tolerance.

## Prevention and daily care

- Apply sunscreen 20 minutes before going out and reapply every two hours.
- Consider window film for the car if you drive long hours.
- Keep a note of medicines that caused reactions.

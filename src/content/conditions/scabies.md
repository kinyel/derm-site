## What scabies looks like on brown and Black skin

The rash is a scatter of small, firm bumps the colour of the skin or darker, with scratch marks and crusts. Burrows are short, wavy, greyish lines and are hard to see on dark skin; the best places to look are the sides of the fingers, the finger webs and the inner wrists.

Typical sites are the finger webs, wrists, elbows, armpits, waistline, navel, buttocks, nipples and male genitals, where firm itchy nodules can last for weeks after cure. The face and scalp are spared in adults but involved in babies. After weeks of scratching, the skin becomes thick and dark, and yellow crusts indicate bacterial infection ([impetigo](/conditions/impetigo/)).

**Crusted scabies** is a severe form in frail or immunosuppressed people: thick, scaly crusts on the hands, feet and scalp, with little itch but millions of mites. It is highly contagious and needs specialist care.

## How to spot it

- The itch keeps you awake.
- Someone you live with or sleep beside is also scratching.
- Bumps cluster between the fingers, at the wrists and around the waist.
- A baby is irritable, feeding poorly and has bumps on the palms and soles.

## Causes and triggers

The female mite burrows into the outer skin and lays eggs; new mites mature in about two weeks. A typical case involves only ten to fifteen mites, which is why the rash is mostly an allergic reaction. Outbreaks occur in families, schools, hostels, barracks and care homes.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - you have an itch that is worse at night for more than two weeks
> - more than one person at home is itching
> - a baby under two months, or a pregnant woman, may have scabies
> - there are yellow crusts, pus or fever
> - the itch has not eased four weeks after treatment

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Steroid and "triple action" creams, which dull the itch while the mites multiply
> - Treating only the person who itches
> - Kerosene, bleach, battery fluid or agricultural chemicals on the skin; these cause burns and poisoning
> - Repeating scabicide many times without advice, which irritates the skin and keeps the itch going

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Scabies is diagnosed from the history and the pattern of the rash. A dermatoscope can show the mite at the end of a burrow, and a scraping examined under the microscope confirms it when needed. Other causes of night-time itch are considered if the picture does not fit; see [Why does my skin itch at night?](/blog/why-does-my-skin-itch-at-night/)

## Scabies treatment at the clinic

1. **A scabicide for everyone on the same day.** The lotion or cream is applied to cool, dry skin from the jawline to the toes, including between fingers and toes, under the nails, the navel and genitals, and left on for the time stated. Babies and older adults also have the scalp and face treated. The treatment is repeated after a week.
2. **Tablets** are used for outbreaks, crusted scabies or where lotion has failed, if suitable for the person.
3. **Bedding and clothes** used in the previous three days are washed hot and dried in the sun or ironed, or sealed in a bag for several days.
4. **Infection** is treated with antibiotics.
5. **The lingering itch** is eased with moisturiser, an anti-itch cream and, at night, an antihistamine.

Children's cases are seen within [children's skin care](/services/childrens-skin-care/). The clinic can give written instructions for schools and households.

## Prevention and daily care

- Treat all contacts together; this is the single step that prevents return.
- Trim fingernails and scrub under them on treatment day.
- Avoid sharing beds, towels and clothes until everyone has been treated.
- Children can return to school the day after the first treatment.

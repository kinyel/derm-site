## What it looks like on brown and Black skin

The patch is the same shape as whatever came before: a round area where discoid eczema was, pale cheeks after childhood eczema, a light ring where a boil healed, or a strip along a graze. Colour is lighter than normal but not pure white, and the border blends into surrounding skin. The contrast is strong on dark skin, which is why it causes more worry than the original rash.

## How to spot it

- You remember a rash, wound or treatment in that exact place.
- The surface feels normal, with no scale.
- It is not spreading.
- Sensation in the patch is normal.

## Causes and triggers

Inflammation interferes with the transfer of pigment from pigment cells to skin cells. Common triggers are [eczema](/conditions/eczema/), [seborrhoeic dermatitis](/conditions/seborrhoeic-dermatitis-and-dandruff/), nappy rash, burns, freezing treatment for warts, steroid injections and over-strong peels or lasers.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - a patch is completely white or getting larger
> - the patch is numb
> - there has been no improvement after six months
> - new pale patches appear where there was no rash

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Steroid or "triple action" creams used for months
> - Lightening the surrounding skin to match
> - Scrubbing the area

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

The dermatologist examines the patch under a Wood's lamp and checks sensation. A scraping excludes [pityriasis versicolor](/conditions/pityriasis-versicolor/), and a [skin biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/) is taken only when the cause is unclear.

## Treatment at the clinic

Treatment is aimed at the condition that caused it. Once that is quiet, moisturiser, sun protection and time are usually enough. Stubborn patches may be offered a prescribed cream or [targeted phototherapy](/services/clinical-dermatology/phototherapy/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Treat rashes early so that inflammation is brief.
- Ask about pigment risk before cryotherapy, peels or laser.
- Moisturise daily and use sunscreen.

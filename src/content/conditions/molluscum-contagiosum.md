## What molluscum looks like on brown and Black skin

The bumps are round, smooth and firm, like tiny pearls set in the skin. On dark skin they are skin-coloured, slightly lighter, or have a shiny, translucent look, and the central dimple is easiest to see with a light shone from the side. A child may have a handful or several dozen, grouped where skin touches skin: the sides of the chest, armpits, inner elbows, behind the knees, and the nappy area.

Patches of dry, itchy eczema often develop around them, and scratching spreads the virus in lines. When the immune system finally recognises a bump, it becomes inflamed and crusted for a week or two; this looks alarming but usually signals the beginning of the end. Healed bumps can leave a small pale or dark dot, or a tiny pit, that fades with time.

## How to spot it

- Bumps are smooth, not rough like [warts](/conditions/warts/).
- Each has a central dimple.
- They appear in crops over weeks.
- The child is otherwise completely well.

## Causes and triggers

A poxvirus infects only the outer layer of skin. It passes by direct contact, by scratching and through wet towels, sponges and bath toys. Children with [eczema](/conditions/baby-eczema/) get more bumps because their skin barrier is weaker and they scratch.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - bumps are on the eyelids or the eye is red
> - bumps are infected: painful, swollen or oozing pus
> - there are very many, or they are large
> - the surrounding eczema is troublesome
> - an adult has bumps on the face or genital area

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Squeezing or picking the bumps; the core is full of virus
> - Acids, bleach, garlic or herbal mixtures
> - Steroid creams on the bumps themselves
> - Shared baths and towels

## How it is diagnosed at the clinic

Molluscum is identified by examination with a dermatoscope. No tests are needed in a healthy child.

## Treatment at the clinic

- **Active waiting** with a moisturiser, and a mild cream for eczema around the bumps so that scratching stops.
- **Removal** of a limited number of bumps by gentle scraping after numbing cream, or light freezing. Both can leave temporary colour change on dark skin, so they are used selectively.
- **Applied treatments** that irritate the bump and prompt the body to clear it.
- **Antibiotics** only for true bacterial infection.

Children are seen with a parent within [children's skin care](/services/childrens-skin-care/).

## Prevention and daily care

- Give the child their own towel and sponge.
- Keep nails short; cover bumps that are being scratched.
- Moisturise daily if the skin is dry.
- Wash hands after touching the bumps.

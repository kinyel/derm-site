## How does laser hair removal work?

The laser emits light that is absorbed by melanin, the pigment in the hair shaft. The light turns to heat, travels down the hair and damages the follicle's growth centre. Hairs in the active growing phase are disabled; resting hairs are untouched, which is why several sessions are needed.

## Why is dark skin different?

The target is melanin in the hair. Dark skin also has plenty of melanin in its surface layer. A laser that cannot tell the two apart heats the skin as well as the hair, causing burns, blisters and lasting dark or pale marks.

Three settings solve this:

- **Wavelength.** At 1064 nm, light is absorbed less by surface pigment and reaches deeper. This is the Nd:YAG laser.
- **Pulse duration.** Longer pulses heat the thick hair steadily while giving the thinner surface layer time to shed heat.
- **Cooling.** A chilled tip, cold air or cryogen spray protects the surface before, during and after each pulse.

## Which devices should I avoid?

- **IPL (intense pulsed light).** Not a laser; it emits a broad band of wavelengths, including short ones strongly absorbed by skin pigment. It is a frequent cause of burns on brown and Black skin.
- **Alexandrite (755 nm)** and **ruby** lasers: designed for light skin.
- **Home devices,** most of which are IPL and state on the box that they are unsuitable for dark skin tones.
- **Any clinic that cannot tell you which machine and wavelength it uses.**

## What are the risks, and how are they reduced?

| Risk | How it is reduced |
|---|---|
| Burns and blisters | Correct wavelength, conservative energy, cooling, test patch |
| Dark or pale marks | No sun, tanning or lightening creams before and after; sunscreen |
| Bumps or folliculitis | Clean technique; aftercare advice |
| Paradoxical increase in fine hair | Uncommon; mainly on the face and neck; adequate energy and review |
| Eye injury | Protective goggles for patient and operator |

A slight warmth and bumps around each follicle for a few hours are expected and show that the follicles have responded.

## What happens before, during and after?

**Before.** A consultation and a test patch. Shave the area a day earlier; do not wax, thread or pluck for four weeks, because the root must be present. Stop retinoids and acids on the area for a few days. Avoid sun exposure and do not use lightening products.

**During.** You wear goggles. Each pulse feels like a hot snap; the cooling helps. Underarms take about ten minutes, legs up to an hour.

**After.** Cool compresses and a plain moisturiser. No hot baths, gym, tight clothing or scrubs for 48 hours. Sunscreen on exposed areas. Treated hairs shed over one to three weeks; this is not regrowth.

## Which areas and concerns respond well?

- Beard and neck in men with [razor bumps](/conditions/razor-bumps/)
- Bikini line and underarms with [ingrown hairs](/conditions/ingrown-hairs/) and dark marks
- Chin, upper lip and jawline in women with [hirsutism](/conditions/hirsutism/), alongside hormonal assessment
- Legs, arms, chest and back

Laser cannot be used over tattoos, and moles in the area are shielded.

## What should I ask a clinic?

1. Which laser and wavelength will you use on my skin?
2. Who operates it, and what training do they have with dark skin?
3. Will you do a test patch and wait before a full session?
4. What cooling does the machine have?
5. What will you do if I blister or darken?
6. How many sessions do you expect, and what result is realistic?

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - You have razor bumps, ingrown hairs or dark marks from hair removal
> - You are a woman with coarse hair on the chin, chest or abdomen
> - You were burned or marked by laser or IPL elsewhere
> - You have a skin condition, keloids or a history of cold sores in the area
> - You take medicines that increase sensitivity to light

## What is each trained to do?

**A pharmacist** is a medicines specialist. A registered pharmacist can recommend non-prescription treatments, explain how to use them, check for interactions and advise when you need a doctor. In Nigeria, look for a premises registered with the Pharmacy Council of Nigeria, with a pharmacist on duty. A patent medicine shop or roadside "chemist" is not the same thing.

**A dermatologist** is a medical doctor who, after qualifying, completes several years of specialist training in conditions of the skin, hair and nails. Dermatologists examine, diagnose, order and interpret tests such as scrapings and biopsies, prescribe, and carry out procedures.

## What can a pharmacy handle well?

- Mild dry skin and chapped lips
- A few pimples or blackheads
- Athlete's foot between the toes
- A single, obvious ring of ringworm on the body
- Mild dandruff
- Insect bites and mild heat rash
- Small cuts and grazes
- Sunscreen and moisturiser advice

A reasonable rule: use the product as directed for **two weeks** (six to eight weeks for acne). If the problem is not clearly improving, stop and seek a diagnosis.

## When should I go straight to a dermatologist?

**Because of how it looks or behaves**

- A rash that is spreading, blistering or painful
- Skin that has turned thin, shiny, stretch-marked or discoloured after creams
- A mole or dark spot that is new, changing, bleeding, or on a palm, sole or nail
- A sore that has not healed in four weeks
- White patches, especially if completely white or numb
- Lumps under the skin that are growing

**Because of where it is**

- Face, eyelids or genitals
- Scalp with hair loss, in adults or children
- Nails that are thick, discoloured, lifting or painful

**Because of who has it**

- Babies and young children with a persistent or widespread rash
- Pregnant women
- People with diabetes, HIV or other conditions affecting immunity

**Because of what has happened so far**

- It has returned after two or more courses of pharmacy treatment
- Several people in the household are itching
- You have been using a steroid, "triple action" or lightening cream for more than two weeks
- It is leaving scars or dark marks
- It is affecting sleep, work, school or confidence

**Urgently, the same day**

- A widespread rash with fever, or skin that is peeling in sheets
- Swelling of the lips, tongue or face, or difficulty breathing
- A rapidly spreading, hot, painful area
- Blisters or sores in the mouth and eyes after starting a new medicine

Go to an emergency department for these.

## Why does delay cost more?

- **The wrong cream changes the picture.** Steroid on a fungal infection blurs its shape and spreads it. See [ringworm or eczema](/blog/ringworm-or-eczema/).
- **Skin damage accumulates.** Months of mixed creams cause thinning and stretch marks that do not reverse; see [skin damage from bleaching and steroid creams](/conditions/skin-damage-from-bleaching-and-steroid-creams/).
- **Scars and pigment are harder to treat than the original problem.** Early acne treatment prevents both.
- **Some hair loss is permanent if not caught early.**
- **Skin cancer on dark skin is often found late** because it was treated as a wart, a fungal nail or a stubborn sore.
- **Resistance.** Repeated partial courses of antibiotics and antifungals make infections harder to clear.

## What happens at a dermatology consultation?

The dermatologist asks about the history, your products and medicines, and your general health, then examines the skin, usually with a dermatoscope. Tests are done if needed: a scraping for fungus, a swab, blood tests or a small biopsy. You leave with a diagnosis or a short list of possibilities, a written plan and a date for review.

## How do I prepare?

- Bring every cream, soap, tablet and supplement you have used, or photographs of the packs.
- Bring photographs of the rash at its worst.
- Do not apply creams or make-up to the area on the day.
- Remove nail polish for nail problems; avoid a fresh hairstyle that hides the scalp.
- Write down your questions.

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - Two weeks of pharmacy treatment has not worked
> - The problem keeps coming back
> - It involves a mole, hair loss, nails, the face or genitals
> - You have used a steroid or mixed cream repeatedly
> - You want a diagnosis, not another guess

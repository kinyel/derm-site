## Is it the sun?

The face, neck and backs of the hands are the only skin most people never cover. In Lagos, ultraviolet light is strong all year, including on cloudy days and through car windows. Melanin responds by increasing, so the face slowly becomes one or two shades deeper than the chest or thighs. Visible light from the sun adds to this in darker skin tones.

Sun-related darkening is even, affects the forehead, nose and cheekbones most, and stops at the collar. It improves with daily broad-spectrum sunscreen, a hat and shade at midday. [Do Black people need sunscreen?](/blog/do-black-people-need-sunscreen/) explains what to choose.

## Could it be melasma?

Melasma produces brown or grey-brown patches with irregular borders on both cheeks, the bridge of the nose, the forehead and the upper lip. It is common in women, starts or worsens in pregnancy or with hormonal contraception, and deepens after sun or heat. From a distance the whole face can look darker. See [the melasma guide](/conditions/melasma/) for how it is treated.

## Is it left over from acne or a rash?

Every pimple, bite or patch of eczema on brown and Black skin can leave a dark mark. When there are many, they merge into a general darkening of the cheeks, jawline or forehead. The clue is that the skin is not uniform when you look closely: there are separate spots of different ages. The guide to [hyperpigmentation](/conditions/hyperpigmentation/) covers this.

## Could a product be causing it?

Three patterns are common in Nigeria:

- **Lightening and "toning" creams.** Stopping them causes rebound darkening, and long use produces sooty patches on the cheekbones and temples. Often the body, where less cream was used or the skin is thicker, looks different from the face. See [skin damage from bleaching and steroid creams](/conditions/skin-damage-from-bleaching-and-steroid-creams/).
- **Allergy or irritation.** Hair dye, fragrance, essential oils, some sunscreens and "black soap" with additives can cause a low-grade [contact dermatitis](/conditions/contact-dermatitis/) that shows as grey-brown darkening of the forehead, temples and neck with little itch.
- **Fragrance plus sunlight.** Perfume or citrus applied to the face and neck reacts with sun to leave dark streaks.

## Are friction and heat involved?

Rubbing darkens skin. Scrubbing with sponges and exfoliating daily, tight headscarves and caps, and resting the forehead on a mat during prayer all thicken and darken the areas they touch. Cooking over open heat for hours can darken the cheeks and forehead in a net-like pattern.

## Is it a skin condition on the face?

[Seborrhoeic dermatitis](/conditions/seborrhoeic-dermatitis-and-dandruff/) causes darker or lighter, slightly scaly areas along the hairline, eyebrows and sides of the nose. Eczema around the eyes and mouth darkens with rubbing. Lichen planus pigmentosus gives slate-grey patches on the forehead, temples and neck and needs a dermatologist's diagnosis.

## Could it be a medicine or an internal cause?

Some medicines darken sun-exposed skin, including certain antimalarials, antibiotics, anti-seizure medicines and hormone treatments. Low vitamin B12, thyroid disease and, rarely, Addison's disease cause wider darkening that also affects the knuckles, gums, palm creases and scars. Raised insulin darkens and thickens the neck and sometimes the forehead; see [dark underarms, inner thighs, knees and neck](/conditions/dark-underarms-inner-thighs-knees-and-neck/).

## What can I do now?

1. Use sunscreen every morning and reapply when outdoors.
2. Stop scrubbing; cleanse with your hands.
3. Stop any lightening or mixed cream. If your skin burns or flares when you stop, get help with withdrawal.
4. Change one hair or skin product at a time if you suspect a reaction.
5. Give any fading product at least three months; follow [how to get rid of dark spots on the face](/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-dark-spots-on-your-face/).

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - Your face has darkened over weeks to months without an obvious reason
> - Darkening is patchy, grey or blue-black
> - It followed a new medicine, hair dye or cream
> - Your gums, palms, knuckles or scars are darkening too, or you feel unwell
> - Three months of sun protection has made no difference

## Where does body odour come from?

The body has two kinds of sweat gland. Eccrine glands cover the whole body and produce watery sweat for cooling. Apocrine glands sit in the armpits, groin and around the nipples, become active at puberty and release a thicker sweat containing proteins and fats. Bacteria that live on the skin digest apocrine sweat into small molecules with a strong smell.

So odour needs three things: sweat, bacteria and time. Bathing removes the products but not the bacteria, which regrow within hours in warm, damp folds. Lagos heat and humidity shorten that time.

## Why does the smell come back so quickly?

**Bacteria remain.** A quick rinse does not clear the armpits, groin, navel, behind the ears and between the toes. Hair holds sweat and bacteria against the skin.

**The smell is in the clothes.** Synthetic fabrics such as polyester trap odour molecules and bacteria that survive a cool, short wash. The shirt smells as soon as it warms on your body, even on clean skin.

**You sweat heavily.** [Hyperhidrosis](/conditions/hyperhidrosis/) keeps the skin wet all day.

**Products are being misused.** Deodorant applied over sweat, or perfume layered on unwashed skin, mixes with odour.

**A skin infection is present:**

- *Erythrasma:* brown, slightly scaly patches in the armpits or groin.
- *Trichomycosis:* yellow or reddish coating on underarm hairs with a sharp smell.
- *Pitted keratolysis:* tiny pits on the soles with very smelly feet.
- *[Athlete's foot](/conditions/athletes-foot/)* and fungal rashes in the groin.
- *Intertrigo:* sore, moist skin folds under the breasts or abdomen.
- *Hidradenitis suppurativa:* recurrent boils in the armpits and groin that discharge.

## What other causes are there?

- **Diet:** garlic, onion, curry spices, alcohol.
- **Hormonal change:** puberty, pregnancy, menopause.
- **Medicines and supplements.**
- **Lightening creams** containing steroids or mercury; see [skin damage from bleaching and steroid creams](/conditions/skin-damage-from-bleaching-and-steroid-creams/).
- **Medical conditions:** poorly controlled diabetes (a fruity smell), kidney or liver disease, and trimethylaminuria (a fishy smell).
- **Genital, dental or sinus infections,** which can be mistaken for body odour.
- **Worry without odour.** Some people are convinced they smell when others cannot detect it; this is distressing and treatable.

## What works?

1. **Wash properly once or twice a day** with soap, paying attention to armpits, groin, feet and folds. An antibacterial wash such as benzoyl peroxide used a few times a week reduces bacteria; rinse well, as it bleaches fabric.
2. **Dry completely,** especially folds and between toes.
3. **Apply antiperspirant at night** to dry armpits, and again in the morning if you wish.
4. **Trim or remove underarm hair.**
5. **Change clothes daily,** including bras and vests. Choose cotton or linen. Wash sweaty items promptly, turn them inside out, and dry them in the sun.
6. **Rotate shoes** and wear cotton socks.
7. **Review your diet** for obvious triggers.

## What does not help, or harms?

- Lime, lemon, alum, baking soda, bleach or methylated spirit on the skin: they irritate and darken it
- Antiseptic liquid in bath water every day
- Douching and perfumed intimate washes
- Scrubbing with rough sponges
- Covering odour with heavy perfume

## What can a dermatologist do?

- Diagnose and treat a bacterial or fungal infection with prescribed creams or tablets
- Prescribe stronger antiperspirants
- Reduce underarm sweating with [botulinum toxin](/services/aesthetic-treatments/botox/)
- Arrange blood sugar and other tests through [internal medicine](/services/internal-medicine/general-medical-consultation/) when a medical cause is possible

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - Odour persists after four weeks of the steps above
> - You have a rash, discolouration, sores or boils in the armpits, groin or feet
> - You sweat enough to soak clothing
> - The smell is new, fruity or fishy, or you feel unwell
> - Odour is affecting your work or relationships

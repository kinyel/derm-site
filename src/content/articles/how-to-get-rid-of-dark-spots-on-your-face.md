## Why do dark spots form?

Skin with more melanin responds to almost any irritation by making extra pigment. A pimple, an insect bite, a scratch, a burn from a hot comb, a rash or a harsh product can each leave a flat brown mark where the inflammation was. Doctors call this post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, and it is the commonest cause of dark spots on brown and Black skin.

Other causes look similar but behave differently:

- **Melasma:** blotchy brown patches on both cheeks, the forehead and upper lip, driven by hormones and light. See [melasma causes and treatment](/conditions/melasma/).
- **Sun spots:** small, defined spots on the cheeks and temples after years of sun.
- **Raised dark bumps:** [dermatosis papulosa nigra (DPN)](/conditions/dermatosis-papulosa-nigra/), which no cream will remove.
- **Darkening from products:** long use of lightening creams can cause blue-black patches called ochronosis.
- **Darkening around the mouth or eyes** from eczema, licking, rubbing or allergy.

The treatment depends on which of these you have, so a spot that does not behave like an ordinary mark deserves a diagnosis first.

## How to get rid of dark spots on face at home: a safe routine

**Step 1: stop new spots.** Treat the acne, eczema, razor bumps or whatever is causing the marks. Fading old spots while new ones form is like mopping with the tap running. For pimples, follow [how to get rid of pimples](/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-pimples/).

**Step 2: sunscreen every morning.** Use a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher, and reapply if you are outdoors. This single step does more than any fading cream, and without it nothing else works well in Lagos sun. [Do Black people need sunscreen?](/blog/do-black-people-need-sunscreen/) explains which types suit humid weather.

**Step 3: one fading ingredient, used patiently.** Introduce it slowly, every other night at first.

**Step 4: be gentle.** No scrubs, no picking, no hot water. Irritation makes pigment.

Take a photograph in the same light once a month. Changes are too slow to see day by day.

## Which ingredients fade dark spots?

- **Azelaic acid:** reduces pigment production and treats acne; safe in pregnancy.
- **Retinoids (adapalene, tretinoin):** speed up the shedding of pigmented cells and prevent pimples. Not for use in pregnancy.
- **Niacinamide:** slows the transfer of pigment to skin cells; rarely irritates.
- **Vitamin C:** an antioxidant that brightens; choose a stable formula and store it away from heat.
- **Tranexamic acid, kojic acid, arbutin and liquorice extract:** useful additions with a gentler effect.
- **Hydroquinone:** effective, but only under a doctor's supervision, at a prescribed strength and for a limited number of months. Unsupervised use is how ochronosis develops.

More is not better. Layering several acids and retinoids together inflames the skin and creates new marks.

## What should I avoid?

- **Bleaching, "toning" and "whitening" creams, soaps and injections.** Read [Is skin lightening safe?](/blog/is-skin-lightening-safe/)
- **"Triple action" and other steroid creams.**
- **Lemon, lime, baking soda and toothpaste.**
- **Harsh scrubs, sponges and frequent facials with extractions.**
- **Home peels bought online** at professional strength.
- **Laser or peels from an operator without experience of dark skin.** The wrong device or setting burns and darkens.

## What can a dermatologist do that I cannot?

A dermatologist first confirms what the spots are, using a dermatoscope and sometimes a special lamp to judge how deep the pigment lies. Then treatment can step up:

- **Prescription creams,** including combinations that are stronger than pharmacy products, used for a defined period.
- **[Chemical peels](/services/aesthetic-treatments/chemical-peels/)** in a series, with acids and strengths selected for brown and Black skin.
- **[Laser pigmentation removal](/services/laser-treatments/laser-pigmentation-removal/)** for selected types of spot, after a test area.
- **Tablets** for stubborn melasma in suitable patients.
- **Treatment of the underlying condition,** so the result lasts.

The condition guide on [hyperpigmentation](/conditions/hyperpigmentation/) describes each option and its risks.

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - Spots have not faded after three months of sunscreen and home treatment
> - Patches are spreading on the cheeks, forehead or upper lip
> - Skin has turned blue-black, thin or red after using a lightening cream
> - A spot is raised, growing, bleeding or changing colour
> - You are pregnant and unsure what is safe
> - The marks are affecting your confidence

## How does their training differ?

**Dermatologist.** A medical degree, a period of general medical practice, then years of specialist residency training with examinations, leading to a fellowship of a postgraduate medical college. A dermatologist is licensed to diagnose disease, prescribe medicines, order tests, operate on skin and use medical devices.

**Aesthetician** (also called esthetician, beauty therapist or facialist). A certificate or diploma course in skincare, lasting months to a year or two depending on the school. Aestheticians are trained to cleanse, exfoliate, massage and apply cosmetic products, and to recognise when a client should see a doctor. They do not diagnose or prescribe.

Both have a place. The question is which problem belongs with whom.

## What is an aesthetician well suited to?

- Relaxing and cleansing facials for healthy skin
- Advice on a basic routine and product textures
- Gentle exfoliation and hydration treatments
- Waxing, threading and similar grooming
- Make-up advice, including camouflage
- Supporting treatments carried out under a doctor's plan

## What needs a dermatologist?

**Diagnosis of any skin, hair or nail condition:** rashes, [acne](/conditions/acne/) beyond a few spots, eczema, fungal infections, hair loss, nail changes, moles and lumps.

**Pigment problems on brown and Black skin:** [hyperpigmentation](/conditions/hyperpigmentation/) and [melasma](/conditions/melasma/) look similar to each other and to several other conditions, and are easily worsened by the wrong peel or product.

**Anything that breaks or heats the skin:**

- Injections: [botulinum toxin](/services/aesthetic-treatments/botox/), fillers, PRP
- [Chemical peels](/services/aesthetic-treatments/chemical-peels/) beyond the mildest cosmetic kind
- [Microneedling](/services/aesthetic-treatments/microneedling/) at medical depths
- Lasers and light devices, including [laser hair removal for dark skin](/services/laser-treatments/laser-hair-removal/)
- Removal of moles, skin tags, warts and [DPN (small dark facial bumps)](/conditions/dermatosis-papulosa-nigra/)
- Treatment of scars and keloids

**Anything needing a prescription.**

## Why does it matter more for brown and Black skin?

Darker skin marks easily after inflammation and is more prone to keloids. Aggressive extractions, strong peels, the wrong laser wavelength or a cautery pen in untrained hands can leave dark patches, pale patches or raised scars that take months or years to treat. A growth removed without examination may have been something that needed a laboratory test.

## What should I ask before any treatment?

1. What are your qualifications, and who regulates you?
2. What is my skin problem called?
3. What exactly is in this product or device, and why is it right for my skin tone?
4. What are the risks, and what will you do if I react?
5. Is a doctor on site?
6. Can I see the product packaging and its registration number?

## What are the warning signs?

- A diagnosis is offered without anyone examining your skin closely
- Creams are sold from unlabelled jars or "mixed" for you
- Promises of a lighter complexion or results in days
- Injections, drips or lasers offered in a salon, hotel room or at home
- Pressure to buy a package on the spot
- No aftercare instructions or emergency contact

## How do the two work together?

In a medical setting, the dermatologist assesses and prescribes, and supporting skincare such as a [medical facial](/services/aesthetic-treatments/medical-facials/) is carried out under that plan. You get the comfort of a treatment and the safety of a diagnosis.

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - A skin concern has lasted more than a few weeks or keeps returning
> - Facials or products are making your skin worse
> - You are considering injections, peels, laser or removal of a growth
> - You were burned, scarred or discoloured by a cosmetic treatment
> - You are not sure what your skin problem is

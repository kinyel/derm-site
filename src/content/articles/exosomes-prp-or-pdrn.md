## What is PRP?

Platelet-rich plasma is made from a small sample of your own blood. The tube is spun in a centrifuge to separate the plasma and concentrate the platelets, which carry growth factors that the body uses to heal wounds. The plasma is then injected into the scalp, or applied to the face during microneedling (sometimes called a "vampire facial").

**Used for:** early pattern hair thinning, acne scars, fine lines and skin texture.
**Evidence:** numerous clinical studies, with the most consistent results in pattern hair loss and as an addition to microneedling for scars. Preparation methods differ between clinics, which partly explains why results vary.
**Good to know:** it is your own tissue, so allergy is not a concern. It is less suitable for people with low platelet counts, blood disorders or on blood thinners.

See [PRP microneedling](/services/aesthetic-treatments/prp-microneedling/) and [PRP for hair loss](/services/hair-and-scalp/prp-for-hair-loss/).

## What is PDRN?

Polydeoxyribonucleotide (PDRN), and the related polynucleotides (PN), are purified fragments of DNA, most often obtained from salmon. In the skin they bind to a receptor involved in tissue repair, encourage fibroblasts to make collagen, and draw in water. They are injected in tiny amounts into the upper skin or applied with microneedling.

**Used for:** thin, dehydrated or crepey skin, the under-eye area, fine lines, and recovery after laser.
**Evidence:** a growing number of studies, many from Korea and Europe, showing improved hydration, elasticity and texture. Larger, longer trials are still needed.
**Good to know:** not suitable for people with a fish allergy. Small bumps at injection points settle within a day or two.

See [PDRN treatment](/services/aesthetic-treatments/pdrn-treatment/).

## What are exosomes?

Exosomes are microscopic packets that cells release to send signals to other cells. Products used in aesthetics contain exosomes harvested from laboratory-grown cells or plants. They are applied to the skin immediately after microneedling or fractional laser, when tiny channels allow them to enter.

**Used for:** calming skin and speeding recovery after procedures, and for general skin quality. Use on the scalp for hair thinning is being studied.
**Evidence:** early. Laboratory research is encouraging and small human studies report benefit, but well-controlled trials are few.
**Good to know:** products vary widely in source and quality. Exosomes should not be injected into a vein or sold as a cure for any disease.

## How do they compare?

| | PRP | PDRN | Exosomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source | Your own blood | Purified salmon DNA | Cultured cells or plants |
| Blood draw needed | Yes | No | No |
| Delivery | Injection or microneedling | Fine injections or microneedling | On the skin after microneedling or laser |
| Main strengths | Hair thinning, scars | Hydration, thin skin, under-eyes | Recovery, skin quality |
| Amount of evidence | Most | Moderate | Least so far |
| Avoid if | Blood or platelet disorder | Fish allergy | Product source is unclear |

## Which one suits which concern?

- **Thinning hair:** PRP, alongside proven medical treatment.
- **Acne scars:** microneedling with PRP, or other scar procedures.
- **Tired, fine-lined under-eyes:** PDRN.
- **Dull skin and recovery after laser or microneedling:** exosomes or PDRN.

They can be combined across a treatment plan. None replaces sunscreen, a retinoid or the treatment of an underlying skin condition, and none lightens skin.

## What should I ask the clinic?

- Which product is this, who makes it and is it registered?
- How is it stored and opened? Is the kit single-use?
- Who performs the treatment?
- What result is realistic, and by when?

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - You are deciding between these treatments
> - You have hair loss that has not been diagnosed
> - You have active acne, eczema, infection or cold sores in the area
> - You developed dark marks, lumps or infection after a treatment elsewhere

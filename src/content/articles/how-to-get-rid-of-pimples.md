## What a pimple is

A pimple forms when a pore blocks with oil and dead skin cells. Bacteria that live on everyone's skin multiply in the plug, and the body responds with inflammation: a tender bump, sometimes with pus. Blackheads and whiteheads are the same blockage without the inflammation. Doctors call the whole process acne.

That explains two things. First, you cannot scrub pimples away, because the problem is inside the pore. Second, treatment has to be applied to the whole area that gets spots, not only to the spot you can see, because the next pimples are already forming under skin that looks clear.

## How to get rid of pimples: a routine that works

Keep it to four steps, morning and night, for at least eight weeks.

1. **Cleanse.** Use a mild, soap-free cleanser and lukewarm water, with your fingertips. Pat dry. In Lagos heat, rinse or cleanse again after heavy sweating.
2. **Treat.** Apply one active product in a thin layer to the entire acne-prone area. Choose from the list in the next section. Start every other night and build up to nightly as your skin gets used to it.
3. **Moisturise.** A light, oil-free moisturiser reduces the dryness and peeling that make people give up on treatment. Oily skin still needs it.
4. **Protect.** In the morning, use a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher. Sun darkens the marks that pimples leave. A gel or fluid texture suits humid weather.

Change one thing at a time. If you start three new products together and your face stings, you will not know which to blame.

## Which pharmacy ingredients work?

- **Benzoyl peroxide** kills acne bacteria and helps unblock pores. Lower strengths work as well as higher ones with less irritation. It bleaches towels and pillowcases.
- **Adapalene** is a retinoid that stops pores from clogging and helps fade marks. Use it at night. Mild dryness in the first weeks is expected.
- **Salicylic acid** loosens blackheads and suits oily skin as a cleanser or leave-on product.
- **Azelaic acid** calms pimples and lightens the dark spots they leave, which makes it useful on brown and Black skin.
- **Niacinamide** reduces oiliness and redness and is gentle.

Retinoids must not be used in pregnancy or when trying to conceive; ask a doctor or pharmacist for safe alternatives. Antibiotic creams and tablets should be used only when prescribed, and never alone, because bacteria become resistant.

Tea tree oil has modest evidence for mild acne. Other home remedies have little, and several cause harm; our guide to [shea butter, black soap and other home remedies](/blog/shea-butter-black-soap-and-home-remedies/) explains which.

## What makes pimples worse?

- **Picking and squeezing.**
- **Steroid and lightening creams.** They can clear the face for a few weeks and then trigger crops of uniform bumps that are hard to treat. See [skin damage from bleaching and steroid creams](/conditions/skin-damage-from-bleaching-and-steroid-creams/).
- **Heavy products:** petroleum jelly, cocoa butter, coconut oil and thick sunscreens on the face.
- **Hair products.** Oils, pomades and edge gels cause pimples along the hairline and temples. Keep them off the skin and wash your face after applying them.
- **Friction and sweat** from helmets, tight caps, face masks and phone screens.
- **Scrubs and harsh soaps.** Read [how to exfoliate your skin safely](/blog/how-to-exfoliate-your-skin-safely/).
- **Stopping treatment** as soon as the skin looks better.

Diet plays a smaller part than most people think. [Does food cause acne?](/blog/does-food-cause-acne/) covers what the research shows.

## How to get rid of the dark marks pimples leave

On dark skin, a flat brown spot remains after most pimples. This is post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, not a scar, and it fades over months. Three things speed it up: preventing new pimples, not picking, and daily sunscreen. Adapalene and azelaic acid treat both the pimples and the marks. Do not reach for a bleaching cream. ## How long should I try before getting help?

Give a consistent routine eight weeks. If you see no change by then, or if any of the points in the box below apply, see a dermatologist. Prescription treatment exists for every type of acne, including hormonal treatment for women and isotretinoin for severe or scarring acne, and early treatment prevents scars. The article [How long does acne treatment take to work?](/blog/how-long-does-acne-treatment-take-to-work/) sets out a realistic timeline.

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - Pimples are large, deep or painful
> - They are leaving dark marks, pits or raised scars
> - Eight weeks of correct home treatment has not helped
> - Acne appeared after you used a steroid, lightening or "triple action" cream
> - You have acne with irregular periods or excess facial hair
> - Your skin is affecting your mood or confidence

## What decides whether hair returns?

One thing: the state of the follicle. Each hair grows from a follicle that cycles through growth, rest and shedding for decades. If something interrupts the cycle but leaves the follicle intact, hair returns when the cause is removed. If inflammation replaces the follicle with scar tissue, nothing can grow there again.

So the question becomes: what kind of hair loss do I have?

## Which types usually grow back on their own?

**Shedding after a shock ([telogen effluvium](/conditions/telogen-effluvium/)).** Hair falls from all over the scalp two to four months after a fever, surgery, crash diet, major stress or delivery. It stops within about six months and recovers fully. See also [Is hair loss after childbirth normal?](/blog/is-hair-loss-after-childbirth-normal/)

**[Hair breakage](/conditions/hair-breakage/).** The follicle is healthy; the shaft is snapping from relaxers, heat or handling. New hair grows normally once the damage stops.

**Hair loss from scalp ringworm** in children regrows after antifungal tablets, provided it is treated before it scars.

**Loss from medicines or chemotherapy** generally recovers after the treatment ends.

## Which types often grow back, with help?

**[Alopecia areata](/conditions/alopecia-areata/).** Smooth round patches caused by the immune system. Small patches often regrow within a year; treatment speeds this. Extensive or long-standing loss is less predictable.

**Early [traction alopecia](/conditions/traction-alopecia/).** Thinning edges from tight braids, weaves, ponytails or wig bands. If you can still see fine hairs and the loss is recent, hair returns over six to twelve months after tension stops.

## Which types need ongoing treatment?

**Pattern hair loss.** In [women](/conditions/female-pattern-hair-loss/), a widening parting and thinner crown; in [men](/conditions/male-pattern-hair-loss/), a receding hairline and bald crown. Follicles shrink gradually. Treatment such as minoxidil keeps the hair you have and thickens some of it, for as long as you continue. Follicles that have been inactive for years do not restart; a [hair transplant](/services/hair-and-scalp/hair-transplant/) moves permanent hair into those areas.

## Which types do not grow back?

**[Scarring alopecia](/conditions/scarring-alopecia/).** A group of conditions that destroy follicles. In women of African descent the commonest is central centrifugal cicatricial alopecia (CCCA), which begins at the crown and spreads outwards, often with itching, tenderness or burning, and sometimes with no symptoms. Treatment cannot bring back lost hair, but it can stop the spread, so every month counts.

**Late traction alopecia.** After years of pulling, the hairline skin is smooth and shiny and the follicles are gone.

**Burns and deep infections** of the scalp, including chemical burns from relaxers.

## What are the signs that regrowth is under way?

- Short, fine hairs of similar length along the hairline or parting
- Less hair in the comb and shower
- In alopecia areata, soft white or fine hairs appearing in the patch, which later darken
- A photograph that looks fuller than the one from three months ago

Take pictures in the same light every month. Memory is unreliable; photographs are not.

## What helps hair return?

1. **Get the diagnosis.** Treatments for different types of hair loss are different, and some, such as tight "protective" styles for shedding, do harm.
2. **Remove the cause:** tension, heat, harsh chemicals, a deficiency, a medicine.
3. **Use proven treatment** for your type, for long enough.
4. **Treat the scalp:** dandruff and inflammation slow recovery.
5. **Eat adequately,** with enough protein and iron.
6. **Be patient.** Hair works on a timescale of months.

## What wastes time?

- Changing products every few weeks
- Oils, onion juice and "growth" pomades in place of treatment
- Supplements at high doses without a blood test
- Covering thinning with tighter styles, glue or heavy extensions
- Waiting to see if a tender, thinning crown improves by itself

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - Shedding has lasted more than six months
> - You have a bald patch, or your hairline or crown is thinning
> - The scalp itches, burns, hurts or has bumps or scale
> - The skin in an area looks smooth and shiny
> - A child is losing hair
> - Hair loss is affecting how you feel

## What happens to skin at night?

Several body rhythms line up against you in the evening:

- **Cortisol falls,** so inflammation is less restrained.
- **Skin warms** as the body sheds heat to prepare for sleep, and warm skin itches more.
- **Water loss through the skin rises,** so dry skin gets drier.
- **Itch-promoting chemicals** released by immune cells increase.
- **Attention shifts.** In a quiet, dark room, the brain has less competing input.

Scratching then damages the skin, which releases more itch signals. This itch-scratch cycle is why a mild daytime itch becomes a sleepless night.

## Which skin conditions cause it?

- **Dry skin.** The commonest cause, especially on the shins, arms and back; worse with hot baths, medicated soaps, air-conditioning and harmattan.
- **[Eczema](/conditions/eczema/).** Dry, rough, darker or greyish patches in the creases and on the hands, neck and eyelids.
- **[Scabies](/conditions/scabies/).** A mite infestation with characteristic night-time itch that affects close contacts.
- **[Heat rash](/conditions/heat-rash/).** Prickling bumps on the neck, chest and back in hot rooms and during power cuts.
- **[Hives](/conditions/hives/).** Raised weals that move around and fade within a day.
- **Insect bites.** Mosquitoes, and bed bugs, which leave lines or clusters of bites on exposed skin and tiny dark specks on the mattress seams.
- **Fungal infections** in the groin and feet, which itch when warm.
- **[Contact dermatitis](/conditions/contact-dermatitis/)** from detergent in bedding, fragrance or a new cream.
- **Pinworms** in children, causing itching around the anus at night.

## Which medical causes produce itch without a rash?

When the skin looks normal apart from scratch marks, doctors consider:

- **Iron deficiency,** with or without anaemia
- **Kidney disease**
- **Liver and bile duct conditions,** sometimes with yellow eyes or dark urine
- **Thyroid over- or underactivity**
- **Diabetes**
- **Medicines,** including some painkillers, blood pressure tablets and antimalarials; chloroquine-related itch is well recognised in people of African descent
- **Pregnancy,** in which severe itch of the palms and soles needs prompt blood tests
- **Infections** such as HIV and certain parasites
- **Blood disorders and lymphoma,** rarely, often with night sweats or weight loss
- **Nerve-related itch** in one area, such as the upper back or forearm

Long-lasting itch is covered in the guide to [chronic itch](/conditions/chronic-itch/).

## What helps tonight?

1. **Cool the room** with a fan or air-conditioning; sleep in loose cotton.
2. **Take a short, lukewarm shower** with a soap-free wash. Hot water feels good for a minute and makes itch worse for hours.
3. **Moisturise within three minutes** of drying, using a fragrance-free cream or ointment. Keep it in the fridge for extra relief.
4. **Press, do not scratch.** A cool, damp cloth or a covered ice pack for a few minutes interrupts the cycle.
5. **Keep nails short** and consider cotton gloves at night.
6. **Avoid triggers** in the evening: alcohol, hot spicy meals and hot drinks.
7. **Wash bedding** in a mild, fragrance-free detergent and rinse well.

## What should I avoid?

- Antiseptic liquids, medicated soaps and scrubbing
- Steroid or "triple action" creams used without a diagnosis; they can hide scabies and fungal infection and thin the skin
- Menthol and camphor balms on broken skin
- Sedating antihistamines every night for weeks

> [!DERM] When to see a doctor
> - Itching has lasted more than two weeks despite moisturising
> - Others in your home are itching
> - There is no rash and the itch is all over
> - You also have weight loss, night sweats, fever, yellow eyes, tiredness or great thirst
> - You are pregnant and itching severely
> - Sleep, work or mood are affected

## Why does it happen?

About nine in ten scalp hairs are growing at any time, and one in ten is resting before it sheds. Pregnancy hormones, especially oestrogen, hold more hairs in the growing phase, which is why many women find their hair thicker in the second half of pregnancy.

After delivery, oestrogen drops within days. The extra hairs move into the resting phase together and are pushed out two to four months later by new hairs growing underneath. Doctors call this postpartum [telogen effluvium](/conditions/telogen-effluvium/). The amount lost roughly equals the amount you would have shed gradually over the previous nine months.

## What does it look like on African hair?

- Handfuls of hair on wash day or when braids are taken down. Because coiled hair holds shed strands in the style, weeks of loss come out at once.
- Thinning at the temples and along the front hairline, where hair is finest. Many women describe it as "losing my edges".
- A few months later, short new hairs standing up along the hairline.
- A scalp that looks and feels normal.

## Is anything else adding to it?

Several things common after childbirth increase or prolong shedding:

- **Low iron** from blood loss at delivery or heavy bleeding afterwards
- **Thyroid changes.** Postpartum thyroiditis affects a minority of women and causes tiredness, palpitations, weight change and hair loss
- **Lack of sleep, stress and missed meals**
- **Tight styles.** Braids or a wig installed to hide thinning pull on a hairline that is already fragile and can cause [traction alopecia](/conditions/traction-alopecia/)
- **Underlying [female pattern hair loss](/conditions/female-pattern-hair-loss/),** which shedding can unmask

## What helps?

1. **Reassurance.** Each shed hair has a replacement growing behind it.
2. **Gentle handling.** Wide-tooth comb, fingers for detangling, no tight ponytails.
3. **Low-tension styles.** Loose twists, larger braids without added hair at the front, a wig over loosely braided hair with a soft cap. Skip edge control and glue.
4. **Food.** Regular meals with protein (fish, eggs, beans, meat), iron-rich foods (liver, leafy greens, beans) and fruit and vegetables.
5. **Continue the supplements** your doctor or midwife prescribed, including iron if you were anaemic.
6. **Wash as usual.** Avoiding wash day does not save hair; resting hairs fall regardless.
7. **A haircut or trim** can make thinner ends look fuller.

## What does not help?

- "Hair growth" oils, onion juice and rice water: no good evidence
- High-dose biotin and multi-ingredient hair supplements without a proven deficiency
- Steroid-containing scalp creams of unknown origin
- Relaxing or colouring fragile regrowth

## When is it not normal?

Postpartum shedding is diffuse, painless and temporary. Look for another cause if:

- shedding continues beyond the baby's first birthday
- there are round bald patches ([alopecia areata](/conditions/alopecia-areata/))
- the scalp is itchy, sore, scaly or has bumps
- the crown is thinning and tender
- you feel exhausted beyond new-parent tiredness, cold or hot all the time, have a racing heart, or your periods are very heavy

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - Shedding has not slowed nine to twelve months after delivery
> - Your hairline is moving back or your parting is widening
> - You can see patches of bare scalp
> - The scalp itches, burns or hurts
> - You have symptoms of anaemia or thyroid problems

## How does Botox work?

"Botox" is the best-known brand of botulinum toxin type A. Tiny amounts are injected into specific facial muscles. The toxin blocks the signal from nerve to muscle, the muscle relaxes, and the skin over it stops folding. The effect starts in three to five days, is complete by two weeks and fades over three to four months.

It treats **dynamic lines**, the ones that appear when you move your face: frown lines between the brows, horizontal forehead lines and crow's feet. It is also used medically, for example for excessive underarm sweating and jaw clenching. Details are on the [Botox](/services/aesthetic-treatments/botox/) page.

## How do fillers work?

Dermal fillers are soft gels injected under the skin with a fine needle or blunt cannula. Most are hyaluronic acid, a substance found naturally in skin that holds water. Filler replaces volume, supports a fold or shapes a feature. The result is visible at once, settles over two weeks and lasts months.

Fillers treat **static lines and volume loss**: flattened cheeks, the folds from nose to mouth, lines below the mouth corners, thin lips, hollow temples and under-eye hollows in carefully selected people. See [dermal fillers](/services/aesthetic-treatments/dermal-fillers/).

## Which one do I need?

| Your concern | Usually treated with |
|---|---|
| Lines that show when you frown, squint or raise your brows | Botulinum toxin |
| Folds and hollows that are present at rest | Filler |
| Thin or uneven lips | Filler |
| Flat or sagging cheeks | Filler, sometimes with skin tightening |
| Dull, crepey or uneven skin | Neither; skin treatments such as peels, microneedling or laser |
| Excessive underarm sweating | Botulinum toxin |

Many faces need a little of more than one approach, and some concerns are better treated with [skin tightening](/services/aesthetic-treatments/skin-tightening/) or resurfacing. A consultation should start with what bothers you, not with a product.

## What are the risks?

**Botulinum toxin:** small bruises, a headache for a day, and, if the toxin spreads to a neighbouring muscle, a temporarily drooping eyelid or brow or an uneven smile. These pass as the effect wears off.

**Fillers:** swelling, bruising and lumps are the common ones. The rare, serious risk is filler entering or pressing on a blood vessel, which can damage skin or, very rarely, vision. This is why fillers should be injected only by a trained medical practitioner who knows facial anatomy, uses reversible products and has the dissolving enzyme and an emergency plan on hand.

On brown and Black skin, any needle puncture can leave a small dark mark for a few weeks, and people prone to [keloids](/conditions/keloids/) should say so, although keloids from these injections are unusual.

## What should I ask before treatment?

- Who is injecting, and what is their medical qualification?
- Which product is it, and can I see the sealed box?
- Is it reversible? What happens if something goes wrong?
- What result is realistic for my face, and how long will it last?
- What should I avoid before and after?

Avoid "Botox parties", home visits by unlicensed injectors and products bought online. Counterfeit toxin and filler circulate widely.

## Who should not have them?

Neither is given in pregnancy or while breastfeeding. Toxin is avoided in certain nerve and muscle conditions; fillers are avoided over active skin infection and in people with a history of severe allergic reaction to the product. Tell the doctor about cold sores, blood-thinning medicines and previous cosmetic procedures.

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - You are unsure which treatment suits your concern
> - You have had a poor result or a lump after treatment elsewhere
> - After filler you notice severe pain, pale or dusky skin, or any change in vision: this is an emergency
> - You want a plan that also addresses skin tone and texture

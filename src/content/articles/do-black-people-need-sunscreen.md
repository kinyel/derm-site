## What does melanin protect against?

Melanin absorbs and scatters ultraviolet light. Deeply pigmented skin filters about twice as much UVB as light skin and has a built-in protection that researchers estimate at around SPF 13. That is why sunburn and sun-related skin cancers are far less common in people with dark skin, and why wrinkles appear later.

It is partial protection, not complete. People with dark skin still burn after long exposure, and still develop [skin cancer](/conditions/skin-cancer-on-dark-skin/), although most cases arise in areas the sun does not reach.

## Then why bother with sunscreen?

Because the main sun problem in brown and Black skin is pigment, not burning.

- **Dark spots last longer.** Marks from acne, bites and rashes stay for months when exposed to sun. See [hyperpigmentation](/conditions/hyperpigmentation/).
- **Melasma is driven by light.** Without daily protection, no treatment holds. See [melasma treatment](/conditions/melasma/).
- **Visible light matters.** The blue-violet part of ordinary daylight triggers pigment in darker skin tones more than in light skin. Standard sunscreens do not block it; iron oxides in tinted products do.
- **Treatments need it.** Retinoids, acids, peels and lasers make skin more sun-sensitive.
- **Uneven tone and texture.** Years of exposure darken the face compared with the body and roughen the skin.
- **Some conditions flare in sun,** including lupus and certain medicine reactions.

People with albinism have little or no melanin and need rigorous protection from childhood.

## Which sunscreen suits Lagos heat and humidity?

Heat, sweat and humidity make heavy creams slide off and clog pores. Look for:

- **"Broad spectrum", SPF 30 to 50.**
- **A light texture:** gel, fluid, gel-cream or milk; "oil-free" or "non-comedogenic" if you get pimples.
- **A tint** that suits your tone if you have dark spots or melasma, for visible-light protection and no grey film.
- **Water or sweat resistance** if you exercise, swim or work outdoors.
- **Fragrance-free** if your skin is sensitive.

Mineral filters (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) sit on the skin and suit sensitive skin; chemical filters absorb into the surface and tend to be invisible. Both protect. The right sunscreen is the one you will wear every day.

Buy from a reliable pharmacy, check for a NAFDAC number and expiry date, and do not leave the bottle in a hot car, where filters break down.

## How much, and how often?

- **Face and neck:** two finger-lengths of product, or about half a teaspoon.
- **When:** every morning, as the last step of skincare and before make-up. Let it settle for a few minutes.
- **Reapply** every two hours when outdoors, and after swimming or heavy sweating. For office days, once in the morning and again before you travel home is practical.
- **Do not forget** the ears, the back of the neck, the backs of the hands and a bald or thinning scalp.

A moisturiser or foundation with SPF helps but is rarely applied thickly enough to deliver the number on the label.

## What else protects skin?

Sunscreen is one layer. Add shade between 10 am and 4 pm, a wide-brimmed hat, sunglasses, long sleeves in a tight weave, and an umbrella. Car windows block burning rays but let UVA through, so drivers often have more pigment on the window side.

## Common worries

- **"It makes me break out."** Change the texture, not the habit. Gels and fluids suit oily skin.
- **"It makes my face look grey."** Use a tinted or clear formula.
- **"I am dark; I never burn."** The goal is even tone and healthy skin, not only avoiding burns.
- **"It is expensive."** Protecting the face, neck and hands uses little product. It costs less than treating the marks it prevents.

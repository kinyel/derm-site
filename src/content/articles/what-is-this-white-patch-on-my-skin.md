## Why do pale patches stand out on dark skin?

Any loss of pigment contrasts strongly with brown and Black skin, so patches that would go unnoticed on light skin are obvious. The contrast also causes alarm, because of the stigma attached to vitiligo and leprosy. In practice, the frequent causes are minor and treatable.

## What are the common causes?

**Pityriasis versicolor.** Many small, pale, slightly scaly patches on the upper chest, back, neck, upper arms and, in young people, the face. Scratching a patch raises a fine dust-like scale. Caused by yeast in hot, humid weather; widely called "eczema" in Nigeria. See [pityriasis versicolor](/conditions/pityriasis-versicolor/).

**Pityriasis alba.** Round or oval, poorly defined pale patches with a little dry scale on the cheeks and upper arms of children and teenagers. It is a mild form of eczema, more visible after sun, and fades with moisturiser and time.

**Marks after inflammation.** When eczema, a burn, a graze, [seborrhoeic dermatitis](/conditions/seborrhoeic-dermatitis-and-dandruff/) or a nappy rash heals, the skin can stay lighter for months. See [post-inflammatory hypopigmentation](/conditions/post-inflammatory-hypopigmentation/).

**Vitiligo.** The immune system destroys pigment cells, leaving chalk-white patches. See [vitiligo treatment](/conditions/vitiligo/).

**Small white dots on the shins and forearms.** Idiopathic guttate hypomelanosis: flat, 2 to 5 millimetre white spots in adults over 40, from ageing and sun. Harmless.

**Product-related lightening.** Steroid creams, steroid injections and some chemicals in rubber, glues and hair dyes can remove pigment where they touch.

## What are the less common causes?

- **Leprosy (Hansen's disease).** One or a few pale or coppery patches with **reduced feeling**: you cannot sense light touch, heat or a pinprick in the patch. There may be loss of hair and sweating in it, or numbness in the hands and feet. It is curable with free multi-drug treatment, and early treatment prevents nerve damage.
- **Naevus depigmentosus.** A pale birthmark, present from infancy, that stays the same.
- **Lichen sclerosus.** White, thin, itchy skin, usually on the genitals.
- **Certain skin lymphomas.** Long-standing pale, slightly scaly patches on covered areas that do not respond to treatment; diagnosed by biopsy.

## How can I check a patch at home?

1. **Colour.** Is it completely white, or just lighter than the skin around it?
2. **Scale.** Scratch gently. Does a fine powder appear?
3. **Edge.** Sharp or blurred?
4. **Feeling.** With eyes closed, can you feel a wisp of cotton wool and tell warm from cool inside the patch?
5. **Site and number.** One or many? On the face, trunk, hands, genitals?
6. **History.** Was there a rash, injury or cream there before?

| Finding | Most likely |
|---|---|
| Lighter, fine scale, many small patches on trunk | Pityriasis versicolor |
| Lighter, blurred, on a child's cheeks | Pityriasis alba |
| Lighter, where a rash or wound was | Post-inflammatory change |
| Pure white, sharp edge, smooth | Vitiligo |
| Pale with loss of feeling | Needs urgent assessment for leprosy |

## What should I not do?

- Do not apply "triple action" or steroid creams; they can lighten skin further and hide the diagnosis.
- Do not scrub the patch or apply bleach, lime or battery fluid.
- Do not use lightening cream on the surrounding skin to "match".
- Do not wait months on a completely white patch; vitiligo responds best early.

## How is it diagnosed and treated?

A dermatologist examines the patch in good light and under a Wood's lamp, which makes true white stand out, tests sensation, and may take a [skin scraping](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/fungal-tests/) or a small [biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/). Treatment follows the cause: antifungal wash, moisturiser, prescription creams, or [phototherapy for vitiligo](/services/clinical-dermatology/phototherapy/).

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - A patch is completely white, or patches are increasing
> - A patch is numb, or you have numbness in your hands or feet
> - White patches are on the genitals or inside the mouth
> - Antifungal treatment has not helped after four weeks
> - A child's patches are spreading or upsetting them

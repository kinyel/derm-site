## Are the braids too tight?

Tension is the first thing to rule out. Pulling inflames the hair follicles, and inflamed follicles itch, ache and then form bumps. Signs that a style is too tight:

- Soreness or headache on the first night
- Skin lifted into little tents at the hairline
- Bumps or pimples around the base of the braids
- Needing painkillers to sleep

If any of these apply, the fix is to loosen or remove the braids, not to medicate the itch. Repeated tight styles lead to [traction alopecia](/conditions/traction-alopecia/), hair loss along the hairline that becomes permanent.

## Is it the synthetic hair?

Synthetic braiding and weave fibres are often finished with an alkaline coating. On some scalps this causes itching, burning and small bumps within a day or two, worst at the nape and hairline where the fibre rests on the skin. Clues: it happens with every install, starts quickly, and eases when the hair is removed.

What helps: pre-washing the hair before installation, choosing a different brand or human hair, and keeping extension hair off the hairline.

## Is it build-up, sweat or dandruff?

A scalp left unwashed for four to eight weeks collects oil, sweat, dead skin and product. In Lagos heat, the yeast that lives on every scalp thrives in that mix and triggers [seborrhoeic dermatitis](/conditions/seborrhoeic-dermatitis-and-dandruff/): itching, greasy flakes and, on dark skin, lighter or darker patches along the hairline and behind the ears. Heavy scalp grease and oils feed it.

Itch that begins two to three weeks into a style, with flakes, is usually this.

## Could it be an allergy?

An allergic [contact dermatitis](/conditions/contact-dermatitis/) causes intense itch, swelling or weeping. Common culprits are:

- hair dye (including dye applied before braiding)
- relaxer left on too long, or applied shortly before braiding
- glue and tape for wigs and lace fronts
- fragranced sprays, braid sheens and edge gels
- nickel in hair accessories

Swelling of the face, eyelids or neck after a new product is a reason to see a doctor quickly. [Patch testing](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/patch-testing/) identifies the ingredient.

## What else could it be?

- **Dry scalp** from frequent use of alcohol-based sprays.
- **Scalp psoriasis:** thick, well-defined scaly plaques.
- **Fungal infection (tinea capitis):** mainly in children; scaly patches with broken hairs and swollen glands.
- **Head lice:** uncommon in tightly coiled hair but possible.
- **[Scalp folliculitis](/conditions/scalp-folliculitis/):** recurrent pus spots.
- **Scarring alopecia:** itching, burning or tenderness at the crown with thinning needs early assessment.

## How do I soothe an itchy scalp with braids in?

1. **Wash.** Dilute shampoo in an applicator bottle, apply along the partings, massage with fingertips, and rinse until the water runs clear. Every one to two weeks.
2. **Use a medicated shampoo** containing ketoconazole or zinc pyrithione on the scalp for five minutes before rinsing if you have flakes.
3. **Dry fully.** Damp braids against the scalp encourage yeast and odour. Use a hooded or handheld dryer on cool.
4. **Go light on products.** A water-based spray or a few drops of light oil on the lengths, not grease on the scalp.
5. **Cool the itch** with a clean, cool cloth.
6. **Limit the time** a style stays in to six to eight weeks, and rest the hair between installs.

## What should I not do?

- Scratch with rat-tail combs, pens or fingernails
- Apply methylated spirit, menthol rubs or steroid creams of unknown strength
- Take painkillers to tolerate tight braids
- Re-braid immediately over a sore, bumpy or flaky scalp

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - Itching continues after the style is removed and the scalp is washed
> - There are bumps, pus, crusts or sores
> - The hairline or crown is thinning
> - The scalp burns, or your face or neck swelled after a hair product
> - A child has scaly patches or hair loss

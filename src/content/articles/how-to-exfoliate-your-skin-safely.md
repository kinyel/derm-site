## What does exfoliation do?

The outer layer of skin sheds dead cells continuously. When shedding slows or oil glues the cells together, the surface looks dull and ashy, pores clog and ingrown hairs form. Exfoliation loosens and removes that outer layer.

It does not make skin cleaner, lighter or "new". It smooths the surface. Done too hard or too often, it strips the barrier that keeps water in and irritants out.

## What are the two types?

**Physical exfoliation** removes cells by rubbing: scrubs, brushes, sponges, loofahs, exfoliating gloves and nets.

**Chemical exfoliation** dissolves the bonds between dead cells:

- **AHAs (alpha hydroxy acids):** glycolic, lactic and mandelic acid. They work on the surface and suit dry, dull or marked skin. Mandelic and lactic acids have larger molecules and are gentler, which makes them good first choices for dark skin.
- **BHA (salicylic acid):** dissolves in oil, so it clears pores. Suits oily and acne-prone skin and ingrown hairs.
- **PHAs (such as gluconolactone):** the mildest, for sensitive skin.
- **Enzymes** from fruit such as papaya: gentle, but can irritate in some people.

## Why does brown and Black skin need extra care?

Melanin-rich skin answers irritation with pigment. A scrub that leaves light skin pink for an hour can leave dark skin with brown patches for months. Daily scrubbing with a rough sponge is a common reason for a face or body that keeps getting darker and for dark knuckles, elbows and knees. Gentle, infrequent exfoliation gives the smoothness without the marks.

## A safe routine for the face

1. **Start with clean, dry skin at night.**
2. **Apply a leave-on exfoliant** in a low strength. Avoid the eyelids, nostrils and lips.
3. **Begin once a week.** If your skin is comfortable after three weeks, move to twice.
4. **Moisturise** afterwards with a plain product.
5. **Wear sunscreen every morning.** Acids make skin more sensitive to sun.
6. **Do not combine** an exfoliant with a retinoid, benzoyl peroxide or vitamin C on the same night until you know your skin tolerates each.

Slight tingling for a minute is normal. Burning is not; rinse off.

## And for the body?

- **Rough arms and thighs** ([keratosis pilaris](/conditions/keratosis-pilaris/)): a lotion with lactic acid or urea after bathing works better than scrubbing.
- **Bikini line, underarms and beard area:** a salicylic or glycolic product two or three times a week helps prevent [ingrown hairs](/conditions/ingrown-hairs/).
- **Feet:** a foot file on damp skin once or twice a week; see [cracked heels](/conditions/cracked-heels/).
- **Knees, elbows and knuckles:** stop scrubbing them. Friction is why they are dark. Use a urea moisturiser.

Wash with your hands or a soft cloth. A daily sponge is already exfoliation.

## How do I know I have overdone it?

- Stinging when you apply ordinary moisturiser
- Tightness and a shiny, "plastic" look
- Flaking, redness or a darker, rough patch
- More breakouts than before

Stop all actives. Use only a gentle cleanser, moisturiser and sunscreen for two to four weeks, then restart at half the frequency.

## Who should not exfoliate?

Skip it on skin with active eczema, a rash, sunburn, open cuts, cold sores or infection, and for a week after waxing, laser or a professional peel. People using prescription retinoids or isotretinoin should ask their dermatologist first. Do not use professional-strength peels bought online; burns and scarring are common.

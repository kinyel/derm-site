## What does harmattan do to skin?

Harmattan is the dry, dusty north-easterly wind that reaches Nigeria from about late November to February. Even on the coast, Lagos humidity falls noticeably, nights are cooler, and fine dust hangs in the air.

Skin holds water in its outer layer with the help of natural oils. When the air is dry, water evaporates from the surface faster than it is replaced. The outer cells shrink and lift, which shows on dark skin as ashiness: a grey-white, flaky look. Tiny cracks form, nerve endings are exposed, and the skin itches and stings. Dust adds irritation, and hot baths, which feel good in cool weather, strip more oil.

## A simple harmattan routine

**Bath**

- Keep it short, five to ten minutes, in lukewarm water.
- Use a mild, fragrance-free cleanser or soap substitute. Skip medicated and antiseptic soaps.
- Wash with your hands or a soft cloth, not a rough sponge.
- Pat dry, leaving the skin slightly damp.

**Moisturise**

- Apply a thick cream or ointment within three minutes of bathing, while the skin is damp.
- Repeat at least once more in the day, and after washing hands.
- Layer if needed: a cream first, then a thin film of petroleum jelly or shea butter on the driest areas such as shins, elbows and heels.

**Face**

- Use a gentle, non-foaming cleanser once or twice a day.
- Choose a richer moisturiser than usual; add a hydrating serum with glycerin or hyaluronic acid underneath if your skin feels tight.
- Reduce exfoliating acids and retinoids to fewer nights if they sting.
- Apply sunscreen every morning.

**Lips**

- Balm many times a day; a thick layer at night.
- Do not lick, peel or bite.

**Hands and feet**

- Hand cream after every wash; cotton gloves overnight for cracked hands.
- For heels, a urea balm at night with socks; see [cracked heels](/conditions/cracked-heels/).

**Hair and scalp**

- Wash regularly; dry scalp is not a reason to stop. Condition every wash.
- Seal moisture into the lengths with a light oil or butter, and cover hair outdoors with a satin-lined scarf or cap.
- Flaking that itches may be [dandruff](/conditions/seborrhoeic-dermatitis-and-dandruff/), which needs medicated shampoo, not oil.

**Inside and around you**

- Drink water through the day. It will not moisturise skin directly, but mild dehydration is common in dry weather.
- Wear long sleeves and trousers in soft cotton, and sunglasses for dust.
- If you use air-conditioning, which dries the air further, moisturise more often. A bowl of water in the room or a humidifier helps at night.

## What about babies and children?

- Bathe once a day, briefly, in lukewarm water with a soap substitute.
- Moisturise the whole body at least twice a day with a fragrance-free ointment or cream.
- Dress in soft cotton layers; avoid wool directly on the skin.
- Protect lips and cheeks with petroleum jelly before going out.
- Do not add antiseptic liquid to bath water.

Children with eczema flare readily in this season; see [baby eczema](/conditions/baby-eczema/).

## If you have a skin condition

- **[Eczema](/conditions/eczema/):** increase moisturiser to three or four times a day and start your prescribed flare treatment at the first sign of itch.
- **Psoriasis:** scales thicken in dry weather; keep up moisturiser and treatment.
- **Acne:** drying treatments may need a lighter schedule and a non-comedogenic moisturiser. Do not swap to heavy butters on the face.
- **Cold sores:** dry, cracked lips and sun trigger [cold sores](/conditions/cold-sores/); use a balm with SPF.
- **Nosebleeds and dry eyes:** a little petroleum jelly inside the nostrils, and lubricating eye drops, help.

## What should I avoid?

- Long, hot baths and bucket baths with very hot water
- Medicated soaps, antiseptic liquids and scrubs
- Fragranced body lotions on cracked skin
- Licking lips or peeling flakes
- Light lotions that vanish in minutes on very dry skin
- Lightening creams, which thin skin and make cracking worse

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - Skin stays itchy, cracked or sore despite two weeks of regular moisturising
> - Cracks are bleeding, weeping or crusted
> - A child is scratching at night or has rough, dark patches
> - Your eczema or psoriasis is not controlled by your usual treatment
> - Itch is all over with no visible rash

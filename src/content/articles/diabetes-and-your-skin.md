## Why does diabetes affect the skin?

Sugar that stays high in the blood attaches to proteins, stiffens collagen, narrows small blood vessels, damages nerves and slows the cells that fight infection. Skin depends on all of these. The result is skin that is drier, heals more slowly, catches infections more easily and, in the feet, cannot feel injury.

## Ten signs to watch

**1. A dark, velvety neck, armpits or groin.** Acanthosis nigricans is caused by high insulin acting on skin cells. It looks like a stain that will not wash off and feels thicker than normal skin. See [dark underarms, inner thighs, knees and neck](/conditions/dark-underarms-inner-thighs-knees-and-neck/).

**2. Many skin tags.** Clusters of [skin tags](/conditions/skin-tags/) on the neck, eyelids and armpits accompany insulin resistance.

**3. Repeated boils and styes.** [Boils](/conditions/boils-and-abscesses/) that keep returning, or one very large one with several openings, should prompt a sugar test.

**4. Fungal and yeast infections.** Itchy rashes under the breasts, in the groin and between the toes, vaginal thrush, and soreness of the foreskin are frequent with high sugar. See [athlete's foot](/conditions/athletes-foot/) and [ringworm](/conditions/ringworm-and-fungal-skin-infections/).

**5. Itching.** Dry skin, yeast and poor circulation cause itch, often on the lower legs. See [chronic itch](/conditions/chronic-itch/).

**6. Shin spots.** Round, light brown, slightly scaly patches on the front of the legs (diabetic dermopathy) are harmless and need no treatment, but point to long-standing diabetes.

**7. Dry, cracked feet.** Nerve damage reduces sweating. Cracks at the heels and between the toes let bacteria in.

**8. Numbness, tingling or burning in the feet.** This is nerve damage, and it removes the warning pain of a blister or stone in the shoe.

**9. Sores that heal slowly.** A cut, blister or burn that has not closed in two weeks, especially on the foot, needs attention. See [diabetic foot ulcers](/conditions/diabetic-foot-ulcers/).

**10. Thick, tight or waxy skin** on the backs of the hands and fingers, with stiff joints, in long-standing diabetes.

Rarer signs include sudden crops of yellowish bumps (from very high blood fats), blisters on the feet without injury, and shiny yellow-brown plaques on the shins.

## What these look like on brown and Black skin

Darkening and thickening are easy to see; redness is not. Infection on dark skin shows as swelling, warmth, shine and a deeper brown or purple tone. Compare one foot or leg with the other, and trust warmth and swelling over colour.

## I have some of these signs. What next?

Ask for a blood sugar test: a fasting glucose or an HbA1c. Testing is quick, and type 2 diabetes found early can often be controlled with food, activity, weight loss and tablets. Family history, excess weight around the waist, high blood pressure and diabetes in pregnancy raise the risk further.

## Skin care if you have diabetes

- Keep blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol within your targets.
- Wash with lukewarm water and mild soap; dry skin folds and between the toes.
- Moisturise daily, but not between the toes.
- Check your feet every day and never walk barefoot.
- Treat cuts at once: clean, cover, and watch.
- Do not soak feet in hot water or cut calluses yourself.
- Wear well-fitting shoes with socks.

> [!DERM] When to see a doctor
> - You have a dark velvety neck, many skin tags or repeated infections and have never been tested
> - Any foot sore, blister or crack has not healed within a week
> - A foot or leg becomes swollen, hot or painful: seek care the same day
> - Itching or a rash persists despite moisturiser
> - You have diabetes and have not had a foot check in the past year

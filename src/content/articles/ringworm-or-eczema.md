## How do they compare side by side?

| | Ringworm | Eczema |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Fungal infection | Inflammation; dry, sensitive skin barrier |
| Shape | Ring or arc | Irregular patch, or coin-shaped in discoid eczema |
| Edge | Raised, scaly, sharply defined | Blurred, fades into normal skin |
| Centre | Clearer, flatter | Same as the rest of the patch |
| How many | One or a few, uneven | Often several, roughly symmetrical |
| Common sites | Trunk, groin, feet, scalp (children), face | Creases of elbows and knees, hands, neck, eyelids |
| Itch | Mild to moderate | Often severe, worse at night |
| Spreads to others | Yes | No |
| History | Contact with an infected person, pet, gym or barber | Dry skin, asthma, hay fever or eczema since childhood |
| Responds to | Antifungal | Moisturiser and anti-inflammatory cream |

## What does each look like on brown and Black skin?

Neither looks red. **Ringworm** shows as a ring whose border is darker brown or grey, bumpy and scaly, while the middle may look almost normal or slightly darker. It grows slowly outwards over weeks. In the groin it forms a half-moon that advances down the inner thigh.

**Eczema** shows as dry, rough areas that are darker, purplish or ashy, with small bumps around the hair follicles and thickened, lined skin where it has been scratched for a long time. After it settles, lighter or darker marks remain.

## Which other rashes get confused with these?

- **[Pityriasis versicolor](/conditions/pityriasis-versicolor/):** many small pale or dark patches with fine scale on the chest, back and neck; not ring-shaped, barely itchy.
- **[Psoriasis](/conditions/psoriasis/):** thick, well-defined plaques with heavy silvery-grey scale on the elbows, knees and scalp.
- **Pityriasis rosea:** a single larger oval patch followed by many small ones on the trunk.
- **[Contact dermatitis](/conditions/contact-dermatitis/):** a rash in the shape of whatever touched the skin.
- **Granuloma annulare:** smooth rings with no scale at all.

## Why does the right diagnosis matter?

Because the treatments are opposite. Steroid cream calms eczema and feeds ringworm. Antifungal cream cures ringworm and does nothing for eczema. Mixed "triple action" creams, which contain a steroid, an antifungal and an antibiotic, blur both pictures: ringworm treated this way becomes widespread and resistant, and eczema treated this way thins the skin.

Ringworm that has been altered by steroid is now one of the commonest reasons for a difficult rash in Nigerian clinics.

## What can I do at home?

- If the patch is a clear ring with a scaly edge, a plain antifungal cream from a pharmacy, applied to the patch and a margin of normal skin for the full time on the pack, is reasonable.
- If the skin is dry and itchy in the creases, start with a fragrance-free moisturiser several times a day and a gentle soap substitute.
- Do not use a mixed cream "to cover both".
- If there is no improvement in two weeks, stop guessing and have it examined.

## How does a dermatologist tell?

By the pattern, a dermatoscope and a [skin scraping](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/fungal-tests/): a few flakes from the edge are examined under the microscope for fungal threads, and cultured when needed. Before the test, stop any antifungal for a few days if you can, so the result is reliable. A [skin biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/) is used occasionally for rashes that fit neither.

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - A patch has not improved after two weeks of treatment
> - The rash is spreading or has changed shape after a mixed or steroid cream
> - It is on the scalp, face, groin or nails
> - Several family members have similar patches
> - A child has a scaly patch with hair loss

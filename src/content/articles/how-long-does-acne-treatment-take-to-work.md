## Why is acne treatment slow?

A pimple that appears today began as a microscopic blockage four to eight weeks ago. Treatment stops new blockages from forming; it does little for those already on their way. You therefore see the effect of this week's treatment in a month or two. Expecting results in days leads people to abandon treatments that were working.

## What should I expect, week by week?

**Weeks 1 to 2.** Dryness, mild peeling or stinging as the skin adjusts to a retinoid or benzoyl peroxide. No visible improvement yet. Use moisturiser and apply every other night if needed.

**Weeks 2 to 6.** Possible purging: a few more spots than usual in your typical areas. Oiliness starts to settle.

**Weeks 6 to 8.** Fewer new spots. Existing ones heal faster. This is the first fair checkpoint.

**Weeks 8 to 12.** Clear improvement for most people: often half or more of the spots gone. Dark marks from earlier spots are still visible and now become the main concern.

**Months 3 to 6.** Maximum benefit from the treatment. Marks fade steadily if sunscreen is used.

**After 6 months.** Maintenance.

## How long does each treatment take?

| Treatment | First change | Full effect |
|---|---|---|
| Benzoyl peroxide | 2 to 4 weeks | 8 to 12 weeks |
| Topical retinoid (adapalene, tretinoin) | 6 to 8 weeks | 12 weeks or more |
| Azelaic acid | 4 to 8 weeks | 12 to 16 weeks |
| Topical antibiotic with benzoyl peroxide | 2 to 6 weeks | 12 weeks |
| Oral antibiotic (with a topical) | 4 to 6 weeks | 12 weeks; then stopped |
| Hormonal treatment for women | 2 to 3 months | 6 months |
| Isotretinoin | 4 to 8 weeks | End of a 5 to 8 month course |

These are typical ranges, not promises; skin varies.

## Why might treatment seem not to work?

- **Too little time.** Stopping at three weeks.
- **Spot-treating.** Applying to individual pimples instead of the whole area.
- **Irregular use.** Three nights a week will not do what nightly use does.
- **Too many products.** Scrubs, toners and several actives together irritate the skin and cause more marks.
- **A trigger is still present:** a steroid or lightening cream, hair pomade, heavy oils, a supplement.
- **The wrong diagnosis.** Fungal folliculitis, [rosacea](/conditions/rosacea/), steroid-induced acne and [shaving bumps](/conditions/razor-bumps/) do not respond to standard acne treatment.
- **The acne is too severe** for creams alone and needs tablets.

## How do I get through the first month?

1. Start low and slow: a pea-sized amount for the whole face, every second or third night, building up.
2. Moisturise before and after a retinoid if your skin is dry.
3. Wear sunscreen every morning.
4. Do not pick.
5. Take a photograph on day one, then every four weeks. Compare photographs, not memories.

## When should the plan change?

A fair trial is twelve weeks of correct, regular use. At that point a dermatologist will step up treatment if there has been little change: a different retinoid, the addition of a tablet, hormonal treatment for women with a hormonal pattern, or isotretinoin for severe, scarring or resistant acne. Do not wait twelve weeks if you are developing scars, deep painful lumps or dark marks that upset you; earlier, stronger treatment prevents permanent change.

Once acne is controlled, attention turns to what it left behind: see [hyperpigmentation](/conditions/hyperpigmentation/) and [acne scars](/conditions/acne-scars/).

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - No improvement after twelve weeks of regular treatment
> - New scars or deep, painful nodules at any stage
> - Severe burning, swelling or a rash from a product
> - Acne that returned quickly after a course of tablets
> - Low mood because of your skin

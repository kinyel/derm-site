## What does a peel do?

A chemical peel is an acid solution applied to the skin for a set time to loosen and shed the outer cells in a controlled way. It clears blocked pores, lifts pigment sitting in the surface, smooths texture and helps prescription creams work better.

Peels are grouped by depth:

- **Superficial:** the outer layer (epidermis) only. Mild flaking for a few days.
- **Medium:** into the upper dermis. Several days of crusting and peeling.
- **Deep:** well into the dermis. Weeks of healing.

## Why can a peel darken brown and Black skin?

Because pigment cells in dark skin react strongly to injury. Any inflammation, including a controlled one, can switch them into overproduction. If the injury is small and the skin has been prepared, the result is brighter, more even skin. If the injury is too deep or the skin is already irritated, the result is post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation: brown patches in the shape of the peeled area, lasting months. Deeper injury can do the opposite and leave pale patches or scars.

## What makes darkening more likely?

- A medium or deep peel
- High-strength acid left on too long or applied in many layers
- No preparation of the skin beforehand
- Peeling over active eczema, a rash, sunburn or broken skin
- Retinoids, scrubs, waxing or threading in the days just before
- Sun exposure or heat in the two weeks afterwards
- Picking or scrubbing the flaking skin
- Recent use of bleaching or steroid creams, which leave the skin thin and reactive
- An operator with little experience of dark skin

## How is a peel made safe for dark skin?

1. **Diagnosis first.** Dark marks, [melasma](/conditions/melasma/), acne and product damage are peeled differently, and some should not be peeled at all.
2. **Priming.** For two to four weeks beforehand you use sunscreen daily and a prescribed cream that calms pigment cells and evens the skin's response.
3. **A test area** if your skin is reactive.
4. **The right acid at the right strength,** starting low and increasing over the course according to how your skin responds.
5. **Timed application and neutralisation.**
6. **Aftercare:** gentle cleanser, bland moisturiser, strict sunscreen, no actives until peeling stops.

## What can peels treat on dark skin?

- Dark marks left by acne and other inflammation; see [hyperpigmentation](/conditions/hyperpigmentation/)
- Active [acne](/conditions/acne/) with blackheads and oiliness (salicylic acid)
- Melasma, as an addition to creams and sunscreen
- Dull, rough texture and razor bump marks
- Mild [acne scars](/conditions/acne-scars/), usually combined with [microneedling](/services/aesthetic-treatments/microneedling/) or other procedures

Peels do not lighten your natural skin colour, and should not be offered for that purpose.

## What does recovery look like?

- **Day 0:** tightness and warmth for a few hours.
- **Days 2 to 5:** fine flaking, sometimes slight temporary darkening of marks before they lift.
- **Day 7:** smoother, brighter skin.

Call the clinic if you have blisters, crusts, increasing pain or swelling.

## Who should wait or avoid peels?

People with active cold sores, infection or eczema in the area; recent isotretinoin use (timing is advised by your dermatologist); pregnancy for certain acids; a history of [keloid scars](/conditions/keloids/) for anything beyond superficial peels; and anyone unable to avoid sun for two weeks.

> - You have melasma, or marks that have not responded to creams
> - You have used lightening or steroid creams and want to treat uneven tone

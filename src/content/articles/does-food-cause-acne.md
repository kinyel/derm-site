## What actually causes acne?

Four things combine inside the pore: hormones increase oil production, dead skin cells block the opening, bacteria multiply in the trapped oil, and the skin becomes inflamed. Genes largely decide how strongly your skin reacts. Food can nudge the first step, hormones and oil, which is how diet enters the picture.

## What is the evidence on sugar and refined carbohydrates?

This is the strongest link. Foods that raise blood sugar quickly, known as high glycaemic index (GI) foods, cause a surge of insulin and a related hormone, IGF-1. Both stimulate oil glands. In controlled trials, young people who switched to a low-GI diet for ten to twelve weeks had fewer spots than those who did not.

High-GI foods common in Lagos: soft drinks and malt drinks, fruit juice with added sugar, white bread, pastries, doughnuts and puff-puff, sweets, instant noodles, and large portions of white rice or refined swallow.

Lower-GI choices: beans and moi-moi, unripe plantain, oats, brown rice or ofada rice, whole fruit, vegetables, nuts, fish, eggs, and swallow made from whole grains in moderate portions with plenty of soup and vegetables.

## What about milk and dairy?

Several large observational studies link milk, particularly skimmed milk, with more acne in teenagers and young adults. The effect is modest, and yoghurt and cheese show little or no link. Milk naturally contains hormones and raises insulin, which may explain it. Whey protein supplements, a concentrated milk product, are a clearer trigger.

If you drink a lot of milk and your acne is stubborn, a trial of cutting down for two to three months is reasonable. Keep up calcium and protein from other foods.

## Which food beliefs are not supported?

- **Oily and fried food:** no proven link through eating.
- **Groundnuts and other nuts:** no proven link.
- **Pepper and spicy food:** no link with acne; it can trigger flushing in [rosacea](/conditions/rosacea/), which looks similar.
- **Eggs, meat, beans:** no link.
- **"Dirty blood" or the need to "flush the system":** not how acne works. Herbal purges and bitters do not treat acne and can harm the liver and kidneys.

## Are any foods helpful?

Diets rich in vegetables, fruit, whole grains and oily fish are associated with less acne in some studies, probably through lower insulin and less inflammation. Zinc and omega-3 supplements show small benefits in limited trials. None is a treatment by itself.

## How do I test whether a food affects me?

1. Keep your skincare and treatment the same.
2. Keep a simple diary of what you eat and how your skin looks for four weeks.
3. If a pattern appears, reduce that one food for eight to twelve weeks. Acne changes slowly; a week tells you nothing.
4. Judge by photographs taken in the same light.
5. Do not cut out several food groups at once. Strict diets cause more harm than acne does, particularly in teenagers.

## Where does diet fit in treatment?

Diet is a supporting player. A person with moderate acne who eats perfectly will still have acne without treatment, and most people who treat their acne properly clear without changing what they eat. Proven treatments act directly on the pore: retinoids, benzoyl peroxide, and for more severe acne, prescribed tablets. Other things that matter as much as food:

- steroid and lightening creams, a major cause of acne in Nigeria
- heavy hair oils and pomades
- picking
- stress and poor sleep
- stopping treatment too early; see [How long does acne treatment take to work?](/blog/how-long-does-acne-treatment-take-to-work/)

> [!DERM] When to see a dermatologist
> - Acne is leaving dark marks or scars
> - You have changed your diet for three months without benefit
> - Pimples are deep, painful or on the chest and back as well
> - You are restricting food because of your skin and losing weight
> - Acne began after a supplement, cream or new medicine

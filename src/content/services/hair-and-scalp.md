## Hair loss treatment for women: start with the cause

Most women who come to the clinic have tried oils, supplements and new stylists before they see a doctor. The reason these often fail is that "hair loss" is not one condition. In Lagos, four causes account for most of it.

**[Traction alopecia](/conditions/traction-alopecia/).** Thinning at the edges and temples from styles that pull: tight braids, cornrows, weaves, glued wigs and sleek ponytails. It is the most common cause we see, and it is preventable.

**[Female pattern hair loss](/conditions/female-pattern-hair-loss/).** Gradual thinning over the crown with a widening parting, while the front hairline stays. It runs in families and progresses slowly without treatment.

**[Telogen effluvium](/conditions/telogen-effluvium/).** Handfuls of hair shed from all over the scalp two to three months after childbirth, illness, surgery, a crash diet or severe stress. Low iron and thyroid disease are common hidden triggers.

**Scarring alopecia.** Inflammation destroys the follicle and replaces it with scar. Central centrifugal cicatricial alopecia, which starts at the crown and spreads outwards, mainly affects women of African descent. Hair lost to scarring does not regrow, so early diagnosis matters most here.

Two or more of these often occur together, which is why examination comes before treatment.

## How hair loss is diagnosed

The dermatologist asks when the loss began, how you style and treat your hair, about your periods, pregnancies, diet, illnesses and medicines, and whether relatives have thinning hair. The scalp is examined with [trichoscopy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/trichoscopy/), a magnified view that shows miniaturised hairs, broken hairs, scale, redness and lost follicle openings. A gentle hair pull test shows whether shedding is active.

Blood tests look for low iron, thyroid disease and, where the signs point to it, hormone changes. A small scalp biopsy is taken when scarring is suspected.

Come with your hair loose and without a wig or weave if you can, so that the whole scalp can be seen.

## Treatment options at the clinic

Treatment is matched to the diagnosis and usually combines several of these:

- **Stopping the damage.** Looser styles, breaks between installs, no glue on the hairline, gentler relaxing and heat. For traction and breakage this is the treatment.
- **Treating the scalp.** Medicated shampoos and lotions for [dandruff and seborrhoeic dermatitis](/conditions/seborrhoeic-dermatitis-and-dandruff/), antifungal treatment for scalp ringworm, and anti-inflammatory treatment for scarring conditions.
- **Medical regrowth treatment.** Regrowth lotion or foam applied to the scalp, and tablets where suitable. These need three to six months before the result can be judged, and they work only while you continue them.
- **Correcting what the blood tests show,** such as iron or thyroid treatment.
- **Steroid injections into the scalp** for [alopecia areata](/conditions/alopecia-areata/) and some scarring conditions.
- **[PRP](/services/hair-and-scalp/prp-for-hair-loss/) and scalp microneedling** as additions to medical treatment for pattern hair loss.
- **[Hair transplant](/services/hair-and-scalp/hair-transplant/)** for stable pattern loss or scarred areas, after assessment of the donor hair.

## Men's hair loss and other scalp conditions

[Male pattern hair loss](/conditions/male-pattern-hair-loss/) follows a recognisable course: a receding hairline and thinning at the crown. It responds best to treatment started early. Men also come to the clinic for bumps at the back of the scalp, [razor bumps](/conditions/razor-bumps/) along the hairline and scalp folliculitis.

Itching, flaking and soreness are scalp skin conditions, and each has its own treatment. An itchy scalp after braiding has several possible causes, covered in our article on [scalp itching after braids](/blog/why-does-my-scalp-itch-after-braiding/).

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist soon if
> - a child has a scaly bald patch, with or without swollen glands in the neck (possible scalp ringworm)
> - your scalp is painful, burning or has pus-filled bumps
> - bald patches are smooth and shiny, with no visible pores
> - hair loss comes with weight change, missed periods, new facial hair or tiredness

## Will it grow back?

It depends on whether the follicles are still alive. Shedding and early traction usually recover. Pattern hair loss can be slowed, and part of what was lost can be regained, but treatment has to continue. Scarred areas do not regrow, though the spread can be stopped. Our article [Will my hair grow back?](/blog/will-my-hair-grow-back/) goes through each type.

## From prescription to routine

A prescription works only if it is used correctly. When your medicines are dispensed, the pharmacy team goes through each one with you: which goes on first, how much to use, whether it goes on the whole area or only the spots, what to expect in the first weeks and what to do if your skin stings or peels.

Many skin medicines irritate at first. Retinoids and acne treatments often cause dryness and flaking for the first few weeks, which is the point at which many people stop. Knowing this in advance, and how to ease it, makes it more likely that the treatment gets a fair trial.

## Types of prescription treatment

- **Acne treatments:** creams and gels that unblock pores and reduce bacteria, and tablets for more severe [acne](/conditions/acne/).
- **Antibiotics,** applied to the skin or taken by mouth, for bacterial infections and some inflammatory conditions.
- **Antifungals** for ringworm, pityriasis versicolor, athlete's foot and nail infection.
- **Corticosteroids,** in the strength suited to the body site, for eczema, psoriasis and other inflammation.
- **Retinoids** for acne, dark marks and sun damage. These are not used in pregnancy.
- **Pigmentation medicines** for melasma and dark spots, used for a set period under supervision.
- **Hair growth medicines** for pattern hair loss.

We do not list doses or product names here. The right one depends on the person, and the label on your prescription is the instruction to follow.

## Medical skincare

Supporting skincare makes prescriptions work better and keeps a condition settled afterwards. The pharmacy stocks gentle cleansers, moisturisers for dry and eczema-prone skin, non-greasy sunscreens that suit darker skin tones, medicated shampoos for dandruff and scalp conditions, nail and scar treatments, and soothing products for use after peels, laser and minor procedures.

A routine for most people is three steps: a mild cleanser, a moisturiser and a sunscreen in the morning. Treatment products are added one at a time.

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Creams with no ingredient list, or sold as "lightening", "toning" or "triple action".
> - Mixing several active products at once; irritation causes dark marks on brown and Black skin.
> - Using someone else's prescription, or continuing a steroid cream beyond the time prescribed.
> - Stopping a prescribed treatment abruptly without asking. Some need to be reduced gradually.

## Sun protection counselling

Sun protection is part of treating melasma, dark marks, vitiligo, lupus and skin after procedures. Counselling covers how much sunscreen to apply, how often to reapply in Lagos heat, shade and clothing, and how to protect children and people with albinism.

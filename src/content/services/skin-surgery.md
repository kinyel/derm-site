## What can be treated

Most lumps and bumps are harmless and need treatment only if they catch, bleed, hurt or bother you. A dermatologist first works out what the growth is, because that decides how it should be removed and whether it needs testing.

- **[Moles](/services/skin-surgery/mole-removal/)** are shaved level with the skin or cut out and stitched, depending on depth and whether they need laboratory examination.
- **[Cysts and lipomas](/services/skin-surgery/cyst-and-lipoma-removal/)** sit under the skin and are removed through a small cut, taking the whole sac or lump so it does not return.
- **[Skin tags](/conditions/skin-tags/)** and the small dark spots of [dermatosis papulosa nigra](/conditions/dermatosis-papulosa-nigra/) are snipped or treated with fine electrosurgery.
- **[Warts](/conditions/warts/)** are frozen, treated with solutions or removed.
- **[Keloid treatment](/conditions/keloids/)** relies mainly on injections, pressure and silicone; cutting one out without other treatment usually brings back a larger one.
- **[Scars](/services/skin-surgery/scar-revision/)** that are wide, tight or badly placed can be revised.
- **[Boils and abscesses](/conditions/boils-and-abscesses/)** that contain pus are opened and drained.

## The techniques, in plain words

**Excision** cuts out the growth and closes the wound with stitches. **Shave removal** takes a raised growth off level with the skin. **Curettage** scrapes away soft growths. **Electrosurgery and electrocautery** use a fine heated tip to remove tissue and seal small vessels. **Cryotherapy** freezes tissue with liquid nitrogen. **Chemical cautery** uses a solution to destroy a small growth or stop bleeding. Larger wounds are closed with a **flap** of nearby skin or a **graft** taken from another site.

On dark skin, freezing and burning can leave a pale mark, because pigment cells are sensitive to cold and heat. The dermatologist takes this into account when choosing the method, especially on the face.

## Before and after

Tell us about blood thinners, diabetes, allergies, a pacemaker and any tendency to keloids. Eat normally beforehand. Afterwards you are given written wound care instructions: how long to keep the dressing dry, how to clean the area, when stitches come out and what is normal. Stitches on the face usually stay for about a week and those on the body for up to two.

> [!DERM] Contact the clinic after a procedure if
> - bleeding soaks through the dressing and does not stop after 15 minutes of firm pressure
> - pain, swelling, heat or redness increase after the second day
> - pus appears, the wound opens or you develop a fever
> - the scar starts to thicken, itch or rise in the weeks that follow

Wounds that are slow to heal are looked after by the clinic's [wound care](/services/wound-care/) service.

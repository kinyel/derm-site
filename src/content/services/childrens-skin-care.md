  - q: Are white patches on my child's face vitiligo?
    a: "Usually not. ## Why children need their own skin care

A child's skin is thinner than an adult's, loses water faster and absorbs more of what is put on it. The same cream at the same strength acts more strongly on a baby than on a parent. Conditions also look different at different ages: eczema starts on the cheeks in babies and moves to the elbow and knee creases in older children. A paediatric dermatologist chooses treatments, strengths and amounts for the child's age and weight.

Children's appointments are unhurried. The dermatologist speaks to the child as well as the parent, examines gently and explains each step. A parent or guardian stays throughout.

## What a pediatric dermatologist in Lagos sees most

**Eczema.** The most common long-term skin condition in children. It causes dry, itchy patches that can keep the whole household awake. On brown skin it often looks dark brown, grey or bumpy rather than red. See [baby eczema](/conditions/baby-eczema/) and [eczema](/conditions/eczema/).

**Heat rash and nappy rash.** Lagos heat and humidity block sweat ducts and irritate skin under nappies. Both usually settle with simple changes, but a rash that persists may be a yeast infection or something else. See [heat rash](/conditions/heat-rash/) and [diaper rash](/conditions/diaper-rash/).

**Infections.** Scalp ringworm spreads between children through shared combs, clippers and close play, and is very common in primary school children. Impetigo, [scabies](/conditions/scabies/), [warts](/conditions/warts/) and [molluscum contagiosum](/conditions/molluscum-contagiosum/) also spread easily at home and school.

**Birthmarks.** Most are harmless. A haemangioma that grows quickly, sits near the eye, nose, mouth or nappy area, or breaks down into a sore needs early assessment, because treatment works best in the first months of life.

**Teenage acne.** Early, consistent treatment prevents scars and dark marks, which are harder to treat than the acne itself. See [acne](/conditions/acne/).

## What to avoid

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> - Do not use adult creams, "triple-action" creams or anything containing a bleaching agent on a child.
> - Do not apply herbal mixtures, engine oil, toothpaste or antiseptic liquid to rashes. These burn and irritate.
> - Do not share a prescription between children, even for what looks like the same rash.
> - Avoid heavily perfumed soaps and baby products on dry or itchy skin.

## How treatment is planned

Most children need no tests. Where one helps, such as a scraping for ringworm or a swab for infection, it is quick. The plan you take home is written for a busy household: how much cream to use, how many times a day, for how many days, and what to do when the rash returns. Medicines come from the clinic's [dermatology pharmacy](/services/dermatology-pharmacy/) so the strength matches the prescription.

Follow-up for long-term conditions can often be done by [online consultation](/services/dermatology-consultations/online-consultation/), which saves a school day.

> [!URGENT] Urgent care
> Take your child to a hospital emergency department if a rash comes with fever and the child is drowsy, floppy or not feeding; if spots do not fade when you press a glass on them; if the skin is blistering or peeling; or if eczema suddenly develops painful, punched-out sores.

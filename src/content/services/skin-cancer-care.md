## What a skin cancer check involves

A check starts with questions about your skin history, sun exposure, family history and any spot that worries you. The dermatologist then examines your skin from scalp to soles. On brown and Black skin, special attention goes to the palms, soles, nails, mouth, genital skin and any scars, because that is where skin cancer most often starts.

Spots that need a closer look are examined by [dermoscopy](/services/skin-cancer-care/mole-mapping-and-dermoscopy/), which shows pigment patterns that are invisible to the naked eye. If you have many moles, digital mole mapping records them so that changes can be spotted at later checks.

## The warning signs

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - a mole changes in size, shape or colour, or looks different from your others
> - a dark patch on your palm or sole is growing or has uneven colour
> - a dark streak appears under one nail and widens, or pigment spreads onto the skin around the nail
> - a sore has not healed after four weeks
> - a lump or sore develops on an old scar, burn or long-standing ulcer

Most dark spots are harmless. Small raised dark spots on the cheeks are usually [dermatosis papulosa nigra](/conditions/dermatosis-papulosa-nigra/), and most moles never change. The point of a check is to find the rare exception early, when treatment is simple.

## Treatment

**Precancerous spots**, such as actinic keratoses, are treated before they can turn into cancer, with freezing, prescription cream or light-based treatment.

**Skin cancers** are removed surgically with a margin of normal skin. The specimen goes to a laboratory, and the pathologist's report confirms the type of cancer and whether the edges are clear. For some cancers on the face, [Mohs micrographic surgery](/services/skin-cancer-care/mohs-surgery/) removes the growth in stages and checks each layer under the microscope, so that as little healthy skin as possible is taken.

Where a cancer needs treatment beyond the skin, such as scans, lymph node surgery or oncology care, the dermatologist refers you and co-ordinates with the hospital team.

## Prevention on dark skin

Melanin gives some protection against sunburn, but not against all sun damage, and none against the cancers that arise on palms, soles and scars. Daily sunscreen matters most for people with albinism, vitiligo, lupus or a history of skin cancer, and for anyone treating melasma or dark marks. Get any wound or ulcer that will not heal examined.

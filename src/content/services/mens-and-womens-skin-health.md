## Women: hormones and the skin

**Pregnancy** darkens existing pigment, brings out [melasma](/conditions/melasma/) on the face and a dark line down the abdomen, and stretches the skin. Some rashes occur only in pregnancy, and a few need monitoring because they can affect the baby. Many usual treatments are unsuitable during pregnancy and breastfeeding, so the plan is adjusted. See [pregnancy skin changes](/conditions/pregnancy-skin-changes/).

**After pregnancy**, heavy hair shedding at three to four months is common and temporary. [Stretch marks](/conditions/stretch-marks/) fade from dark to pale over a year or more; treatment works better while they are still coloured.

**Hormonal conditions** such as polycystic ovary syndrome cause acne along the jawline, [excess facial and body hair](/conditions/hirsutism/), thinning scalp hair and dark velvety skin on the neck. Treatment combines skin treatment with tests and, where needed, care from the clinic's [internal medicine service](/services/internal-medicine/).

**Menopause** lowers oestrogen, and the skin becomes drier, thinner and itchier. Hair may thin and facial hair may increase. Moisturising, sun protection and treatment of specific problems help.

## Men: shaving and the beard area

Tightly curled hair, cut short, tends to curve back into the skin. The result is [razor bumps](/conditions/razor-bumps/): firm, itchy bumps and dark marks on the neck, jaw and cheeks, and sometimes the back of the scalp after a close cut. Infection of the hair follicles in the beard looks similar but contains pus and needs different treatment. Shared or poorly cleaned barber's clippers spread both bacterial infection and scalp ringworm.

Changing the shaving method is the foundation of treatment. Prescription creams calm the bumps and fade marks, and [laser hair removal](/services/laser-treatments/laser-hair-removal/) reduces the hair that causes them.

## Intimate-area skin

Itching, soreness, lumps, sores, colour change or splitting of genital or anal skin are common and treatable, and most are not sexually transmitted. Causes include eczema, irritation from soaps and wipes, fungal infection, psoriasis, lichen sclerosus and warts. Because they look alike and are treated differently, an examination is needed, sometimes with a swab or a small biopsy.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - genital itching or soreness lasts more than two weeks
> - the skin turns white, thin or shiny, or tears easily
> - you find a lump, sore or ulcer that does not heal
> - a rash in a skin fold keeps returning after treatment

## Sweating

[Excessive sweating](/conditions/hyperhidrosis/) of the underarms, hands or feet is treated in steps: strong antiperspirants, iontophoresis for hands and feet, tablets, and [Botox injections](/services/aesthetic-treatments/botox/), which reduce underarm sweating for several months at a time. Darkening of the underarms and inner thighs has its own causes and treatment; see [dark underarms, inner thighs, knees and neck](/conditions/dark-underarms-inner-thighs-knees-and-neck/).

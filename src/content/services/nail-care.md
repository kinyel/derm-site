## What your nails can tell a doctor

A nail grows slowly, about three millimetres a month for a fingernail and half that for a toenail, so it keeps a record of what has happened to it. Pits and ridges can point to psoriasis or eczema. Yellow crumbling suggests fungus. Pale, spoon-shaped nails can follow low iron. A green tint under a lifted nail is a bacterial infection. Because many of these look alike, the first step is finding out which one you have.

## Tests before treatment

Treating a nail takes months, so it is worth confirming the cause first. A [fungal test](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/fungal-tests/) on a nail clipping shows whether fungus is present and which type. When the cause is still unclear, or a dark streak needs explaining, a nail biopsy under local anaesthetic gives the answer.

## Treatment

- **Fungal nail infection:** antifungal tablets or nail lacquer, chosen by how many nails are involved and how deep the infection goes. See [fungal nail infection](/conditions/fungal-nail-infection/).
- **Ingrown toenails:** early cases settle with soaking, correct trimming and wider shoes. Repeated or infected ingrown nails are treated by removing the edge of the nail under local anaesthetic and treating the root so that edge does not regrow. See [ingrown toenails](/conditions/ingrown-toenails/).
- **Nail psoriasis and eczema:** treatment applied to the nail fold, injections beside the nail or tablets for severe disease.
- **Infection around the nail:** warm soaks, drainage if pus has collected, and treatment of the cause, often wet work or cuticle damage.
- **Damaged, brittle and splitting nails:** protection, moisturising and removal of the cause, such as harsh removers, gel or acrylic damage, or frequent hand washing.

> [!DERM] See a dermatologist if
> - one nail has a new dark streak, or a streak is getting wider
> - a nail is lifting, crumbling or thickening
> - the skin around a nail is red, swollen, painful or leaking pus
> - you have diabetes and any toenail problem
> - a nail change has lasted more than a month

## Nail salons and nail health

Gel, acrylic and frequent buffing thin the nail plate, and cutting the cuticle removes the seal that keeps water and germs out of the nail fold. If you have regular manicures, leave the cuticles uncut, take breaks between sets and bring your own tools if you can. People with diabetes should avoid pedicures that cut skin or cuticle and have their feet checked at the clinic instead. See [foot skin care](/services/foot-skin-care/).

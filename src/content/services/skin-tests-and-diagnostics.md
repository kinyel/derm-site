## Why test at all?

Many skin conditions can be named by looking. Others imitate each other. Ringworm can look like eczema, and treating it with a steroid cream makes it spread. A dark nail streak is usually harmless, but occasionally it is melanoma. A test replaces a guess with a diagnosis, and it often saves months of the wrong treatment.

The dermatologist will explain why a test is recommended, what it involves and when the result will be ready before anything is done.

## The main groups of tests

**Biopsy.** A small sample of skin, scalp or nail is taken under local anaesthetic and examined under a microscope by a pathologist. Direct immunofluorescence is a special stain used for blistering and autoimmune conditions. See [skin biopsy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/skin-biopsy/).

**Infection tests.** Scales, nail clippings or plucked hairs are examined with potassium hydroxide (KOH) to look for fungus and may be cultured to identify it. Swabs identify bacteria and the antibiotic that will work. See [fungal tests](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/fungal-tests/).

**Allergy tests.** [Patch testing](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/patch-testing/) finds the cause of contact dermatitis, such as hair dye, fragrance, nickel or rubber. Prick and blood tests look for immediate allergies. Phototesting and photopatch testing are used when a rash is brought on by light.

**Hair tests.** [Trichoscopy](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/trichoscopy/) examines the scalp and hair shafts under magnification and often removes the need for a biopsy.

**Skin analysis.** A Wood's lamp shows pigment and some infections that are hard to see in ordinary light. Instruments measure oil, hydration, barrier function and pH to guide skincare and track progress.

**Blood tests.** Used to look for an underlying cause, to screen for related conditions and to monitor medicines.

## Before a test

Tell the dermatologist about any creams you have used on the area in the last two weeks, especially antifungal or steroid creams, because they can make a test falsely negative. Do not wash off, pick or treat the area on the day. For patch testing, you will be asked to stop certain medicines and avoid sun on your back beforehand.

  - q: When is a wound "not healing"?
    a: "A wound that shows no real improvement after four weeks, or has not closed after about three months, is treated as a chronic wound. It usually means something is holding healing back: poor blood flow, high blood sugar, pressure, swelling, infection or, rarely, a skin cancer. Finding that cause is the main task."
  - q: Should I leave a wound open to dry?
    a: "No. Wounds heal faster and scar less when kept clean and slightly moist under a suitable dressing. A dry scab slows new skin from spreading across the wound. Powder, methylated spirit, hot water, herbs and antiseptic poured in undiluted damage healing tissue and should be avoided."
  - q: How often does a dressing need changing?
    a: "It depends on the wound and the dressing. Some modern dressings stay on for several days; wet or infected wounds need more frequent changes. Changing too often disturbs healing. The clinic tells you the schedule and shows you or a relative how to do it at home if that is suitable."
  - q: Can a wound that will not heal be cancer?
    a: "Occasionally. A long-standing ulcer, old burn scar or chronic wound can develop a skin cancer within it, and on dark skin this is one of the more common ways skin cancer appears. A wound with raised, rolled or growing edges, or one that fails to respond to good care, should have a biopsy."

## Why some wounds do not heal

A healthy wound moves through bleeding, inflammation, new tissue and closure in a few weeks. It stalls when the body cannot deliver what healing needs. The common reasons are:

- **Diabetes:** high blood sugar slows repair, and nerve damage means injuries to the feet go unnoticed.
- **Poor circulation:** weak valves in leg veins cause swelling and ulcers near the ankle; narrowed arteries starve tissue of oxygen.
- **Pressure:** lying or sitting in one position, or a shoe that rubs.
- **Infection or dead tissue** in the wound.
- **Poor nutrition, anaemia, smoking and some medicines.**

So wound care is more than dressings. Each wound is assessed and measured, the limb's circulation and sensation are checked, and blood sugar and other tests are arranged where needed. The clinic's [internal medicine service](/services/internal-medicine/) helps control [diabetes](/services/internal-medicine/diabetes/) and other conditions that hold healing back.

## What treatment involves

**Cleaning** removes debris and bacteria without harming new tissue. **Debridement** removes dead tissue and thick slough, which otherwise shelter bacteria and block healing; it is done under local anaesthetic when needed. **Dressings** are chosen to keep the wound at the right moisture and to protect it. **Infection** is treated with antibiotics chosen from a wound swab or tissue culture when the wound shows spreading redness, heat, pus or smell. **Pressure relief and compression** deal with the cause: offloading a foot ulcer, or compression bandaging for a venous ulcer once arterial supply has been checked. Wounds that cannot close on their own may need a **skin graft**, followed up at the clinic.

## Burns

Cool a fresh burn under cool running water for 20 minutes, remove rings and tight clothing, and cover it loosely with cling film or a clean non-fluffy cloth. Do not apply ice, butter, toothpaste, egg, palm oil or powder. Small superficial burns can be managed at the clinic with dressings and follow-up. See [burns](/conditions/burns/).

> [!URGENT] Urgent care
> Go to a hospital emergency department for a burn larger than the person's hand, a burn that is white, leathery or charred, any burn to the face, hands, genitals or a joint, chemical or electrical burns, and any burn in a baby. For a wound, go the same day if redness is spreading quickly, the limb is hot and swollen, you have a fever or feel unwell, or a diabetic foot turns black, smells or becomes numb.

## Scars after healing

On brown and Black skin a healed wound is often darker or lighter than the skin around it for many months. Sun protection and time improve this. Raised, itchy scars that grow beyond the wound are [keloids](/conditions/keloids/) and respond best when treated early.

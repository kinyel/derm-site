## Why feet get their own skin problems

Feet spend the day enclosed, warm and damp, carrying the body's weight. The skin of the sole is the thickest on the body and has no oil glands, so it dries and cracks. Between the toes, moisture softens the skin and lets fungus and bacteria in. In Lagos, the rainy season adds wet shoes and floodwater, and open sandals add dust, friction and cuts.

## Common conditions and how they are treated

**[Athlete's foot](/conditions/athletes-foot/)** is treated with antifungal cream for several weeks, continued after the skin looks normal, and with drying between the toes and rotating shoes. Infection that covers the sole, keeps returning or involves the nails may need tablets.

**[Plantar warts](/conditions/plantar-warts/)** are treated with paring and wart solutions, cryotherapy or other clinic procedures. They can be stubborn and usually need several treatments.

**[Cracked heels](/conditions/cracked-heels/)** respond to softening creams and removal of thick skin. Deep fissures are dressed so they can close.

**Foot eczema and psoriasis** cause itching, scaling, blisters or thick plaques on the soles. They are often mistaken for fungal infection, and a scraping test separates them.

**Dark patches** on the tops of the feet and ankles usually come from friction with footwear, repeated scratching or old inflammation. Treatment addresses the cause first.

**[Sweaty feet](/conditions/hyperhidrosis/)** are treated with strong antiperspirants, iontophoresis or Botox injections where suitable.

## Diabetes and the feet

Diabetes can numb the feet and reduce their blood supply. A blister or crack that goes unfelt can become a deep [ulcer](/conditions/diabetic-foot-ulcers/) within days. A diabetic foot skin assessment checks sensation, pulses, skin, nails and footwear, treats dryness, fungal infection and hard skin, and teaches daily foot care. The clinic's [internal medicine service](/services/internal-medicine/diabetes/) manages the diabetes itself.

> [!URGENT] Urgent care
> If you have diabetes and notice a wound, blister, blackened skin, pus, new swelling, warmth or a bad smell on a foot, see a doctor the same day. Do not wait for pain; a numb foot may not hurt.

## Daily foot care in Lagos

- Wash and dry carefully between the toes every day.
- Moisturise heels and soles, but not between the toes.
- Change socks daily; let shoes dry for a day between wears.
- Wear slippers in shared bathrooms, gyms, mosques and hotel rooms.
- Cut toenails straight across and not too short.
- Do not use blades or corn plasters on your own feet if you have diabetes.

  - q: Why has my rash not cleared with the cream I bought?
    a: "The cream may not match the cause. A steroid cream calms eczema but feeds a fungal infection; an antifungal does nothing for psoriasis. Mixed creams sold in pharmacies can blur the picture and cause thinning or lightening. A diagnosis, sometimes with a skin scraping, shows what will work."
  - q: Is my skin condition contagious?
    a: "Most are not. Eczema, psoriasis, vitiligo and acne cannot be passed on. Scabies, ringworm, impetigo, warts and molluscum can spread by close contact or shared towels and bedding. If you are unsure, a dermatologist can tell you after examining it."
  - q: How long will treatment take to work?
    a: "It depends on the condition. Skin takes about four weeks to renew itself, so many treatments need four to twelve weeks before you can judge them. Dark marks fade over months. Your treatment plan sets out what to expect and when you will be reviewed."

## How medical skin conditions are treated

Treatment starts with the diagnosis. Many rashes look alike, and on brown and Black skin the usual clue, redness, often shows as violet, grey or dark brown instead. The dermatologist examines the skin, asks about what you have used, and arranges [tests](/services/skin-tests-and-diagnostics/) when they are needed.

Most conditions are then treated with a combination of:

- **Topical treatment:** creams, ointments, lotions and washes applied to the skin.
- **Tablets or capsules** for conditions that are widespread, deep or not controlled by creams. Some need blood tests before and during treatment.
- **Procedures** such as cryotherapy for warts, injections into keloids or drainage of an abscess.
- **[Phototherapy](/services/clinical-dermatology/phototherapy/):** controlled ultraviolet light for vitiligo, psoriasis, eczema and some itching conditions, given in the clinic's full-body booth or with a handheld unit for small areas.

The clinic's [dermatology pharmacy](/services/dermatology-pharmacy/) dispenses the prescription, so you receive the product the doctor intended.

## Why Lagos matters

Heat and humidity make sweat rashes, fungal infections and folliculitis more common here than in cooler climates. Harmattan dries the skin and flares eczema. Strong sun deepens melasma and dark marks all year round. A treatment plan written for Lagos takes these into account: lighter textures, antifungal care for skin folds and daily sunscreen for pigment problems.

> [!AVOID] What to avoid
> Do not use a cream that contains a steroid, such as a "triple-action" cream, for more than a few days without a doctor's advice. On the face, groin and skin folds these creams thin the skin, cause stretch marks and acne-like spots, and can make fungal infections spread. See [skin damage from bleaching and steroid creams](/conditions/skin-damage-from-bleaching-and-steroid-creams/).

## Long-term conditions

Eczema, psoriasis, vitiligo, acne and rosacea are managed over time rather than cured in one consultation. The aim is clear or nearly clear skin with the least treatment, and a plan for what to do when the condition flares. Reviews can often be done by [online consultation](/services/dermatology-consultations/online-consultation/).

> [!URGENT] Urgent care
> Go to a hospital emergency department if a rash comes with fever and blistering or peeling skin, sores in the mouth or eyes, swelling of the lips or tongue, or difficulty breathing, especially after starting a new medicine. A hot, painful, rapidly spreading area of skin with fever also needs same-day care.
